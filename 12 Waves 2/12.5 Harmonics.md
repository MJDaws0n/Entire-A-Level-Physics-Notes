## Core notes

### 12.5 Harmonics

*Source slide 1.*

- The distance between nodes is equal to half the [[Glossary#Wavelength|wavelength]] therefore the wavelength equals 3 m.

### Tension as a force

*Source slide 3.*

- Tension is described as the pulling [[Glossary#Force|force]] transmitted axially (along a line) by the means of a string, a cable, chain, or similar one-dimensional continuous object.
- Applying a force upon a string or rope will increase tension.

### Stringed Instruments

*Source slides 4–11.*

- Stringed instruments are stretched between two fixed points.
- These points act as nodes.
- When a string is plucked a [[Glossary#Progressive Wave|progressive wave]] travels along the string and reflects backwards.
- This creates two progressive waves traveling in opposite directions that then form a [[Glossary#Stationary Wave|stationary wave]].
- When the string is plucked, it vibrates in its fundamental [[Glossary#Frequency|frequency]], fo.
- The wavelength of the progressive wave is double that of the length of the string (2L).
- Each string has a fundamental mode of vibration.

### Harmonic Series

*Source slide 13.*

- Wavelength of the progressive wave.
- L is the length of the string.
- Frequency in multiples of fo
- Shape of the standing wave

### Timbre of an instrument

*Source slide 15.*

- This is because of the harmonic series.
- Stringed instruments usually produce a mixture of the fundamental and higher harmonics in different proportions.
- This creates the different musical colour (timbre) of each instrument and is one reason why a guitar does not sound like a grand piano when they both play the same fundamental frequency (note).

### Harmonic (Overtone and Undertone) Series

*Source slide 16.*

- This video will comment on the overtone series and the undertone series.
- You will not be asked about the undertone series in exams yet it is an interesting phenomena.
- Stringed instrument that utilise bows are incredibly complicated instruments to model physically.
- Studying this area has led to advances in the study nonlinear systems and friction.
- Jim Woodhouse of Cambridge university is a world leader in the physics of violins

## Equations

### Stationary waves and harmonics

$$f_n=\frac{nv}{2L}\quad(n=1,2,3,\ldots)$$

String or open-open air column.

$$f_n=\frac{nv}{4L}\quad(n=1,3,5,\ldots)$$

Closed-open air column.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Mark boundary conditions first: fixed/closed ends are displacement nodes; open ends are displacement antinodes.
2. Count quarter- or half-wavelength sections to relate \(L\) and \(\lambda\).
3. Use \(v=f\lambda\) after finding the allowed wavelength.

## Common mistakes

- A stationary wave transfers no net energy along the pattern.
- Adjacent points between the same two nodes oscillate in phase; neighbouring loops are in antiphase.
