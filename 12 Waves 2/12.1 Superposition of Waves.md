## Core notes

### Unwanted Noise

*Source slide 5.*

- Some headphones are noise cancelling.
- This means that they ‘block’ out unwanted children screaming on buses or drunken people talking about their personal lives on trains.
- To achieve this affect they utilise the principle of [[Glossary#Superposition|superposition]].
- They have microphones in built into the head phones to detect the unwanted ambient noise and then alters the frequencies that you hear to minimise said children.

### Principle of Superposition

*Source slide 6.*

- The principle of superposition is an integral part to physics and engineering.
- It states that any linear system can be described in total if we know its component parts and vice versa.
- You have been using the principle of superposition since you learnt addition!

### Resultant Waves

*Source slides 7–8.*

- When progressive waves pass each other they can superpose and produce resultant waves.
- Superposition of waves can result in a resultant wave with an increased or decreased [[Glossary#Amplitude|amplitude]].
- The initial waves are still present but we can totally describe the system with the resultant wave.

### Constructive Interference

*Source slide 10.*

- When the amplitude of the resultant wave is greater than the individual components we call this constructive interference.

### Destructive Interference

*Source slide 11.*

- If two waves are in total anti-phase then we can achieve total destruction of the waves.
- This is how the noise cancelling headphones work.

### If two sound waves constructively interfere what happens?

*Source slide 13.*

- Two progressive waves of equal size that are in anti-phase are heading towards each other.
- Two progressive waves which are in phase are heading towards each other.
- One is twice the size of the other.
- Their amplitudes increase
- Increase by a factor 3 compared to the smaller wave.

## Equations

### Superposition and interference

$$\Delta x=n\lambda$$

Constructive interference.

$$\Delta x=\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\lambda$$

Destructive interference.

$$\lambda=\frac{ax}{D}$$

Double-slit relationship for small angles.

### Progressive waves

$$v=f\lambda$$

Wave speed.

$$f=\frac{1}{T}$$

Frequency and period.

$$\Delta\phi=2\pi\frac{\Delta x}{\lambda}=2\pi\frac{\Delta t}{T}$$

Phase difference in radians.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Add displacements algebraically at the same place and time.
2. For stable fringes, sources must be coherent: same frequency with constant phase difference.
3. In the double-slit formula, use slit separation \(a\), fringe spacing \(x\), and screen distance \(D\).
4. Measure wavelength between neighbouring points in the same phase.
5. Keep particle motion separate from the direction of energy transfer.
6. Use peak amplitude, not peak-to-peak displacement, unless instructed otherwise.

## Common mistakes

- Fringe spacing is the distance between adjacent bright fringes, not from the centre to an arbitrary fringe unless divided by fringe number.
- Wave speed is not the speed of oscillating particles in the medium.
