## Core notes

### 26.1 Einstein's Mass-Energy Equation

*Source slide 1.*

- [[Glossary#Energy|Energy]] is not created or destroyed it is simply transferred.
- Energy before equals energy afterwards.
- In a chemical reaction, the mass of the reactants equals the mass of the products.

### Einstein's Mass-Energy Equivalence

*Source slide 3.*

- In 1905, Einstein published his work on the theory of special relativity.
- This groundbreaking work showed that the physical world operated differently than previously thought.
- The law of [[Glossary#Conservation Of Energy|conservation of energy]] and law conservation of mass where superseded into a single law.
- Energy is not conserved.
- Mass is not conserved.

### Mass-energy Equivalence

*Source slide 7.*

- Mass is a form of energy.
- Particles and antiparticles annihilate each other and convert into two high energy photons.

### Interpretation of mass-energy equivalence

*Source slides 9–10.*

- The second interpretation of mass-energy equivalence states that, objects that increase in energy, gain mass.
- Einstein’s theory of special relativity showed that no object can accelerate beyond the speed of light.
- The speed of light became the universal speed limit.
- Mass is also called inertia.
- Increasing the mass of an object means it is harder to accelerate.
- Increasing the energy of an object increases the mass of that object.
- At zero [[Glossary#Kinetic Energy|kinetic energy]] an object has a rest mass.

## Equations

### Mass-energy and binding energy

$$\Delta E=\Delta mc^2$$

Energy released from a mass decrease.

$$E_b=\Delta mc^2$$

Nuclear binding energy from mass defect.

$$1\,\mathrm{u}=1.661\times10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$$

Atomic mass unit conversion.

$$1\,\mathrm{eV}=1.60\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J}$$

Electronvolt conversion.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Find mass defect using consistent particle or atomic masses.
2. Convert the mass difference to kilograms before using \(c^2\), unless using a stated \(\mathrm{MeV/u}\) conversion.
3. Compare binding energy per nucleon to explain energy release in fission or fusion.

## Common mistakes

- Binding energy is the energy required to separate a nucleus; an increase in binding energy corresponds to energy released when the nucleus forms.
