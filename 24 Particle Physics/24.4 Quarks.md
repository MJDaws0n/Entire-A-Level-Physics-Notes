## Core notes

### 24.4 Quarks

*Source slide 1.*

- Leptons are fundamental.
- Leptons do not experience the strong [[Glossary#Force|force]].

### The Standard Model

*Source slides 3–4.*

- The Standard Model classifies all known fundamental particles.
- It is also known as the particle zoo.
- The Standard Model of particle physics is the theory describing three of the four known fundamental forces.
- It describes, the electromagnetic, weak nuclear, and strong nuclear interactions.
- The gravitational interaction is described by Einstein’s general theory of relativity.
- Covered in A-level physics

### Quarks

*Source slides 6–8.*

- Quarks are fundamental particles that experience each of the four fundamental forces.
- The standard model of elementary particle physics contains six quarks and six antiquarks.
- Quarks possess a fractional fundamental charge (e).
- In the A-level assessments you will only need to know about the up, down, and strange quarks and their antiparticles.
- You will be given the fractional charge of the up, down, and strange on the OCR Data booklet.

### Hadrons

*Source slide 9.*

- Last lesson we saw that hadrons are not fundamental particles they are composite particles.
- Hadrons are either made of three quarks or two quarks.
- Hadrons made of three quarks are called baryons.
- Hadrons made of two quarks are called mesons.

## Method

1. Classify hadrons as baryons (three quarks) or mesons (quark-antiquark).
2. Conserve charge, baryon number and lepton number in reactions.
3. An antiparticle has the same mass and opposite charge and additive quantum numbers.

## Common mistakes

- Leptons are fundamental; hadrons are composite particles made from quarks.
