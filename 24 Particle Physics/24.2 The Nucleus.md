### The Neutron

*Source slide 3.*

- It was not until 1932 that the neutron was discovered by James Chadwick.
- Chadwick was Rutherford's assistant director of research at the Cavendish Laboratory, at the University of Cambridge, at the time.
- He was awarded the Nobel prize for the discovery in 1935.
- The discovery of the neutron turned the 20th century to the nuclear age.

### The Nucleon

*Source slide 4.*

- Protons and neutrons are called nucleons.
- Atoms have a nucleus in their centre.
- The nucleus is composed of positive protons and neutral neutrons.
- The charge of the nucleus is positive.
- Negative electrons surround the nucleus in [[Glossary#Energy|energy]] levels (electron shells).

### Number of Protons

*Source slide 5.*

- The number of protons defines the name of the atom.
- All atoms with 1 proton in the nucleus is called hydrogen.
- All atoms with 2 protons in the nucleus is called helium.
- All atoms with 3 protons in the nucleus is called lithium.
- All atoms with 4 protons in the nucleus is called beryllium.

### Isotopes

*Source slides 7–9.*

- An [[Glossary#Isotope|isotope]] is a type of atom with the same number of positive protons and a different number of neutral neutrons.
- All atoms with 6 protons in the nucleus is called carbon.

### Nuclear notation

*Source slide 11.*

- We can represent elements, their isotopes, and radiation with nuclear notation.

### Atomic Mass Units (u)

*Source slide 15.*

- The masses of atoms and nuclear particles are often expressed in atomic mass units (u).
- One atomic mass unit (1 u) is equal to a twelfth of the mass of neutral carbon-12 atom.

### Nuclear Forces

*Source slide 25.*

- The attractive gravitational [[Glossary#Force|force]] between the protons is far too small to overcome the electrostatic force repelling the protons apart.
- There is another force that is attractive and acts in the nucleus.
- The strong nuclear force acts between all nucleons.
- It is a very short-range force and is effective only over a few femtometres.

### Strong Nuclear Force

- Decrease distance between particles

## Equations

### Nuclear and particle structure

$$R=r_0A^{1/3}$$

Nuclear radius model.

$$A=Z+N$$

Mass number equals proton plus neutron number.

$$Q_{\mathrm{before}}=Q_{\mathrm{after}}$$

Charge conservation in particle reactions.

### Density and pressure

$$\rho=\frac{m}{V}$$

Density.

$$p=\frac{F}{A}$$

Pressure caused by a normal force.

$$\Delta p=h\rho g$$

Pressure difference in a fluid column.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Balance charge, baryon number and lepton number in particle equations.
2. For nuclear notation, conserve total nucleon number and proton number.
3. Use antiparticles with equal mass and opposite charge and relevant quantum numbers.
4. Convert mass, volume, length and area to SI units before substituting.
5. For liquid pressure, use vertical depth below the surface.
6. For total pressure, add atmospheric pressure only when the question requires absolute pressure.

## Common mistakes

- Atomic number \(Z\) counts protons; mass number \(A\) counts nucleons, not atomic mass in kilograms.
- \(1\,\mathrm{cm^3}=10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}\), not \(10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^3}\).
