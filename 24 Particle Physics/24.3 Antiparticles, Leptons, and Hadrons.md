## Core notes

### Antiparticles and Antimatter

*Source slide 3.*

- Paul Dirac proposed in 1928 that all particles have a corresponding antiparticle.
- Antiparticle have the same properties of their partner particle but the opposite charge.
- For example, electrons have an antiparticle called the positron.
- Electrons and positrons have the same mass me but opposite charge, +e.

### Antiparticles

*Source slides 4–5.*

- A bar or tilde above the particles symbol to denote it is an antiparticle.
- The neutron and antineutron seem to be identical.
- They both have the same mass and zero charge.
- Yet, we can identify when a neutron and antineutron meet.
- Particles and antiparticles annihilate each other and convert into two high [[Glossary#Energy|energy]] photons.

### Fundamental Forces

*Source slide 7.*

- There are only four fundamental forces.

### Fundamental Particles

*Source slide 10.*

- Electrons are fundamental particles.
- Protons and neutrons are not fundamental particles.
- Protons and neutrons are composite particles made of three fundamental particles called quarks.
- Mesons are composite particles made of two quarks.

### Hadrons

*Source slide 11.*

- A [[Glossary#Hadron|hadron]] is a composite subatomic particle made of two or more quarks held together by the strong nuclear [[Glossary#Force|force]].
- Example of hadrons are, protons, neutrons, and mesons.
- Hadrons that are electric charged are affected by the electromagnetic force.
- Hadrons decay via the weak force.

### Leptons

*Source slide 12.*

- Leptons are fundamental particles.
- Leptons are not made of smaller units.
- Examples of leptons are, electrons, and neutrinos
- (more about neutrinos in lesson 24.5)
- Leptons do not experience the strong force.

## Method

1. Classify hadrons as baryons (three quarks) or mesons (quark-antiquark).
2. Conserve charge, baryon number and lepton number in reactions.
3. An antiparticle has the same mass and opposite charge and additive quantum numbers.

## Common mistakes

- Leptons are fundamental; hadrons are composite particles made from quarks.
