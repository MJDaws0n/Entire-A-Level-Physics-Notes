## Core notes

### 14.2 Solids, Liquids and Gases

*Source slide 1.*

- Word Bank: [[Glossary#Density|Density]], [[Glossary#Kinetic Energy|kinetic energy]], electrostatic forces.

### Solids

*Source slide 4.*

- Solids generally have the greatest density for a given phase of a substance.
- Solids have very strong electrostatic bonds between the atoms (or molecules).
- Solids vibrate on a fixed lattice point.
- When solids are heated they gain kinetic [[Glossary#Energy|energy]] and can overcome their electrostatic bonds and change shape.

### Liquids

*Source slide 5.*

- Liquids have weaker electrostatic bonds than solids.
- Therefore, liquids can move around and have no fixed shape.
- When a liquid is heated it gains kinetic energy and then some particles can break away to form a gas.
- Liquid water is unusual as it has a greater density than ice (solid water).
- This is due to the crystalline pattern ice forms.

### Gas

*Source slide 6.*

- In a gas we can presume that there are no forces of attraction between the gas particles.
- A gas occupies a greater volume due to their distance apart form each other.
- Gas particles have the greatest kinetic energy and therefore travel at higher speeds.
- When gas particles collide with container walls they exert a [[Glossary#Pressure|pressure]].

### Density

*Source slide 7.*

- Density tells you how much mass there is per volume.
- Solids have the greatest density because they have more particles in a given volume.

### Proportionalities

*Source slide 11.*

- As I increase the volume I decrease the density.
- As I increase the mass I increase the density.
- Volume and density are inversely proportional.
- Mass and density are proportional.

### Brownian Motion

*Source slide 12.*

- In 1827 a botanist, called Robert Brown, had a microscope and discovered something rather odd.
- He placed ancient pollen into a cup of water and it seemed to be alive!
- Not to scale (movement or dimensions)

## Method

1. Internal energy is the sum of random kinetic and intermolecular potential energies.
2. Temperature measures the mean random kinetic energy of particles, not the total internal energy.

## Common mistakes

- Temperature remains constant during an ideal change of state even while internal energy changes.
