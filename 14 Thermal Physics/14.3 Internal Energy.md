## Core notes

### 14.3 Internal Energy

*Source slide 1.*

- Water expands about four percent when heated from room temperature to its boiling point.
- The ocean will expand when heated.
- As the temperature increases the particles’ motion ([[Glossary#Kinetic Energy|kinetic energy]]) increases.
- This results in more collisions per second between the particles.

### Internal Energy Definition

*Source slide 3.*

- The sum of randomly distributed kinetic and potential energies of atoms or molecules within the substance.

### Minimum Internal Energy

*Source slides 4–5.*

- The water contains a huge number of water molecules travelling at hundreds of meter per second.
- The [[Glossary#Internal Energy|internal energy]] of the water is the sum of all the individual kinetic energies of the water molecules in the glass and the sum of potential energies due to the electrostatic forces between the molecules.
- Now we cool the beaker to 0 K.
- The internal [[Glossary#Energy|energy]] of a system is the sum of randomly distributed kinetic and potential energies of atoms or molecules within the substance.
- At 0 K there is no movement (kinetic energy) but there will still be a contribution from the potential energy of electrostatic forces between the molecules.

### Increasing Internal Energy

*Source slide 6.*

- Increasing temperature increases the kinetic energy of the particles and thus increases the internal energy.
- At a phase change the temperature doesn’t change but the internal energy increases.
- Increase in internal energy.
- Increase in the electrostatic potential.

## Method

1. Internal energy is the sum of random kinetic and intermolecular potential energies.
2. Temperature measures the mean random kinetic energy of particles, not the total internal energy.

## Common mistakes

- Temperature remains constant during an ideal change of state even while internal energy changes.
