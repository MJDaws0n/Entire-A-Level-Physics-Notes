## Core notes

### 14.1 Temperature

*Source slide 1.*

- Celsius (°C) Fahrenheit (F) Kelvin (K)

### Celsius Temperature Scale

*Source slide 3.*

- There are many different types of temperature scales.
- We have seen that there are, Celsius (°C), Fahrenheit (F), and Kelvin (K) scales.
- Each one of these scales are based upon different physical phenomena.
- For example, the Celsius scale is based upon phase changes of water at standard atmospheric [[Glossary#Pressure|pressure]].

### Kelvin Temperature Scale

*Source slide 4.*

- The kelvin is an SI base unit defined by fixing the Boltzmann constant; [[Glossary#Absolute Temperature|absolute temperature]] is linked to mean particle [[Glossary#Kinetic Energy|kinetic energy]].
- We heat up a substance and the particles move around more.
- We cool the substance and the motion slows.
- As temperature approaches absolute zero, thermal motion is minimised; absolute zero cannot be reached and quantum zero-point motion remains.

### Fahrenheit Temperature Scale

*Source slide 6.*

- The Fahrenheit scale is based upon a really cool discovery in 1724 by physicist Daniel Fahrenheit.
- He would add ice and ammonium chloride to water and it would always achieve the same temperature.
- This is called a frigorific mixture.
- The temperature that this mixture came to would be called 0 F.

### Temperature and thermal equilibrium

*Source slide 7.*

- The triple point is a special place where a substance can exist in all three stages of matter at the same time.
- When this happens the substance is in thermal equilibrium.
- There is no net flow of thermal [[Glossary#Energy|energy]].

### Temperature and Thermal Energy

*Source slide 8.*

- Thermal energy can flow from hot objects to cooler objects.
- Net heat transfers spontaneously from hotter to colder objects; transfer from colder to hotter requires external work.
- Thermal energy will stop flowing when the objects become the same temperature.
- This is called being in thermal equilibrium.

Absolute temperature.

### SI units, prefixes and conversions

$$Q_{\mathrm{base}} = Q_{\mathrm{prefixed}}\times 10^n$$

Replace the prefix by its power of ten.

$$T\,(\mathrm{K}) = \theta\,({}^{\circ}\mathrm{C}) + 273.15$$

Convert Celsius temperature to kelvin.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Internal energy is the sum of random kinetic and intermolecular potential energies.
2. Temperature measures the mean random kinetic energy of particles, not the total internal energy.
3. Write the prefix as a power of ten before doing the calculation.
4. Convert every value to coherent SI units, then substitute.
5. Carry extra calculator digits and round only the final answer.

## Common mistakes

- Temperature remains constant during an ideal change of state even while internal energy changes.
- A capital letter can change a prefix: \(\mathrm{m}\) is milli but \(\mathrm{M}\) is mega.
- For squared or cubed units, the conversion factor must also be squared or cubed.
