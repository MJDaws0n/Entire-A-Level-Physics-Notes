## Core notes

### 11.7 Polarisation

*Source slide 1.*

- EM waves do not need a medium to travel through.
- X-rays and gamma rays are characterised by their source not their [[Glossary#Wavelength|wavelength]].

### Polarisation of waves

*Source slide 3.*

- As we saw previously, waves can be polarised and this property can be used to make sunglasses more effective.
- This fact has also been used to help detect gravitational waves which was the last predicted component of general relativity to be found.

### Polarisers in Electromagnetic Waves

*Source slides 4–5.*

- There are two types of polarisers, vertical and horizontal.
- A vertical polariser allows through vertical aligned fields.
- A horizontal polariser allows through horizontal aligned fields.

### Uses of Polarisers

*Source slide 7.*

- Polarisers are used in many applications.
- They are used in communication transmitters in order to reduced interference between different transmitters.
- When 3D cinema experiences were popular they utilised polarisers in the glasses.
- One was a left hand circular polariser and the other a right hand circular polariser.

## Method

1. Expect the greatest diffraction when aperture size is comparable with wavelength.
2. Only transverse waves can be polarised.
3. For crossed polarisers, compare the transmission axes, not their angles to the bench.

## Common mistakes

- Diffraction does not require a change of medium.
