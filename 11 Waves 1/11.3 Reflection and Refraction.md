## Core notes

### Definitions

*Source slide 3.*

- Ray: A representation of a wave.
- A straight line with an arrow indicating the direction of [[Glossary#Energy|energy]] transport.
- Reflection occurs when a wave changes direction at a boundary between two materials.
- Refraction occurs when a wave changes direction and speed when passing from one material to another.

### Similarities and Differences

*Source slide 4.*

- Reflection and refraction both occur when a wave changes direction at boundaries of different mediums.
- Reflection only changes direction while refraction also changes the wave’s speed.

### The Law and Rules of Reflection

*Source slide 5.*

- The law of reflection applies whenever a wave is reflected.
- When waves reflect their [[Glossary#Wavelength|wavelength]] and [[Glossary#Frequency|frequency]] do not change.
- A reflected wave gains an additional 90o phase difference from the incident wave.

### Refraction

*Source slide 7.*

- Refraction is when the wave changes [[Glossary#Velocity|velocity]] (speed and direction).
- If the refracted ray bends towards the [[Glossary#Normal|normal]] the wave has slowed down.
- When the refracted ray bends away its speed has increased.
- If the wave slows down the wavelength decreases and the frequency stays constant.

### Wave Speed and Wavelength in Refraction

*Source slide 8.*

- when the energy in the wave is constant.

## Equations

### Progressive waves

$$v=f\lambda$$

Wave speed.

$$f=\frac{1}{T}$$

Frequency and period.

$$\Delta\phi=2\pi\frac{\Delta x}{\lambda}=2\pi\frac{\Delta t}{T}$$

Phase difference in radians.

### Refraction and total internal reflection

$$n=\frac{c}{v}$$

Absolute refractive index.

$$n_1\sin\theta_1=n_2\sin\theta_2$$

Snell's law.

$$\sin C=\frac{n_2}{n_1}$$

Critical angle from higher index \(n_1\) to lower index \(n_2\).

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Measure wavelength between neighbouring points in the same phase.
2. Keep particle motion separate from the direction of energy transfer.
3. Use peak amplitude, not peak-to-peak displacement, unless instructed otherwise.
4. Measure every angle from the normal.
5. On entering a higher refractive index, a ray slows and bends towards the normal.
6. Total internal reflection requires travel from higher to lower refractive index and incidence greater than the critical angle.

## Common mistakes

- Wave speed is not the speed of oscillating particles in the medium.
- Frequency remains constant at a boundary; speed and wavelength change.
