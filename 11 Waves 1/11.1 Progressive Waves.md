## Core notes

### What is a progressive wave?

*Source slide 3.*

- A [[Glossary#Progressive Wave|progressive wave]] is an oscillation (vibration) that transfers [[Glossary#Energy|energy]] but does not transfer matter.
- Some progressive waves need a medium to travel through.
- These are called mechanical waves.
- For example, sound waves are mechanical waves.
- Other progressive waves can travel through a vacuum.

### Mechanical Waves and Forces

*Source slide 4.*

- Mechanical waves are composed of the movement of matter that transfer energy.
- As the progressive wave travels through the medium (material) the particles move from their equilibrium position to a new position.
- For the particles to move a [[Glossary#Force|force]] must be applied.
- The particles in the medium exert a force on their neighbours.
- We can model this as if there were springs between the particles.

### Parallel and Perpendicular

*Source slide 5.*

- Parallel lines are lines that are always the same distance apart.
- Perpendicular lines are lines that intersect at a right (90o) angle.

### Types of waves

*Source slides 7–8.*

- Transverse waves are when energy is transported perpendicular to the direction oscillation.
- Longitudinal waves are when energy is transported parallel to the direction oscillation.

### Amplitude

*Source slides 11–12.*

- Waves are oscillations (vibrations) that transfer energy.
- [[Glossary#Amplitude|Amplitude]] is the maximum [[Glossary#Displacement|displacement]] from the rest position.
- As the amplitude increases the energy transferred by the wave increases.

### Wavelength

*Source slide 14.*

- [[Glossary#Wavelength|Wavelength]] is the distance for a wave’s shape to repeat.

### Frequency

*Source slide 18.*

- [[Glossary#Frequency|Frequency]] is the number of waves passing a point in a second.
- Frequency has units Hz.
- There are 6 wavelengths.
- The frequency is 1 Hz.

## Equations

### Progressive waves

$$v=f\lambda$$

Wave speed.

$$f=\frac{1}{T}$$

Frequency and period.

$$\Delta\phi=2\pi\frac{\Delta x}{\lambda}=2\pi\frac{\Delta t}{T}$$

Phase difference in radians.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Measure wavelength between neighbouring points in the same phase.
2. Keep particle motion separate from the direction of energy transfer.
3. Use peak amplitude, not peak-to-peak displacement, unless instructed otherwise.

## Common mistakes

- Wave speed is not the speed of oscillating particles in the medium.
