## Core notes

### 11.4 Diffraction and Polarisation

*Source slide 1.*

- Angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection.
- If a refracted ray bends towards the [[Glossary#Normal|normal]] then its speed slows down and the [[Glossary#Frequency|frequency]] is constant.

### Diffraction and Direction

*Source slide 3.*

- When a wave passes through a gap they spread out.
- This is [[Glossary#Diffraction|diffraction]].
- All waves can undergo diffraction.
- The speed, [[Glossary#Wavelength|wavelength]] and frequency of a wave does not change when diffraction occurs.
- Diffraction redistributes a wave into new directions; within the same medium its speed and frequency do not change.

### The Magnitude of Diffraction

*Source slide 5.*

- Size of gap (or obstacle).
- Diffraction is increased when the size of the gap is comparable to the wavelength of the wave.
- Large gap relative to wavelength
- The relative size of a doorway and the wavelengths of light and sound is why you can hear someone around a doorway but you cannot see them.
- Gap relative size to wavelength

### Polarisation

*Source slides 11–12.*

- Waves are oscillations that transfer [[Glossary#Energy|energy]].
- To date we have only considered waves that oscillate in one dimension.
- Yet we live in a three dimensional world so it makes sense that the oscillations can be in three dimensions too.
- Waves that oscillate in one plane (one dimension) are called plane polarised.
- Waves that oscillate in many possible planes are called unpolarised.
- Light from a filament lamp is unpolarised light.

### Longitudinal Plane Polarisation?

*Source slide 13.*

- For longitudinal waves the direction of energy transport is parallel to oscillation by definition.
- As their oscillations are limited to one plane it does not make sense to talk about them being plane polarised.
- Therefore, we say longitudinal waves cannot be plane polarised.

### Reflected Partial Polarisation

*Source slides 14–15.*

- When transverse waves are reflected off a surface they become partially polarised.
- This means that there are more waves oscillating on one particular plane but not completely plane polarised.
- Majority of oscillations in this plane
- This is how polarised sunglasses help to reduce glare from water.
- The glasses block the majority of the partially polarised light but not the unpolarised light.
- Partially polarised light is blocked

## Method

1. Expect the greatest diffraction when aperture size is comparable with wavelength.
2. Only transverse waves can be polarised.
3. For crossed polarisers, compare the transmission axes, not their angles to the bench.
4. Measure wavelength between neighbouring points in the same phase.
5. Keep particle motion separate from the direction of energy transfer.
6. Use peak amplitude, not peak-to-peak displacement, unless instructed otherwise.

## Common mistakes

- Diffraction does not require a change of medium.
- Wave speed is not the speed of oscillating particles in the medium.
