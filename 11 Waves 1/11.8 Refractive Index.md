## Core notes

### Refractive Index and Refraction

*Source slide 3.*

- Opticians use a device called a refractometer.
- This device can measure the precise angles that rays of light are refracted by the eye.
- When a ray is refracted through an angle its speed also changes.
- The change of speed, and thus the angle of refraction, can be described with the use of the [[Glossary#Refractive Index|refractive index]].

### Refractive Index

*Source slide 4.*

- When light passes from one medium to another light will refract.
- Refraction changes the speed of the wave and also its relative angle to the [[Glossary#Normal|normal]].
- n is the refractive index
- c is the speed of light
- v is the [[Glossary#Velocity|velocity]] of the wave in that medium.

### Refraction Law

*Source slide 6.*

- ѳ is the angle between the normal and the incident ray and k is a constant.

## Equations

### Refraction and total internal reflection

$$n=\frac{c}{v}$$

Absolute refractive index.

$$n_1\sin\theta_1=n_2\sin\theta_2$$

Snell's law.

$$\sin C=\frac{n_2}{n_1}$$

Critical angle from higher index \(n_1\) to lower index \(n_2\).

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Measure every angle from the normal.
2. On entering a higher refractive index, a ray slows and bends towards the normal.
3. Total internal reflection requires travel from higher to lower refractive index and incidence greater than the critical angle.

## Common mistakes

- Frequency remains constant at a boundary; speed and wavelength change.
