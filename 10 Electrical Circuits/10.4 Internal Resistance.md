## Core notes

### All cells are not created equal

*Source slides 3–4.*

- If must be pretty obvious that all cells and batteries have their own characteristics.
- For example, you would never place a car battery into a television remote and you would never place a 9V battery into a mobile phone.
- The reason for this is that different cells produce different electromotive forces (emf) and currents.
- One of the determining factors for the magnitude of current produced is the internal [[Glossary#Resistance|resistance]] of the cell.

### Modelling Current in a Power Source

*Source slide 5.*

- Current is simply a net flow of charge carriers ‘moving’ in a direction.
- The charge carriers have to travel through some material in the [[Glossary#Power|power]] source.
- This requires the charge carriers to do work moving through the power source.
- Therefore, the charge carriers have ‘lost’ [[Glossary#Energy|energy]] before they leave the cell.
- So, the terminal [[Glossary#Potential Difference|potential difference]] is less than the emf of the power source.

### Equations for Internal Resistance

*Source slide 6.*

- V – Terminal potential difference (V)
- I – current flowing through the cell (A)

### Connecting Cells in Parallel

*Source slide 19.*

- Previous application for power supplies in parallel: tube radios.

### Determining Internal Resistance

*Source slides 20–21.*

- We vary the resistance of the circuit and thus the current flowing through the circuit.
- The graph of V against I will yield a negative gradient equal to the internal resistance and a y intercept being the value of the emf.
- Plotting the data into a graph.

## Equations

### Internal resistance

$$\mathcal{E}=I(R+r)$$

EMF for external resistance \(R\) and internal resistance \(r\).

$$\mathcal{E}=V+Ir$$

Terminal potential difference while supplying current.

$$V=\mathcal{E}-Ir$$

Straight-line form for a \(V\)-against-\(I\) graph.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. From a \(V\)-against-\(I\) graph, the intercept is \(\mathcal{E}\) and gradient is \(-r\).
2. Calculate lost volts \(Ir\) before finding the terminal potential difference.

## Common mistakes

- Terminal potential difference equals EMF only when current is zero or internal resistance is negligible.
