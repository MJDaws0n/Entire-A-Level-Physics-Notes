## Equations

### Kirchhoff's laws

$$\sum I_{\mathrm{in}}=\sum I_{\mathrm{out}}$$

First law: conservation of charge at a junction.

$$\sum \mathcal{E}=\sum V$$

Second law around a complete loop: conservation of energy per unit charge.

### Resistance and resistivity

$$R=\frac{V}{I}$$

Resistance at a particular operating point.

$$R=\frac{\rho L}{A}$$

Resistance of a uniform conductor.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Mark current directions; a negative answer means the true direction is opposite.
2. Apply the junction rule, then write one independent loop equation at a time.
3. Treat a potential rise and potential drop with consistent signs around each loop.
4. For an ohmic conductor, use the gradient of a \(V\)-against-\(I\) graph.
5. In resistivity work, measure \(L\) and diameter carefully and use \(A=\pi d^2/4\).

## Common mistakes

- Current is not used up by a component; energy is transferred from each coulomb of charge.
- The reciprocal gradient is required if the graph plots \(I\) against \(V\).
- Resistivity is a material property; resistance also depends on dimensions.
