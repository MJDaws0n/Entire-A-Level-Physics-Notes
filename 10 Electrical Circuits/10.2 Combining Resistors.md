### Resistors in Series

*Source slide 3.*

- When resistors are placed into single loop we call this series.
- Calculating the total [[Glossary#Resistance|resistance]], RT, of resistors in series is the easiest task you will have at A level physics.

### Resistors in Parallel

*Source slides 4–6.*

- When resistors are placed into different loops we call this parallel.
- It is a slightly more complicated task to calculate the total resistance, RT, in a parallel circuit compared to a series circuit.
- There are a couple of methods to solve the total resistance in a parallel circuit.
- One is the advised simple A level method and the other is the physicist method which is harder and more likely to result in errors.
- Now we have a relationship between the size of the resistors and RT.

### Key Points: Combining Resistors

*Source slide 7.*

- Reduce whichever series or parallel group is unambiguous first, then redraw the remaining network.
- For resistors connected purely in parallel, the total resistance is lower than the smallest branch resistance.

## Equations

### Resistors in series and parallel

$$R_{\mathrm{series}}=R_1+R_2+\cdots$$

Series combination.

$$\frac{1}{R_{\mathrm{parallel}}}=\frac{1}{R_1}+\frac{1}{R_2}+\cdots$$

Parallel combination.

### Resistance and resistivity

$$R=\frac{V}{I}$$

Resistance at a particular operating point.

$$R=\frac{\rho L}{A}$$

Resistance of a uniform conductor.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Simplify one clear series or parallel group at a time.
2. Redraw the circuit after each simplification.
3. For parallel branches, the equivalent resistance is smaller than the smallest branch resistance.
4. For an ohmic conductor, use the gradient of a \(V\)-against-\(I\) graph.
5. In resistivity work, measure \(L\) and diameter carefully and use \(A=\pi d^2/4\).

## Common mistakes

- Components are in series only if the same current must pass through them with no junction between.
- The reciprocal gradient is required if the graph plots \(I\) against \(V\).
- Resistivity is a material property; resistance also depends on dimensions.
