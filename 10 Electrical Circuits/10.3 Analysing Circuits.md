## Core notes

### Analysing Electrical Circuits

*Source slide 3.*

- Analysis of electrical circuits is a very important skill which is easily transferable to many engineering applications.
- Electrical circuits are everywhere.
- From your pocket calculators to cutting edge technologies such as space probes.
- The equations you have learnt and Kirchhoff's laws are still used to analysis these circuits.

## Equations

### Kirchhoff's laws

$$\sum I_{\mathrm{in}}=\sum I_{\mathrm{out}}$$

First law: conservation of charge at a junction.

$$\sum \mathcal{E}=\sum V$$

Second law around a complete loop: conservation of energy per unit charge.

### Resistors in series and parallel

$$R_{\mathrm{series}}=R_1+R_2+\cdots$$

Series combination.

$$\frac{1}{R_{\mathrm{parallel}}}=\frac{1}{R_1}+\frac{1}{R_2}+\cdots$$

Parallel combination.

### Electrical energy and power

$$P=VI=I^2R=\frac{V^2}{R}$$

Equivalent power relationships.

$$W=Pt=VIt$$

Electrical energy transferred.

$$\text{cost}=\text{energy in kWh}\times\text{price per kWh}$$

Domestic electricity cost.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Mark current directions; a negative answer means the true direction is opposite.
2. Apply the junction rule, then write one independent loop equation at a time.
3. Treat a potential rise and potential drop with consistent signs around each loop.
4. Simplify one clear series or parallel group at a time.
5. Redraw the circuit after each simplification.
6. For parallel branches, the equivalent resistance is smaller than the smallest branch resistance.
7. Choose the power form that uses known quantities.
8. For billing, convert power to kilowatts and time to hours.
9. For SI energy in joules, use power in watts and time in seconds.

## Common mistakes

- Current is not used up by a component; energy is transferred from each coulomb of charge.
- Components are in series only if the same current must pass through them with no junction between.
- A kilowatt-hour is a unit of energy, not power.
