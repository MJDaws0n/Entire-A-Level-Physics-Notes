## Core notes

### The Cosmological Principle

*Source slide 3.*

- We have assumptions about the Universe that gives us a starting point to build our scientific theories from.
- The laws of physics are universal.
- They do not change from place to place or through time.
- The Universe is homogeneous.
- Matter is distributed uniformly over the Universe.

### Edwin Hubble

*Source slide 5.*

- Edwin Hubble was an American astronomer and is considered to be one of the most important cosmologists of the 20th century.
- He showed that the universe had many more galaxies than previously thought and his work also led on to concepts, such as, the expanding universe and the Big Bang.

### Hubble’s Observations

*Source slide 6.*

- He confirmed earlier observations that light from most galaxies was red-shifted.
- He found that in general the further away a galaxy was the greater the red-shift and hence the speed relative to earth.

### Hubble’s Constant

*Source slide 9.*

- Cosmologists prefer speed in km s-1 and distance in Mpc.

### Expanding Universe

*Source slide 12.*

- Hubble’s law is key evidence for the Big Bang theory (Lesson 20.4) and the expanding Universe model.
- As light from galaxies in all directions are red-shifted it means that they are all moving away from us.
- The further the galaxy the greater the speed of recession.
- This model suggest that all galaxies are not just simply moving away from each other but the space between the galaxies is expanding.

### Crisis in Cosmology – Beyond A-level

*Source slide 15.*

- There are currently several methods of measuring the recessional speeds of galaxies.
- Unfortunately for modern cosmologists these different methods do not agree with each other.
- Therefore, scientist believe there is either something wrong with our current methods of measurement or there are fundamental problems with the scientific models we use to describe the Universe.

## Equations

### Hubble's law and the Hubble time

$$v=H_0d$$

Hubble's law.

$$t_{\mathrm{H}}\approx H_0^{-1}$$

Hubble-time estimate after converting \(H_0\) to \(\mathrm{s^{-1}}\).

### Doppler shift and redshift

$$\frac{\Delta\lambda}{\lambda}\approx\frac{v}{c}$$

Non-relativistic Doppler redshift.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Convert megaparsecs to metres before using \(H_0^{-1}\) in seconds.
2. For redshift, \(\Delta\lambda=\lambda_{\mathrm{observed}}-\lambda_{\mathrm{rest}}\).
3. Use the approximation only when speed is much smaller than the speed of light.

## Common mistakes

- Hubble's law describes large-scale cosmic expansion, not ordinary motion through local space.
- Redshift means observed wavelength is longer; blueshift means it is shorter.
