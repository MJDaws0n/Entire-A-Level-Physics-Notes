## Core notes

### 20.2 Doppler Effect

*Source slide 1.*

- Arcsecond is a unit of angle.

### Doppler Shift

*Source slides 4–5.*

- Whenever a source of waves moves relative to an observer, the [[Glossary#Wavelength|wavelength]] and [[Glossary#Frequency|frequency]] of the waves received by the observer change compared with what would be observed without relative motion.
- The magnitude of doppler shift depends upon the relative speed of the wave source to an observer.
- Increasing the relative speed will increase the apparent change in the wavelength and frequency.

### Doppler Red Shift

*Source slide 6.*

- Stars emit (give out) light.
- Light has a wavelength.
- When stars move away from us the light emitted appears to be red shifted.
- Red shift – increase wavelength
- and decreases frequency.

### Absorption Spectra

*Source slides 9–10.*

- Different atoms absorb different wavelengths of electromagnetic radiation.
- The electromagnetic radiation that is absorbed depends upon the difference in [[Glossary#Energy|energy]] of the electron’s energy levels.
- We can use this fact to identify an element.
- The absorption spectra from stars in galaxies can be red-shifted (moving away from Earth) or blue-shifted (moving towards).
- Red shifted Hydrogen absorption spectra

## Equations

### Doppler shift and redshift

$$\frac{\Delta\lambda}{\lambda}\approx\frac{v}{c}$$

Non-relativistic Doppler redshift.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. For redshift, \(\Delta\lambda=\lambda_{\mathrm{observed}}-\lambda_{\mathrm{rest}}\).
2. Use the approximation only when speed is much smaller than the speed of light.

## Common mistakes

- Redshift means observed wavelength is longer; blueshift means it is shorter.
