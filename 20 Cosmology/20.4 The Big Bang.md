## Core notes

### 20.4 The Big Bang theory

*Source slide 1.*

- The laws of physics are universal.
- Laws do not change from place to place or through time.
- The Universe is homogeneous.
- Matter is distributed uniformly over the Universe.
- The Universe is isotropic.

### Red Shifted Galaxies

*Source slides 3–4.*

- Edwin Hubble observed that the light from stars in most galaxies is red shifted.
- Hubble observed that the further away the galaxy away from Earth the more red shifted the light was.
- This means that distant galaxies are moving away faster.
- This is evidence that the Universe is expanding.
- In 1927 Roman Catholic priest, Georges Lemaître, said that because everything was moving away from us this could be traced back in time to an originating single point.
- As galaxies move away from us they are red shifted.

### Georges Lemaître’s Theory

*Source slide 5.*

- Georges Lemaître proposed in 1931 a description of the early universe.
- The Big Bang model describes an early hot, dense Universe and expansion of space; it does not identify an explosion point in pre-existing space.
- As the Universe expanded, it cooled.
- He simply played Hubble’s observations in reverse.

### Support for the Big Bang Model

*Source slide 6.*

- Scientific theories, unlike some other ‘theories’, need evidence to be accepted in the scientific community.
- The two pieces of evidence which support the Big-bang model are Hubble’s law (expanding Universe) and the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB).
- Hubble’s law alone doesn’t solely support the Big Bang model.
- Although when Hubble’s law is coupled with the CMB no other theory can currently explain the evidence more succinctly.

### Microwave Background Radiation

*Source slide 8.*

- The CMB was discovered in 1964 by Robert Wilson and Arno Penzias.
- No matter which direction they pointed their telescope they had a background noise in their signal.
- They took to great lengths to identify the source of the signal.
- They climbed upon their telescope and notice some “white dielectric material” which they cleaned off.
- Yet this was to no avail.

### Cosmic Microwave Background

*Source slide 10.*

- Although it appears that there are hotter sections (in red) the differences in temperature are extremely small.
- On this map, the hot regions, shown in red, are 0.0002 K hotter than the cold regions, shown in blue.

### Source of the Radiation

*Source slide 11.*

- It had been predicted that the Big-bang model was correct there would be a uniformed background radiation and this was the source of Robert Wilson and Arno Penzias signal.
- When the universe was young and extremely hot, space was saturated with high [[Glossary#Energy|energy]] gamma photons.
- The expansion of the universe stretched the [[Glossary#Wavelength|wavelength]] of these photons to be radio waves and microwaves.
- The universe was very hot yet the expansion of the universe has left it being cooler, 2.7 K on average.
- We can treat the universe as a ‘black body’ and get a peak wavelength of 1 mm which is in the region of microwaves.

## Method

1. Connect each conclusion to evidence: galactic redshift, cosmic microwave background radiation, and primordial light-element abundances.
2. Describe expansion as increasing scale between unbound galaxies, not an explosion from a point into pre-existing space.
