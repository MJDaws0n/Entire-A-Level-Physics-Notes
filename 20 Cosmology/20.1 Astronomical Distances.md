## Core notes

### Hubble Ultra Deep Field Image from the Hubble Space telescope in 2004

*Source slide 3.*

- This image shows a tiny portion of space from the southern hemisphere.
- This portion of space is less than the size of a grain of rice held at arm's length.
- Due to the increasable distances involved in the universe we will need to define some new and more convenient units to start our brief journey into cosmology.

### Astronomical Distances

*Source slide 4.*

- The Earth is on average 150 million km from the Sun.
- This is 1 astronomical unit (1 AU).

### Parsec (pc)

*Source slide 9.*

- The parsec is defined as the distance
- at which a radius of one AU
- subtends an angle of one arcsecond.

### Distance and Angle

*Source slide 12.*

- At 1 pc the angle was 1 arcsecond.
- As the distance increases from 1 pc to 2 pc
- the angle decreases.
- At 2 pc the angle is ½ arcsecond.
- At 3 pc the angle is 1/3 arcsecond.

### Parallax Angle

*Source slides 13–14.*

- Objects appear to shift their position depending on your position.
- Small people appear shorter to tall people.
- Tall people appear taller to shorter people.
- This affect can be used to measure the distance to objects less than 100 pc away from Earth!
- Apparent position of nearby star

## Method

1. Use half the total apparent annual angular shift as the parallax angle when appropriate.
2. Match the distance unit to the scale and convert only when the calculation requires it.

## Common mistakes

- A light-year is a distance, not a time.
