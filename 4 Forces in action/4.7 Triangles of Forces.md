### Model Dolphin

*Source slide 3.*

- A model dolphin is suspended by two ropes in a museum.
- Net zero [[Glossary#Force|force]] means the object is in equilibrium.
- As the dolphin is in equilibrium then the forces need to be balanced and this means they form a closed triangle.

### Why does this work?

*Source slides 4–5.*

- At GCSE we discussed what it means for forces to be balanced (zero net force) in 1 dimension.
- Triangle of forces is simply an extension of this idea to 2 dimensions.
- We can then split this into two 1 dimensions again.
- We can then show this mathematically with resolving the vectors into their independent axes.
- For example, we can resolve T into its vertical and horizontal components.

## Equations

### Forces and Newton's laws

$$\sum F=ma$$

Resultant force causes acceleration.

$$W=mg$$

Weight in a gravitational field.

### Vectors and components

$$F_x=F\cos\theta$$

Component adjacent to the stated angle.

$$F_y=F\sin\theta$$

Component opposite the stated angle.

$$R=\sqrt{R_x^2+R_y^2}$$

Magnitude from perpendicular components.

$$\theta=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{R_y}{R_x}\right)$$

Direction from perpendicular components; check the quadrant.

### Vector geometry

$$a^2=b^2+c^2$$

Pythagoras for a right-angled vector triangle.

$$a^2=b^2+c^2-2bc\cos A$$

Cosine rule.

$$\frac{a}{\sin A}=\frac{b}{\sin B}=\frac{c}{\sin C}$$

Sine rule.

## Method

1. Isolate the object and draw only forces acting on it.
2. Choose axes, resolve forces if needed, then write \(\sum F=ma\) along each axis.
3. For equilibrium or constant velocity, set the resultant force to zero.
4. Choose positive directions and draw a labelled sketch.
5. Resolve every vector along the same pair of perpendicular axes.
6. Add components with signs, then find the magnitude and direction of the resultant.
7. Draw vectors head-to-tail and label every side and angle.
8. Use Pythagoras for perpendicular vectors; otherwise use the sine or cosine rule.
9. Give the direction relative to a named axis or compass direction.

## Common mistakes

- Newton's third-law pair acts on different objects, so the two forces do not cancel on one free-body diagram.
- Mass is measured in kilograms; weight is a force measured in newtons.
- A negative component means the vector points opposite to the chosen positive direction.
- State directions with the final magnitude; a magnitude alone is not a complete vector answer.
- The internal angle in the triangle may be \(180^{\circ}\) minus the angle shown between forces.
