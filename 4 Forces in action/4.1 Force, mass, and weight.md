### Force and acceleration

*Source slide 4.*

- Forces (push and pulls) changes an objects position.
- Therefore forces change an objects [[Glossary#Velocity|velocity]] (speed and direction).
- Change of velocity is [[Glossary#Acceleration|acceleration]].
- When mass is constant.

### Understanding Mass: Beyond the Course

*Source slides 7–8.*

- Isaac Newton in the 17th century developed the concept of mass.
- Mass is an abstract quantity which can be thought of as the constant of proportionality between [[Glossary#Force|force]] and acceleration.
- In Newtonian physics mass is presumed to be independent of velocity.
- In 1905, Einstein published his theory of special relativity which stated that mass does depend upon your velocity.
- The faster you go the more massive you become.
- m is the relativistic mass (kg)
- mo is the rest mass of the object (kg)

## Equations

### Forces and Newton's laws

$$\sum F=ma$$

Resultant force causes acceleration.

$$W=mg$$

Weight in a gravitational field.

## Method

1. Isolate the object and draw only forces acting on it.
2. Choose axes, resolve forces if needed, then write \(\sum F=ma\) along each axis.
3. For equilibrium or constant velocity, set the resultant force to zero.

## Common mistakes

- Newton's third-law pair acts on different objects, so the two forces do not cancel on one free-body diagram.
- Mass is measured in kilograms; weight is a force measured in newtons.
