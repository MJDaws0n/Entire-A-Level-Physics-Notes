## Core notes

### 4.5 Moments and Equilibrium

*Source slide 1.*

- The [[Glossary#Drag|drag]] [[Glossary#Force|force]] is due to an object colliding with the particles in that fluid.

### Turning Moments

*Source slides 3–4.*

- Moments is a turning effect and can be clockwise or anticlockwise.
- A turning effect in physics is a moment.
- Moments can be clockwise or anti-clockwise.
- To achieve a moment you need a pivot and a force some distance away from the pivot.

### Equilibrium: The Principle of Moments

*Source slide 10.*

- When a body is in equilibrium, the net forces and the net moments on that object are zero.
- This means the object is not accelerating – no net forces.
- This means the object is not rotating – no net moment.

### Defining the Moment

*Source slide 11.*

- The moment is a turning effect about some axis or point.

### Moments at different points

*Source slides 18–20.*

- In a system that is in static equilibrium we can take moments at any point and the clockwise moments will be equal to the anticlockwise moment.
- Previously, with the figure below, we took moments about R.
- Now we will take moments at A and B.
- Therefore, we have shown in cases of static equilibrium, we can choose which ever point we want to take moments from and this will not affect our result.
- In many problems we will pick a point that will simplify the problem.
- We will pick a point so we can ignore that points contribution to the moment.
- Let’s say I didn’t know the force at A nor B.

## Equations

### Moments, couples and equilibrium

$$M=Fx$$

Moment equals force times perpendicular distance from the pivot.

$$\tau=Fd$$

Torque of a couple; \(d\) is the perpendicular separation of the forces.

$$\sum M_{\mathrm{clockwise}}=\sum M_{\mathrm{anticlockwise}}$$

Rotational equilibrium.

### Forces and Newton's laws

$$\sum F=ma$$

Resultant force causes acceleration.

$$W=mg$$

Weight in a gravitational field.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Choose a pivot that removes as many unknown forces as possible.
2. Use the perpendicular distance from pivot to line of action, not simply the labelled length.
3. For full equilibrium, also require zero resultant force.
4. Isolate the object and draw only forces acting on it.
5. Choose axes, resolve forces if needed, then write \(\sum F=ma\) along each axis.
6. For equilibrium or constant velocity, set the resultant force to zero.

## Common mistakes

- Moment and energy share the unit \(\mathrm{N\,m}\), but a moment is not written in joules.
- Newton's third-law pair acts on different objects, so the two forces do not cancel on one free-body diagram.
- Mass is measured in kilograms; weight is a force measured in newtons.
