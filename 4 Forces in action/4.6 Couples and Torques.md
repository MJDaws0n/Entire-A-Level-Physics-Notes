### You spin me right around…

*Source slides 3–4.*

- Imagine you are pushing around a small child.
- If the [[Glossary#Force|force]] (line of action) is applied through the child’s centre of gravity it will travel in a straight line.
- If the force (line of action) is applied not through the child’s centre of gravity it will rotate yet with translational motion.
- Translational motion: moving through space.
- We can apply equal and opposite forces on the child.
- Forces that produce rotational motion and no translational motion are called couples.

### Moments, Couples, and Torques

*Source slide 5.*

- I have an object with a centre of mass which has a diameter of d.
- I apply two forces in opposite directions both with magnitude F.
- Each force is contributing to the moment as both are in the clockwise direction.
- : The moment of a couple is called a [[Glossary#Torque|torque]].

## Equations

### Moments, couples and equilibrium

$$M=Fx$$

Moment equals force times perpendicular distance from the pivot.

$$\tau=Fd$$

Torque of a couple; \(d\) is the perpendicular separation of the forces.

$$\sum M_{\mathrm{clockwise}}=\sum M_{\mathrm{anticlockwise}}$$

Rotational equilibrium.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Choose a pivot that removes as many unknown forces as possible.
2. Use the perpendicular distance from pivot to line of action, not simply the labelled length.
3. For full equilibrium, also require zero resultant force.

## Common mistakes

- Moment and energy share the unit \(\mathrm{N\,m}\), but a moment is not written in joules.
