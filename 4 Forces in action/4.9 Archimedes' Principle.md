## Core notes

### 4.9 Archimedes’ Principle

*Source slide 1.*

- You would determine the mass in kilograms by placing the object upon a scale.
- You would then obtain a measuring cylinder and place a known amount of water (500 mL) into that cylinder.
- You would then submerge the irregular object into the cylinder and find the total displaced water by finding the difference from the starting measurement to the final measurement.
- The displaced water is the volume of the object.

### What is a Fluid?

*Source slide 3.*

- To a physicist gases and liquids are classed as fluids.
- Liquids and gases are called fluids because they can be made to flow.
- In any fluid, the molecules themselves are in constant, random motion, colliding with each other and with the walls of any container producing a [[Glossary#Pressure|pressure]].

### Archimedes’ Principle

*Source slides 7–8.*

- The [[Glossary#Upthrust|upthrust]] exerted on a body immersed in a fluid, whether fully or partially submerged, is equal to the weight of the fluid that the body displaces.
- For a floating object the upthrust must be equal to the weight of the object.
- This in turn means the weight of the object equals the weight of the displaced fluid.

## Equations

### Upthrust and Archimedes' principle

$$U=\rho_{\mathrm{fluid}}V_{\mathrm{displaced}}g$$

Upthrust equals the weight of displaced fluid.

$$U=W\quad\text{for a floating object in equilibrium}$$

Balanced vertical forces.

### Density and pressure

$$\rho=\frac{m}{V}$$

Density.

$$p=\frac{F}{A}$$

Pressure caused by a normal force.

$$\Delta p=h\rho g$$

Pressure difference in a fluid column.

### Forces and Newton's laws

$$\sum F=ma$$

Resultant force causes acceleration.

$$W=mg$$

Weight in a gravitational field.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Find the volume of fluid displaced, not automatically the whole object volume.
2. Use the fluid density in the upthrust equation.
3. Apply vertical force balance or \(\sum F=ma\) after calculating upthrust.
4. Convert mass, volume, length and area to SI units before substituting.
5. For liquid pressure, use vertical depth below the surface.
6. For total pressure, add atmospheric pressure only when the question requires absolute pressure.
7. Isolate the object and draw only forces acting on it.
8. Choose axes, resolve forces if needed, then write \(\sum F=ma\) along each axis.
9. For equilibrium or constant velocity, set the resultant force to zero.

## Common mistakes

- A submerged object can have an upthrust smaller than, equal to, or greater than its weight depending on its motion.
- \(1\,\mathrm{cm^3}=10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}\), not \(10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^3}\).
- Newton's third-law pair acts on different objects, so the two forces do not cancel on one free-body diagram.
- Mass is measured in kilograms; weight is a force measured in newtons.
