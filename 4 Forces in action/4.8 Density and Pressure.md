## Core notes

### Proportionalities

*Source slides 4–10.*

- As I increase the volume I decrease the [[Glossary#Density|density]].
- As I increase the mass I increase the density.
- Volume and density are inversely proportional.
- Mass and density are proportional.
- As I increase the area I decrease the [[Glossary#Pressure|pressure]].
- As I increase the [[Glossary#Force|force]] I increase the pressure.
- Force and area are inversely proportional.

### Converting between cm3 and m3

*Source slide 5.*

- We will at times need to convert between cm3 and m3.
- For this we will need to use standard form and the rules for indices.

### Determining Density

*Source slide 7.*

- To determine density we need to measure the mass of that object with a scale.
- For regularly shaped objects (cuboids) we use a ruler, digital callipers or a micrometre to measure the dimensions of that object and then calculate the volume.

### Regular and Irregular Shapes

*Source slide 8.*

- To measure the volume of an irregular shape we can use the [[Glossary#Displacement|displacement]] of a liquid.
- The volume of liquid displaced is the volume of the object.

### Pressure

*Source slide 9.*

- Pressure if the [[Glossary#Normal|normal]] force per unit cross-sectional area.
- You are continually under a huge amount of pressure.
- The earth’s atmosphere exerts a pressure of 1.0 x 105 Pa on everything on the earth’s surface.
- Small fluctuations in this pressure can contribute to large weather fluctuations.
- F: Force normal to the surface – N

## Equations

### Density and pressure

$$\rho=\frac{m}{V}$$

Density.

$$p=\frac{F}{A}$$

Pressure caused by a normal force.

$$\Delta p=h\rho g$$

Pressure difference in a fluid column.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Convert mass, volume, length and area to SI units before substituting.
2. For liquid pressure, use vertical depth below the surface.
3. For total pressure, add atmospheric pressure only when the question requires absolute pressure.

## Common mistakes

- \(1\,\mathrm{cm^3}=10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}\), not \(10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m^3}\).
