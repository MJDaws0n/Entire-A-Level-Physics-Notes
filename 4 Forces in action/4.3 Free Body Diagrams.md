### Free-Body Diagrams

*Source slide 3.*

- A free-body diagram is a simplified drawing with [[Glossary#Vector|vector]] arrows placed upon it.
- Anchor our vectors from the centre of mass.

### Forces

*Source slides 4–7.*

- Weight is the gravitational force produced when a mass is placed into a gravitational field, g.
- Weight acts through the object’s centre of mass (gravity).
- Friction is a force that opposes motion of an object.
- [[Glossary#Drag|Drag]] is a force that opposes motion when an object is travelling through a fluid (gas or liquid).
- An upward buoyancy force acting on an object when it’s in a fluid.
- This is due to the [[Glossary#Pressure|pressure]] difference between the top and bottom of a submerged object.
- The force within a stretched cable or rope.

### Forces on a slope

*Source slide 9.*

- We need to be able to describe forces when they are on a slope in a mathematical way.
- Let us assume that there is no friction and the forces acting is the object’s weight and the [[Glossary#Normal|normal]].

## Equations

### Forces and Newton's laws

$$\sum F=ma$$

Resultant force causes acceleration.

$$W=mg$$

Weight in a gravitational field.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Isolate the object and draw only forces acting on it.
2. Choose axes, resolve forces if needed, then write \(\sum F=ma\) along each axis.
3. For equilibrium or constant velocity, set the resultant force to zero.

## Common mistakes

- Newton's third-law pair acts on different objects, so the two forces do not cancel on one free-body diagram.
- Mass is measured in kilograms; weight is a force measured in newtons.
