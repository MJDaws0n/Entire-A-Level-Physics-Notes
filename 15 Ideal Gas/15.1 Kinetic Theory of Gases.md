### Particles, Moles and Avogadro

*Source slides 4–5.*

- We can express the number of atoms or molecules in a given volume of gas using moles (mol), the SI unit of measurement for the amount of substance.
- If I ask for two moles of a substance I will get simply two times Avogadro's constant.

### Kinetic Theory of Gases

*Source slides 7–8.*

- The kinetic theory of gases allows us to link microscopic quantities to macroscopic quantities.
- The gas contains a very large number of atoms or molecules moving in ‘random’ directions with ‘random’ speeds.
- The atoms or molecules of the gas occupy a negligible volume compared with the volume of gas.
- Large number with ‘random’ direction and speed.

### Describing Pressure

*Source slides 10–12.*

- With Newton’s law and the kinetic theory of gases for an ideal gas we will be able to derive the macroscopic quantity of [[Glossary#Pressure|pressure]] from microscopic quantities.
- Equations we will need
- Lets us take one particle from the ideal gas.
- It is going to collide with the fixed container wall.
- The particle will start with a [[Glossary#Velocity|velocity]] u.
- It collides with the wall elastically.
- The particle will continue to bounce backwards and forwards between the container walls.

## Equations

### Ideal gases and kinetic theory

$$pV=NkT=nRT$$

Ideal gas equation.

$$pV=\frac{1}{3}Nm\langle c^2\rangle$$

Kinetic-theory pressure relationship.

$$\frac{1}{2}m\langle c^2\rangle=\frac{3}{2}kT$$

Mean translational kinetic energy.

$$c_{\mathrm{rms}}=\sqrt{\langle c^2\rangle}=\sqrt{\frac{3kT}{m}}$$

Root-mean-square molecular speed.

## Method

1. Use kelvin, cubic metres and pascals in gas calculations.
2. Choose \(N\) with \(k\), or moles \(n\) with \(R\); do not mix them.
3. For a fixed amount of gas, compare states using \(pV/T=\text{constant}\).

## Common mistakes

- The mean molecular velocity can be zero even though mean speed and rms speed are not.

> [!note]- Slide 9: Assumptions for an ideal gas:
> - Collisions are perfectly elastic, so total [[Glossary#Kinetic Energy|kinetic energy]] is conserved, although particles may exchange energy.
> - The time of collisions between the atoms or molecules is negligible compared to the time between collisions.
> - Electrostatic forces between atoms or molecules are negligible except during collisions.

> [!note]- Slide 10: Describing Pressure
> - Pressure = [[Glossary#Force|Force]] / Area (P = F / A)
> - [[Glossary#Momentum|Momentum]] = mass x velocity (p = mv)
> - Force = ∆momentum / ∆time (F = ∆p / ∆t)
> - ∆ = change = finial – initial.

> [!note]- Slide 11: Describing Pressure
> - Initial momentum: p = m u
> - Finial momentum: p = -m u
> - ∆ p = - m u – m u
> - ∆ p = - 2 m u

> [!note]- Slide 12: Describing Pressure
> - According to Newton’s third law for each actions there is corresponding force.
> - This means that a force is exerted on the walls of F = ∆ p / ∆ t where t is the time between collision and ∆ p = - 2mu.
> - If we have N particles colliding on the walls we can determine the pressure on the walls from P = F / A where A is the cross-sectional area of the container wall.
