### Ludwig Boltzmann

*Source slide 3.*

- Ludwig Boltzmann was an Austrian physicist and philosopher operating at the turn of the 19th to 20th century.
- At this time the question of the reality of atoms as physical objects was debated between the atomists and non-atomists.
- Boltzmann was a strong atomists and argued for atoms to be considered as real entities not just a mathematical framework.
- Due to his ground-breaking work in statistical physics, linking microscopic phenomena to macroscopic measurable quantities, he had a constant named in his honour, the Boltzmann constant, kB or k.

### Where is all the helium?

*Source slide 10.*

- Therefore, we can now explain why the earth’s atmosphere has so little helium compared to the rest of the universe.
- The range of speeds of particles in a gas at a given temperature is known as the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution

### Internal Energy of an Ideal Gas

*Source slide 11.*

- The [[Glossary#Internal Energy|internal energy]] of a substance is the sum of kinetic energies and potential energies.
- For an ideal gas we presume that there are only electrostatic forces between particles and the container walls when they collide and no forces between the individual gas particles.
- Therefore we are treating an ideal gas as it has no potential [[Glossary#Energy|energy]] component.
- So, when we increase the [[Glossary#Kinetic Energy|kinetic energy]] of the particles by a factor of two we are also increasing the internal energy by a factor of two.

## Equations

### Ideal gases and kinetic theory

$$pV=NkT=nRT$$

Ideal gas equation.

$$pV=\frac{1}{3}Nm\langle c^2\rangle$$

Kinetic-theory pressure relationship.

$$\frac{1}{2}m\langle c^2\rangle=\frac{3}{2}kT$$

Mean translational kinetic energy.

$$c_{\mathrm{rms}}=\sqrt{\langle c^2\rangle}=\sqrt{\frac{3kT}{m}}$$

Root-mean-square molecular speed.

## Method

1. Use kelvin, cubic metres and pascals in gas calculations.
2. Choose \(N\) with \(k\), or moles \(n\) with \(R\); do not mix them.
3. For a fixed amount of gas, compare states using \(pV/T=\text{constant}\).
