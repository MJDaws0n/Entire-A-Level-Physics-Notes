## Core notes

### 15.2 Gas Laws

*Source slide 1.*

- The gas contains a very large number of atoms or molecules moving in ‘random’ directions with ‘random’ speeds.
- The atoms or molecules of the gas occupy a negligible volume compared with the volume of gas
- Collisions are perfectly elastic, so total [[Glossary#Kinetic Energy|kinetic energy]] is conserved, although particles may exchange energy.
- The time of collisions between the atoms or molecules is negligible compared to the time between collisions.
- Electrostatic forces between atoms or molecules are negligible except during collisions.

### Pressure and Volume

*Source slides 3–4.*

- As you change the volume of a container the [[Glossary#Pressure|pressure]] also changes.
- It would increase.
- Therefore, pressure and volume are inversely proportional.
- P – pressure (Pa or N/m2)
- We can make the proportional symbol into an equality with the use of a constant.

### Pressure and Temperature

*Source slide 6.*

- Pressure is a macroscopic quantity related to microscopic quantity, [[Glossary#Velocity|velocity]] of the particles.

### Estimating Absolute Zero

*Source slide 7.*

- From the relationship of pressure and temperature we can now estimate the value of absolute zero for a mixed volume and mass of gas.
- At absolute zero the [[Glossary#Internal Energy|internal energy]], by definition, must be at a minimum and therefore the pressure must be zero as the particles are not moving.
- The data was collected in an investigation into the pressure of a fixed mass and volume of gas as the temperature is changed.
- a) Plot a graph of pressure against temperature.
- b) Use the graph to determine a value of absolute zero.

*Source slide 10.*

- For one mole of an ideal gas, the constant in the combined relationship above is called the molar gas constant, R, and is equal to 8.31 JK-1mol-1.
- This relationship is called the equation of state of an ideal gas.
- The molar gas constant is the same for all gases, as long as we treat them as being ideal.

## Equations

### Ideal gases and kinetic theory

$$pV=NkT=nRT$$

Ideal gas equation.

$$pV=\frac{1}{3}Nm\langle c^2\rangle$$

Kinetic-theory pressure relationship.

$$\frac{1}{2}m\langle c^2\rangle=\frac{3}{2}kT$$

Mean translational kinetic energy.

$$c_{\mathrm{rms}}=\sqrt{\langle c^2\rangle}=\sqrt{\frac{3kT}{m}}$$

Root-mean-square molecular speed.

## Method

1. Use kelvin, cubic metres and pascals in gas calculations.
2. Choose \(N\) with \(k\), or moles \(n\) with \(R\); do not mix them.
3. For a fixed amount of gas, compare states using \(pV/T=\text{constant}\).

## Common mistakes

- The mean molecular velocity can be zero even though mean speed and rms speed are not.
