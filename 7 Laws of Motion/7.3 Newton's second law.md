### Newton’s Second Law

*Source slides 4–5.*

- Newton’s second law links together [[Glossary#Force|force]] and the change of [[Glossary#Momentum|momentum]].
- The net (resultant) force acting on an object is directly proportional to the rate of change of its momentum.
- We define the unit of force as being: the force required to give a 1 kg mass an [[Glossary#Acceleration|acceleration]] of 1 m s-2 is 1 N.
- From this equation state the units of force.

## Equations

### Momentum and impulse

$$p=mv$$

Linear momentum.

$$F=\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$$

Mean resultant force.

$$J=F\Delta t=\Delta p$$

Impulse for a constant/mean force.

$$\sum p_{\mathrm{before}}=\sum p_{\mathrm{after}}$$

Momentum conservation in an isolated system.

### Forces and Newton's laws

$$\sum F=ma$$

Resultant force causes acceleration.

$$W=mg$$

Weight in a gravitational field.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Choose a positive direction and give every velocity a sign.
2. Calculate total momentum before and after along each independent axis.
3. Conserve momentum only when external impulse is negligible.
4. Isolate the object and draw only forces acting on it.
5. Choose axes, resolve forces if needed, then write \(\sum F=ma\) along each axis.
6. For equilibrium or constant velocity, set the resultant force to zero.

## Common mistakes

- Kinetic energy is conserved only in an elastic collision; momentum is conserved in every isolated collision.
- Newton's third-law pair acts on different objects, so the two forces do not cancel on one free-body diagram.
- Mass is measured in kilograms; weight is a force measured in newtons.
