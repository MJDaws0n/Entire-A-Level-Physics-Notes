### Newton’s First Law

*Source slide 3.*

- “An object will continue to stay at rest, or move with constant [[Glossary#Velocity|velocity]], unless a resultant [[Glossary#Force|force]] acts upon it.” (Isaac Newton, 1687.)
- “Things don’t change unless you poke it or tug at it”

### Balanced and Unbalanced Forces

*Source slide 4.*

- Forces can be unbalanced.
- This means that the object accelerates.
- Forces can be balanced.
- This means there is no [[Glossary#Acceleration|acceleration]].

### Newton’s Third Law

*Source slide 7.*

- “When two objects interact, they exert equal and opposite forces on each other.”
- “When you push, things push back!”

### Equal and Opposite Forces

*Source slide 8.*

- The forces exerted are always equal and opposite to each other.

### Fundamental Forces

*Source slide 9.*

- Gravitational force – produced with a mass in a gravitational field.
- Electrostatic force – between charged objects.
- Strong nuclear force - attractive force between protons and neutrons that keep the nucleus together.
- Weak nuclear force – governs certain radioactive decays.

### Ginny on a lonely walk

*Source slide 10.*

- Ginny walks down her school corridor while pondering which forces are at play.
- It is the electrostatic repulsion between the electrons that ensure Ginny doesn’t fall through the ceiling of the classroom below!

## Equations

### Forces and Newton's laws

$$\sum F=ma$$

Resultant force causes acceleration.

$$W=mg$$

Weight in a gravitational field.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Isolate the object and draw only forces acting on it.
2. Choose axes, resolve forces if needed, then write \(\sum F=ma\) along each axis.
3. For equilibrium or constant velocity, set the resultant force to zero.

## Common mistakes

- Newton's third-law pair acts on different objects, so the two forces do not cancel on one free-body diagram.
- Mass is measured in kilograms; weight is a force measured in newtons.
