## Core notes

### 7.2 Linear Momentum

*Source slide 1.*

- “An object will continue to stay at rest, or move with constant [[Glossary#Velocity|velocity]], unless a resultant [[Glossary#Force|force]] acts upon it.”
- “When two objects interact, they exert equal and opposite forces on each other.”

### Linear Momentum

*Source slide 3.*

- Linear (meaning straight line and not rotational) [[Glossary#Momentum|momentum]] depends upon the mass of the object and its velocity.

### Momentum as a Vector

*Source slide 4.*

- Imagine two cars both with a mass of 2000 kg.
- Both travel with a velocity of magnitude 20 ms-1.
- Yet they are traveling in opposite directions.
- To find the momentum we need to state a positive direction.

### Principle of Conservation of Momentum

*Source slide 5.*

- When two objects interact or collide the total momentum of those two objects does not change providing no external forces act.
- “For a system of interacting objects, the total momentum in a specified direction remains constant, as long as no external forces act on the system.”

### Conservation of Momentum

*Source slide 6.*

- We know that these cars have a momentum of magnitude 4 x 104 kg ms-1.
- The total momentum in each direction is 4 x 104 kg ms-1.
- Therefore, after the collision the total momentum will be the same.

### Elastic and Inelastic Collisions

*Source slide 7.*

- Momentum and total [[Glossary#Energy|energy]] are both conserved when two objects collide.
- All the [[Glossary#Kinetic Energy|kinetic energy]] could be retained by the objects or this kinetic energy can be transferred to a thermal store.
- Momentum, total energy and kinetic energy are conserved.
- During inelastic collisions kinetic energy is not conserved.

## Equations

### Momentum and impulse

$$p=mv$$

Linear momentum.

$$F=\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$$

Mean resultant force.

$$J=F\Delta t=\Delta p$$

Impulse for a constant/mean force.

$$\sum p_{\mathrm{before}}=\sum p_{\mathrm{after}}$$

Momentum conservation in an isolated system.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Choose a positive direction and give every velocity a sign.
2. Calculate total momentum before and after along each independent axis.
3. Conserve momentum only when external impulse is negligible.

## Common mistakes

- Kinetic energy is conserved only in an elastic collision; momentum is conserved in every isolated collision.
