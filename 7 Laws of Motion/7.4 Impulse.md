### 7.4 Impulse

*Source slide 1.*

- The minus means the [[Glossary#Force|force]] acts in the opposite direction to the initial [[Glossary#Velocity|velocity]].

### What is Impulse?

*Source slide 3.*

- When a ball is kicked, a car crashes, or a child falls, it happens in a certain time.
- With the idea of [[Glossary#Impulse|impulse]] we can characterise these situations.
- Impulse of a force is defined as the product of force and the time for which this force acts on an object.
- So, impulse equals the change of [[Glossary#Momentum|momentum]].
- The units of impulse is N s or kg ms-1.

### Impulse and force-time graphs

*Source slides 4–6.*

- If a constant force is applied we can determine the impulse simply by finding the area of the rectangle.
- It the force changes over time we may be able to split it into triangles and rectangles.
- It the force changes over time in a non-linear way
- we will need to count the squares!

### Photons and Momentum

*Source slide 8.*

- Photons are subatomic particles which travel at the speed of light and so have no mass.
- Although photons have no mass they still have a momentum.
- Photons exert a tiny force when they collide with objects.
- The photons from the sun exert a radiation [[Glossary#Pressure|pressure]] of around 9 μPa per meter squared on Earth.

## Equations

### Momentum and impulse

$$p=mv$$

Linear momentum.

$$F=\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$$

Mean resultant force.

$$J=F\Delta t=\Delta p$$

Impulse for a constant/mean force.

$$\sum p_{\mathrm{before}}=\sum p_{\mathrm{after}}$$

Momentum conservation in an isolated system.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Choose a positive direction and give every velocity a sign.
2. Calculate total momentum before and after along each independent axis.
3. Conserve momentum only when external impulse is negligible.

## Common mistakes

- Kinetic energy is conserved only in an elastic collision; momentum is conserved in every isolated collision.
