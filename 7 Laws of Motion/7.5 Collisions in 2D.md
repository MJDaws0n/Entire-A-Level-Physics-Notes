## Core notes

### Forces in 2D

*Source slide 3.*

- We can solve problems ranging from snooker balls collisions to particle collisions with the same physics.
- Total [[Glossary#Energy|energy]] is conserved, and linear [[Glossary#Momentum|momentum]] is conserved when the external [[Glossary#Impulse|impulse]] on the system is negligible.

### We can start with two balls.

*Source slide 4.*

- The red is at rest and the cue ball has a [[Glossary#Velocity|velocity]], v, and momentum, p.
- When they collide the balls move off with different momentum in different directions.
- As linear momentum is conserved we can create a [[Glossary#Vector|vector]] triangle.

### Vector Triangles

*Source slide 5.*

- Let us have another look.
- Particle A hits stationary particle B.
- After the collision particles A and B move off with momentum p2 and p1 respectively.
- Since linear momentum is conserved, p1 + p2 (finial momentum) must equal the initial momentum, p.
- Therefore we can draw a vector triangle.

### Resolving Vectors

*Source slide 9.*

- We could also split the momentum into its vertical (y) and horizontal (x) components.
- Then we simply equate before and after and solve for the unknown quantity we want.

## Equations

### Momentum and impulse

$$p=mv$$

Linear momentum.

$$F=\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$$

Mean resultant force.

$$J=F\Delta t=\Delta p$$

Impulse for a constant/mean force.

$$\sum p_{\mathrm{before}}=\sum p_{\mathrm{after}}$$

Momentum conservation in an isolated system.

### Vectors and components

$$F_x=F\cos\theta$$

Component adjacent to the stated angle.

$$F_y=F\sin\theta$$

Component opposite the stated angle.

$$R=\sqrt{R_x^2+R_y^2}$$

Magnitude from perpendicular components.

$$\theta=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{R_y}{R_x}\right)$$

Direction from perpendicular components; check the quadrant.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Choose a positive direction and give every velocity a sign.
2. Calculate total momentum before and after along each independent axis.
3. Conserve momentum only when external impulse is negligible.
4. Choose positive directions and draw a labelled sketch.
5. Resolve every vector along the same pair of perpendicular axes.
6. Add components with signs, then find the magnitude and direction of the resultant.

## Common mistakes

- Kinetic energy is conserved only in an elastic collision; momentum is conserved in every isolated collision.
- A negative component means the vector points opposite to the chosen positive direction.
- State directions with the final magnitude; a magnitude alone is not a complete vector answer.
