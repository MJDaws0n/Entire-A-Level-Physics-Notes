## Core notes

### 25.4 Radioactive Decay Calculations

*Source slide 1.*

- Radioactive decays are random and spontaneous.
- They are random because we cannot predict when a particular nucleus will decay.
- Each nucleus has the same chance of decaying.
- Decays are spontaneous because the decay is unaffected by the presence of other nuclei
- and external factors such as [[Glossary#Pressure|pressure]].

## Equations

### Radioactive decay

$$A=\lambda N$$

Activity.

$$\frac{\Delta N}{\Delta t}=-\lambda N$$

Decay rate.

$$N=N_0e^{-\lambda t},\qquad A=A_0e^{-\lambda t}$$

Exponential decay.

$$\lambda t_{1/2}=\ln 2$$

Half-life relationship.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Subtract background count rate before analysing source data.
2. Use repeated half-lives for simple factors of two; otherwise use the exponential equation.
3. A plot of \(\ln A\) or \(\ln N\) against \(t\) has gradient \(-\lambda\).

## Common mistakes

- Decay is random for one nucleus but statistically predictable for a large sample.
- Activity is measured in becquerels; count rate depends on detector efficiency and geometry.
