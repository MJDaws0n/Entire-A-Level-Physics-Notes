## Core notes

### 25.2 Nuclear Decay Equations

*Source slide 1.*

- A nucleus emits radiation when it is unstable.
- α has +2e charge β– has –e charge
- β+ has +e charge γ has no charge.

### Conservation Rules

*Source slide 5.*

- Conservation rules are important in physics.
- Fermi used [[Glossary#Conservation Of Momentum|conservation of momentum]] to postulate a new subatomic particle called the neutrino (Lesson 24.5)
- Einstein showed that mass and [[Glossary#Energy|energy]] are interchangeable.
- This means that and conservation of mass-energy is also a law of physics (Lesson 26.1).
- In nuclear decays the [[Glossary#Nucleon|nucleon]] number A and the proton number (atomic number) Z are conserved.

### Nuclear notation

*Source slide 6.*

- We can represent elements, their isotopes, and radiation with nuclear notation.

### Decay Equations

*Source slide 7.*

- In nuclear decays the parent nuclei is unstable.
- The parent decays into a daughter nuclei
- and emits a form of radiation.
- The nucleon number (A) is conserved.
- The atomic number (Z) is conserved.

### Decay Chains

*Source slide 18.*

- Nuclear decays can be a multistep process.
- A decay chain is formed when the daughter nucleus is unstable and will also decay.

## Equations

### Particle and nuclear decay equations

$${}^{A}_{Z}X\rightarrow{}^{A-4}_{Z-2}Y+{}^{4}_{2}\alpha$$

Alpha decay.

$$n\rightarrow p+e^-+\bar{\nu}_e$$

Beta-minus decay; quark change \(d\rightarrow u\).

$$p\rightarrow n+e^++\nu_e$$

Beta-plus decay; quark change \(u\rightarrow d\).

$$e^-+e^+\rightarrow2\gamma$$

Electron-positron annihilation.

### Nuclear and particle structure

$$R=r_0A^{1/3}$$

Nuclear radius model.

$$A=Z+N$$

Mass number equals proton plus neutron number.

$$Q_{\mathrm{before}}=Q_{\mathrm{after}}$$

Charge conservation in particle reactions.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Balance nucleon number, charge and lepton number.
2. Include the electron antineutrino in beta-minus decay and electron neutrino in beta-plus decay.
3. Balance charge, baryon number and lepton number in particle equations.
4. For nuclear notation, conserve total nucleon number and proton number.
5. Use antiparticles with equal mass and opposite charge and relevant quantum numbers.

## Common mistakes

- A beta particle is created in the weak interaction; it was not already inside the nucleus.
- Atomic number \(Z\) counts protons; mass number \(A\) counts nucleons, not atomic mass in kilograms.
