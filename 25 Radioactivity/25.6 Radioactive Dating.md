## Core notes

### Carbon Dating

*Source slide 3.*

- Carbon dating, or radiocarbon dating, is a technique which allows us to determine how much time has passed since a living organism ceased respiring.
- Carbon dating is achieved though analysing the ratio of carbon-12 to carbon-14.
- Carbon-14 is unstable and will decay over time.
- While the amount of carbon-12 is constant.

### Carbon Dating – How it works

*Source slide 5.*

- The ratio between carbon-12 and carbon-14 helps us calculate the age of the object.
- When using the equations for radioactivity we should use seconds.
- This is on the OCR data sheet.

### Creation of Carbon-14

*Source slide 7.*

- When cosmic rays enter the atmosphere, they undergo various transformations, including the production of neutrons.
- Carbon-14 is produced when thermal neutrons are absorbed by nitrogen atoms.

### Carbon-14 Decays

*Source slide 8.*

- Carbon-14 will decay into nitrogen-14.

### Dating Rocks

*Source slide 10.*

- You cannot use carbon dating to date rocks because the [[Glossary#Half-Life|half-life]] of carbon-14 is not long enough.
- Instead, geologists use other radioactive substances with longer half-lives.
- Geologists use rubidium-87 which has a half-life of 49 billion years.

### Calibration issues with carbon dating

*Source slide 11.*

- The amount of carbon-14 in the atmosphere has not been constant.
- The amount of carbon-14 has fluctuated greatly.
- This variation is caused by both natural processes and human activity.
- Cosmic rays and changes in Earth’s climate can cause irregularities in the amount of carbon-14 in the atmosphere.
- Humans began making an impact during the Industrial Revolution.

## Equations

### Radioactive decay

$$A=\lambda N$$

Activity.

$$\frac{\Delta N}{\Delta t}=-\lambda N$$

Decay rate.

$$N=N_0e^{-\lambda t},\qquad A=A_0e^{-\lambda t}$$

Exponential decay.

$$\lambda t_{1/2}=\ln 2$$

Half-life relationship.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Subtract background count rate before analysing source data.
2. Use repeated half-lives for simple factors of two; otherwise use the exponential equation.
3. A plot of \(\ln A\) or \(\ln N\) against \(t\) has gradient \(-\lambda\).

## Common mistakes

- Decay is random for one nucleus but statistically predictable for a large sample.
- Activity is measured in becquerels; count rate depends on detector efficiency and geometry.
