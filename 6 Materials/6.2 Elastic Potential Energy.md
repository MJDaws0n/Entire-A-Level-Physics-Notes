## Core notes

### Recoverable Work Done and Springs

*Source slide 3.*

- It requires work to compress or extend a spring.
- This work is recoverable if the spring is not extended beyond its elastic limit.
- If you stretch a spring beyond its elastic limit then all the [[Glossary#Energy|energy]] is not recoverable.
- The energy which is not recoverable has been transferred to the material to permanently change the relative positions of the atoms.

### Work Done and Area

*Source slide 4.*

- If we look at a [[Glossary#Force|force]]-extension graph we can see that the area under the curve is the [[Glossary#Work Done|work done]].
- The elastic potential energy stored by the spring is the area under the curve.

### Deriving Elastic Potential Energy

*Source slide 5.*

- We know that the are under the curve is elastic potential energy.
- So doubling the extension will quadruple the energy stored in the spring.

## Equations

### Springs and elastic energy

$$F=kx$$

Hooke's law within the limit of proportionality.

$$E_{\mathrm{elastic}}=\frac{1}{2}Fx=\frac{1}{2}kx^2$$

Area under a linear force-extension graph.

$$k_{\mathrm{parallel}}=k_1+k_2+\cdots$$

Parallel springs have the same extension and their forces add.

$$\frac{1}{k_{\mathrm{series}}}=\frac{1}{k_1}+\frac{1}{k_2}+\cdots$$

Series springs carry the same force and their extensions add.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Use extension, not total length.
2. Find \(k\) from the gradient of a force-extension graph in the linear region.
3. For a non-linear graph, elastic energy is the area under the curve.

## Common mistakes

- The limit of proportionality and elastic limit are related but not identical.
