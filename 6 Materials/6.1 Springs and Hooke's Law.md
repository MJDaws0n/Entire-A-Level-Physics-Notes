## Core notes

### Terminology

*Source slide 3.*

- Forces that cause objects to be compressed are called compressive forces.
- Forces that produce and extension are called tensile forces.
- Deformation is the change of shape of an object.

### Springs

*Source slide 4.*

- Springs can undergo compressive deformation and tensile deformation.

### Analysing Hooke’s Law Data

*Source slide 5.*

- If a [[Glossary#Force|force]] is applied above the elastic limit then the spring will have been permanently structurally changed.
- It will never return to its initial length.

### Hooke’s Law

*Source slide 6.*

- Hooke’s Law only applies to the region where the spring is undergoing [[Glossary#Elastic Deformation|elastic deformation]].
- From this we can also see that the spring constant is the gradient of a force-extension graph shown on the previous slide.
- The spring constant is a measure of the stiffness of the spring.
- The larger k the more force is required for a given extension.

### Hooke’s Law and Errors

*Source slide 10.*

- You need to make sure that you use a set square.
- Also, you need to make sure your eyes are level with the ruler to reduce parallax effects.

### Adding Spring Constants

*Source slide 11.*

- We can sum springs in parallel.

### Summing Spring Constants

*Source slide 12.*

- The total spring constant, kT, is always lower than the smallest spring constant value.

## Equations

### Springs and elastic energy

$$F=kx$$

Hooke's law within the limit of proportionality.

$$E_{\mathrm{elastic}}=\frac{1}{2}Fx=\frac{1}{2}kx^2$$

Area under a linear force-extension graph.

$$k_{\mathrm{parallel}}=k_1+k_2+\cdots$$

Parallel springs have the same extension and their forces add.

$$\frac{1}{k_{\mathrm{series}}}=\frac{1}{k_1}+\frac{1}{k_2}+\cdots$$

Series springs carry the same force and their extensions add.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Use extension, not total length.
2. Find \(k\) from the gradient of a force-extension graph in the linear region.
3. For a non-linear graph, elastic energy is the area under the curve.

## Common mistakes

- The limit of proportionality and elastic limit are related but not identical.
