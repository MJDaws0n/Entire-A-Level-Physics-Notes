### The Nature of Light

*Source slide 3.*

- The wave nature of light was truly supreme at the start of the 20th century.
- This wave model of light would continue until someone was prepared to make the conclusions from an experiments that was known for decades to their logical end.
- This would radically changed our perception of light and award the young person a Nobel prize.
- The nature of light had long been debated.
- In the 1600’s, Newton believed it was corpuscular (particle) in nature and used this model to explain colour .

### Photons & the Photoelectric Effect

*Source slide 6.*

- If the incident radiation is above the threshold [[Glossary#Frequency|frequency]] increasing the [[Glossary#Intensity|intensity]] doesn’t increase the maximum [[Glossary#Kinetic Energy|kinetic energy]] of the photoelectrons only more electrons are emitted.
- Increasing the frequency of the photons increased the kinetic [[Glossary#Energy|energy]] of the photoelectrons.
- Note: Photoelectrons are just electrons that have been liberated with the use of a photon.
- Photoelectrons are only emitted if the incident radiation is above a certain frequency.
- This is called the threshold frequency for each metal.

### Einstein’s Conclusions

*Source slide 8.*

- The observations can be explained if you switch from a wave model to a particle model for electromagnetic radiation.
- He suggested that each electron needed a specific amount of energy to escape the metal.
- So if the frequency of each photon was below the [[Glossary#Work Function|work function]] then the electron couldn’t escape.
- If the frequency of each photon was below the work function the intensity, number of photons, wouldn’t be able to release a photoelectron.
- There was no time delay as there are only one to one interactions between the electrons and photons.

### The Gold Leaf Electroscope

*Source slide 9.*

- We can negatively charge the metal plate and the gold leaf showing that like charges repel.
- Yet, over time the gold leaf and the metal plate come back together.
- UV light from the surroundings knocks off the additional electrons producing photoelectrons.

## Equations

### The photoelectric effect

$$hf=\phi+E_{k,\max}$$

Einstein's photoelectric equation.

$$E_{k,\max}=eV_s=\frac{1}{2}mv_{\max}^2$$

Maximum kinetic energy and stopping potential.

$$f_0=\frac{\phi}{h}$$

Threshold frequency.

### Photons and wave-particle duality

$$E=hf=\frac{hc}{\lambda}$$

Photon energy.

$$p=\frac{h}{\lambda}$$

Photon momentum and de Broglie relationship.

$$\lambda=\frac{h}{p}$$

de Broglie wavelength of a particle.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Compare photon energy \(hf\) with work function \(\phi\).
2. Use the excess energy as the maximum electron kinetic energy.
3. Increasing intensity above threshold increases emission rate, not maximum kinetic energy.
4. Convert wavelength to metres before using \(hc/\lambda\).
5. For a massive non-relativistic particle, find momentum from \(p=mv\).

## Common mistakes

- Below threshold frequency, no electrons are emitted however intense the light is.
- Intensity changes the number of photons per second; frequency changes the energy of each photon.
