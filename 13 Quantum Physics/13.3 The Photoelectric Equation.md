## Core notes

### 13.3 The Photoelectric Equation

*Source slide 1.*

- Photons incident on metal with a [[Glossary#Frequency|frequency]] below the [[Glossary#Work Function|work function]].
- Photons incident on metal with a frequency equal to the work function.
- Photons incident on metal with a frequency equal to the work function and the [[Glossary#Intensity|intensity]] is increased.
- Photoelectrons emitted with zero [[Glossary#Kinetic Energy|kinetic energy]].
- Additional Photoelectrons emitted with zero kinetic [[Glossary#Energy|energy]].

### Conservation of Energy

*Source slide 3.*

- Einstein realised that the energy of photons and the [[Glossary#Work Done|work done]] on the electrons needed to be conserved.
- f – frequency of the photon (Hz)
- KE – kinetic energy of the electron (J)

### Work Function and Kinetic Energy

*Source slides 4–9.*

- We can imagine the work function as a potential energy hill with the electron placed at the bottom.
- Electrons in the metal can absorb photons with different energies.
- Different metals can have different work functions
- Therefore, some metals will emit electrons while others will not.
- The kinetic energy of the emitted electrons will differ from metal to metal.
- This is equation tells us the maximum kinetic energy a photoelectron may have.
- But not all electrons are at the surface of the metal and so require more work to remove them.

### Analysing Graphs

*Source slides 10–11.*

- When we increase the frequency of the individual photons then we increase the kinetic energy of the photoelectrons.
- There must be a minimum frequency, fo, of the photon to produce a photoelectron with zero kinetic energy.
- This is called the threshold frequency.
- We can determine the threshold frequency the work function and Plank’s constant from graphical analysis.
- Energy (maximum kinetic energy by an emitted electron)

## Equations

### The photoelectric effect

$$hf=\phi+E_{k,\max}$$

Einstein's photoelectric equation.

$$E_{k,\max}=eV_s=\frac{1}{2}mv_{\max}^2$$

Maximum kinetic energy and stopping potential.

$$f_0=\frac{\phi}{h}$$

Threshold frequency.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Compare photon energy \(hf\) with work function \(\phi\).
2. Use the excess energy as the maximum electron kinetic energy.
3. Increasing intensity above threshold increases emission rate, not maximum kinetic energy.

## Common mistakes

- Below threshold frequency, no electrons are emitted however intense the light is.
