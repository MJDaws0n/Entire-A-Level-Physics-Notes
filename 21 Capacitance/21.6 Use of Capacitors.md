### Capacitors

*Source slide 7.*

- Capacitors are very useful devices because they store [[Glossary#Energy|energy]].
- Unlike chemical cells, capacitors can release the energy they store in a very short amount of time.
- This is particularly useful when constructing camera flashes
- and particle accelerators like the one situation at Fermilab, Illinois.

### A.C. to D.C

*Source slide 18.*

- Alternating current (AC) is supplied from the National Grid to homes and industrial units.
- The current rapidly changes due to the supply potential difference cycles from positive to negative.
- A simple rectifier circuit can change an alternating p.d. to a smooth p.d.

## Equations

### Capacitance

$$C=\frac{Q}{V}$$

Capacitance.

$$C=\frac{\varepsilon_0A}{d}$$

Parallel plates in vacuum or air, neglecting edge effects.

$$C=4\pi\varepsilon_0R$$

Capacitance of an isolated conducting sphere of radius \(R\).

### Energy stored by a capacitor

$$W=\frac{1}{2}QV=\frac{Q^2}{2C}=\frac{1}{2}CV^2$$

Energy stored.

### Capacitor charging and discharging

$$\tau=CR$$

Time constant.

$$x=x_0e^{-t/CR}$$

Discharge of \(Q,V\), or \(I\) magnitude.

$$x=x_0\left(1-e^{-t/CR}\right)$$

Charging rise for \(Q\) or capacitor voltage.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Use the charge magnitude stored on either plate, not the sum of magnitudes on both plates.
2. Read capacitance from the gradient of a \(Q\)-against-\(V\) graph.
3. Choose the equivalent energy form containing the known quantities.
4. Energy is the area under a \(V\)-against-\(Q\) graph.
5. For exponential graphs, one time constant leaves \(e^{-1}\approx0.37\) of a decaying quantity.
6. Linearise discharge data using \(\ln x=\ln x_0-t/CR\).
7. State which quantity \(x\) represents; charging current decreases rather than rises.

## Common mistakes

- Capacitance depends on geometry and dielectric, not on the present values of \(Q\) and \(V\) for an ideal linear capacitor.
- The factor of one half is essential because capacitor voltage rises from zero during charging.
- A capacitor voltage cannot change instantaneously in an ideal lumped circuit.
