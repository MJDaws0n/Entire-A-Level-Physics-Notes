## Core notes

### Work Done

*Source slide 3.*

- The amount of [[Glossary#Energy|energy]] stored by a capacitor depends upon the initial [[Glossary#Potential Difference|potential difference]] and [[Glossary#Capacitance|capacitance]].
- Work is done to move the charges.
- Electrons will be attracted to the positive plate and repulsed from the negative plate.
- Therefore, it requires more work the more charge is present on the plates.

### Storing Energy

*Source slide 4.*

- The external work to overcome the electrostatic repulsion and to charge the capacitor is provided by the battery or [[Glossary#Power|power]] supply connected to the capacitor.
- The energy stored in the capacitor is supplied from the battery or power supply.

## Equations

### Capacitance

$$C=\frac{Q}{V}$$

Capacitance.

$$C=\frac{\varepsilon_0A}{d}$$

Parallel plates in vacuum or air, neglecting edge effects.

$$C=4\pi\varepsilon_0R$$

Capacitance of an isolated conducting sphere of radius \(R\).

### Energy stored by a capacitor

$$W=\frac{1}{2}QV=\frac{Q^2}{2C}=\frac{1}{2}CV^2$$

Energy stored.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Use the charge magnitude stored on either plate, not the sum of magnitudes on both plates.
2. Read capacitance from the gradient of a \(Q\)-against-\(V\) graph.
3. Choose the equivalent energy form containing the known quantities.
4. Energy is the area under a \(V\)-against-\(Q\) graph.

## Common mistakes

- Capacitance depends on geometry and dielectric, not on the present values of \(Q\) and \(V\) for an ideal linear capacitor.
- The factor of one half is essential because capacitor voltage rises from zero during charging.
