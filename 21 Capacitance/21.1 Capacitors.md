### Uses of Capacitors

*Source slide 11.*

- Capacitors are electrical components in which charge is separated.
- Capacitors are used in capacitive touchscreens.
- In these touch screen a layer of material momentarily stores electrical charge at a point.
- This allows the device to understand where your finger is placed.

### Construction of a Capacitor

*Source slide 12.*

- Capacitors are simply two metal plates
- This insulator could be air, paper, ceramic or mica.
- The insulator is often called a dielectric material.

### Separating Charge

*Source slides 14–15.*

- We start with a capacitor, conductors, and a cell with an emf (Lesson 9.2).
- There is not a complete circuit as there is an insulator in between the plates of the capacitor.
- There is equal charge around the circuit.
- Yet, when the cell is initially added to the circuit, current flows for a brief period of time.
- Current flows until the [[Glossary#Potential Difference|potential difference]] across the plates of the capacitor equals the emf of the cell.
- This leads to one of the plates becoming negatively charged while the other, with a deficit of electrons, becomes positively charged.

## Equations

### Capacitance

$$C=\frac{Q}{V}$$

Capacitance.

$$C=\frac{\varepsilon_0A}{d}$$

Parallel plates in vacuum or air, neglecting edge effects.

$$C=4\pi\varepsilon_0R$$

Capacitance of an isolated conducting sphere of radius \(R\).

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Use the charge magnitude stored on either plate, not the sum of magnitudes on both plates.
2. Read capacitance from the gradient of a \(Q\)-against-\(V\) graph.

## Common mistakes

- Capacitance depends on geometry and dielectric, not on the present values of \(Q\) and \(V\) for an ideal linear capacitor.
