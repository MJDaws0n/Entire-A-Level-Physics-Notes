## Core notes

### Supercapacitors

*Source slide 3.*

- Supercapacitors are capacitors with a value of [[Glossary#Capacitance|capacitance]] which can reach into the thousands of farads (F).
- These capacitors can be used as alternatives to battery packs in memory backup devices and emergency lighting systems.
- Capacitors have an advantage over rechargeable batteries as they do not degrade over time like batteries do.

## Equations

### Capacitance

$$C=\frac{Q}{V}$$

Capacitance.

$$C=\frac{\varepsilon_0A}{d}$$

Parallel plates in vacuum or air, neglecting edge effects.

$$C=4\pi\varepsilon_0R$$

Capacitance of an isolated conducting sphere of radius \(R\).

### Capacitors in series and parallel

$$C_{\mathrm{parallel}}=C_1+C_2+\cdots$$

Capacitors in parallel.

$$\frac{1}{C_{\mathrm{series}}}=\frac{1}{C_1}+\frac{1}{C_2}+\cdots$$

Capacitors in series.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Use the charge magnitude stored on either plate, not the sum of magnitudes on both plates.
2. Read capacitance from the gradient of a \(Q\)-against-\(V\) graph.
3. In series, each capacitor has equal charge magnitude and the voltages add.
4. In parallel, each capacitor has the same potential difference and the charges add.

## Common mistakes

- Capacitance depends on geometry and dielectric, not on the present values of \(Q\) and \(V\) for an ideal linear capacitor.
- Capacitor combination rules are the reverse pattern of resistor rules.
