## Core notes

### Work Done

*Source slide 3.*

- Hint use equation for gravitational [[Glossary#Energy|energy]]
- Energy is defined as the capacity to do work.

### Work Done and Direction

*Source slide 4.*

- Work is done when a [[Glossary#Force|force]] is applied to an object and that object moves.
- If there is no movement there is no [[Glossary#Work Done|work done]].
- Work is done when you are overcoming an other force.
- For example, the force of friction.

### Work Done at an Angle

*Source slide 10.*

- To determine the work done when the force is applied at an angle we need to use trigonometry.
- A box is pulled at an angle and travels in the horizontal direction.

## Equations

### Work and mechanical energy

$$W=Fx\cos\theta$$

Work done by a constant force; \(\theta\) is between force and displacement.

$$E_k=\frac{1}{2}mv^2$$

Kinetic energy.

$$\Delta E_p=mg\Delta h$$

Change in gravitational potential energy near Earth's surface.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Identify the initial and final energy stores.
2. Include work done against resistive forces as energy transferred to thermal stores.
3. Equate energy changes only after accounting for every transfer.

## Common mistakes

- Use vertical height change in \(mg\Delta h\), not distance along a slope.
