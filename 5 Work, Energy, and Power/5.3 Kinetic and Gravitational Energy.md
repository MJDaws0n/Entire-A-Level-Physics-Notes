## Core notes

### 5.3 Kinetic and Gravitational Energy

*Source slide 1.*

- [[Glossary#Conservation Of Energy|Conservation of energy]] states that the total energy of a closed system remains constant.
- [[Glossary#Energy|Energy]] cannot be created or destroyed, but it can be transferred from one form to another.

### Gravitational Potential Energy

*Source slide 8.*

- [[Glossary#Gravitational Potential|Gravitational potential]] energy (GPE) is the capacity for doing work as a results of an object’s position in a gravitational field, g.
- You can calculate the change in GPE in a uniformed gravitational field from the objects mass and the change in height.
- Ep: Gravitational Potential Energy – J
- g: [[Glossary#Gravitational Field Strength|Gravitational field strength]] – N kg-1

### Work Done: Gaining and Losing GPE

*Source slide 9.*

- You need to do work to get an object to increase its height.
- Therefore when the object gets higher it gains GPE.
- As the object falls through a gravitational field it loses GPE.

### Energy Exchange

*Source slides 11–12.*

- There are many situations where we have an energy exchange between [[Glossary#Kinetic Energy|kinetic energy]] to gravitational potential energy and vice versa.
- This is only valid when we ignore resistive forces ([[Glossary#Drag|drag]] and heating).

## Equations

### Work and mechanical energy

$$W=Fx\cos\theta$$

Work done by a constant force; \(\theta\) is between force and displacement.

$$E_k=\frac{1}{2}mv^2$$

Kinetic energy.

$$\Delta E_p=mg\Delta h$$

Change in gravitational potential energy near Earth's surface.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Identify the initial and final energy stores.
2. Include work done against resistive forces as energy transferred to thermal stores.
3. Equate energy changes only after accounting for every transfer.

## Common mistakes

- Use vertical height change in \(mg\Delta h\), not distance along a slope.
