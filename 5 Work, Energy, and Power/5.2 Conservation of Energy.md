## Core notes

### 5.2 Conservation of Energy

*Source slide 1.*

- A box is pulled at an angle of 30 degrees to the horizontal over a rough plane with a [[Glossary#Force|force]] of 25 N.
- The box travels in the horizontal direction a distance of 10 m.

### Energy and Conservation

*Source slide 3.*

- [[Glossary#Energy|Energy]] is the capacity to do work.
- Energy is a [[Glossary#Scalar|scalar]] quantity with the SI unit of a Joule (J), the same unit as [[Glossary#Work Done|work done]].
- [[Glossary#Conservation Of Energy|Conservation of energy]] states that the total energy of a closed system remains constant.
- Energy cannot be created or destroyed, but it can be transferred from one form to another.

### Sankey Diagrams

*Source slide 8.*

- Sankey diagrams are a way of pictorially representing transfers in energy.

## Equations

### Work and mechanical energy

$$W=Fx\cos\theta$$

Work done by a constant force; \(\theta\) is between force and displacement.

$$E_k=\frac{1}{2}mv^2$$

Kinetic energy.

$$\Delta E_p=mg\Delta h$$

Change in gravitational potential energy near Earth's surface.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Identify the initial and final energy stores.
2. Include work done against resistive forces as energy transferred to thermal stores.
3. Equate energy changes only after accounting for every transfer.

## Common mistakes

- Use vertical height change in \(mg\Delta h\), not distance along a slope.
