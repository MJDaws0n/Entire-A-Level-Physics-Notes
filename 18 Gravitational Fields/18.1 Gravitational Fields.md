## Core notes

### Gravitational Fields

*Source slide 3.*

- All objects with the property of mass create a gravitational field around themselves.
- Gravitational field lines extend from infinity toward the object's centre of mass.
- Any object with mass that is placed into the gravitational field will experience an attractive [[Glossary#Force|force]] towards the other object’s centre of mass.
- For objects on Earth, we call this gravitational attraction the object’s weight.

### Gravitational Field Lines

*Source slide 4.*

- Gravitational field lines are drawn pointing toward and terminating at the source mass.
- They are lines of force which point towards the centre of mass.
- The [[Glossary#Density|density]] of field lines decreases with distance.
- Close to the surface of a planet we presume that they form a uniform field.

### Gravitational Field Strength

*Source slide 5.*

- The strength of the gravitational field, g, at a certain point is defined as the gravitational force exerted per unit mass on a mass object placed at that point within the field.

### Gravitational Field Strength on Earth

*Source slide 6.*

- The [[Glossary#Gravitational Field Strength|gravitational field strength]] on earth varies slightly from place to place.
- At the equator, at sea level, the average gravitational field strength is 9.81 N kg-1.
- Altitude, latitude and local geology all affect the local value of \(g\).

### A final note on terminology

*Source slide 7.*

- We talk about gravitational force, gravitational field strength and gravitational field, nothing else.
- Objects with mass create gravitational fields.
- Gravitational fields have a certain strength at different points.
- This is called the gravitational field strength.

## Equations

### Gravitational fields

$$g=\frac{F}{m}$$

Gravitational field strength: force per unit mass.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Use field lines to show the direction of force on a small test mass.
2. Closer field-line spacing represents a stronger field.

## Common mistakes

- Gravitational potential is energy per unit mass and is scalar; field strength is force per unit mass and is vector.
