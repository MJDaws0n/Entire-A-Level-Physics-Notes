### 18.6 Gravitational Potential

*Source slide 1.*

- Geostationary satellites orbit above the equator.
- The time to orbit is 24 hours.
- The satellites appear to be stationary over a fixed position above Earth.
- These satellites are used for telecommunication and weather monitoring.

### Gravitational Fields

*Source slides 3–4.*

- All masses produce gravitational fields.
- Gravitational fields point towards the centre of mass.
- The magnitude of the gravitational field depends on the size of the mass producing them.
- As you increases distance from the centre of mass the strength of the gravitational field decreases.
- When you travel infinitely far away from the mass the value of the gravitational field is zero.

### Gravitational Potential

*Source slides 6–17.*

- The [[Glossary#Gravitational Potential|gravitational potential]], Vg , at a point in a gravitational field is the [[Glossary#Work Done|work done]] per unit mass to move an object to that point from infinity.
- The units for gravitational potential, Vg , are J kg-1.
- Gravitational potential is a [[Glossary#Scalar|scalar]] quantity – it only has a magnitude.
- At infinity, the gravitational potential is zero.
- The gravitational [[Glossary#Force|force]] is attractive.
- It requires work ([[Glossary#Energy|energy]]) to move objects apart.
- The value of the gravitational potential not at infinity is negative.

## Equations

### Gravitational potential

$$V_g=-\frac{GM}{r}$$

Potential with zero at infinity.

$$\Delta W=m\Delta V_g$$

Work/energy change for a mass moved between potentials.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Use zero potential at infinity and keep the negative sign.
2. Add gravitational potentials from several masses as scalars.

## Common mistakes

- Potential becomes less negative as distance from an isolated mass increases.
