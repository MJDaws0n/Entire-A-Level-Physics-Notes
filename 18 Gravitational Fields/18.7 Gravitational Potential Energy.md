### 18.7 Gravitational Potential Energy

- The [[Glossary#Gravitational Potential|gravitational potential]], Vg , at a point in a gravitational field is the [[Glossary#Work Done|work done]] per unit mass to move an object to that point from infinity.

### Gravitational Potential Energy

- The gravitational potential energy E of an object with mass m within a gravitational field is the work done to move the mass from infinity to that point in the gravitational field.
- We are normally concerned with the change of gravitational potential energy.
- This is because we are moving masses between points in a gravitational field and not from a theoretical infinity.

*Source slide 15.*

### Force-distance

*Source slide 17.*

- As you increase the distance from a mass the gravitational [[Glossary#Force|force]] decreases.
- The relationship between distance and force is an inverse square law.

### Escape Velocity

*Source slides 19–20.*

- To escape the gravitational field of a planet the object must gain enough gravitational potential energy to leave the field.
- The minimum amount of energy to escape the gravitational field of a planet is when the initial [[Glossary#Kinetic Energy|kinetic energy]] is equal to the gravitational potential energy.
- The escape [[Glossary#Velocity|velocity]] does not depend upon the mass of object.
- The escape velocity is the same for all objects irrespective of their mass.

## Equations

### Gravitational potential

$$V_g=-\frac{GM}{r}$$

Potential with zero at infinity.

$$\Delta W=m\Delta V_g$$

Work/energy change for a mass moved between potentials.

### Gravitational potential energy

$$E_p=mV_g=-\frac{GMm}{r}$$

Potential energy with zero at infinity.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Use zero potential at infinity and keep the negative sign.
2. Add gravitational potentials from several masses as scalars.
3. Calculate final minus initial potential energy with signs retained.
4. Use conservation of energy when kinetic and gravitational potential energy are exchanged.

## Common mistakes

- Potential becomes less negative as distance from an isolated mass increases.
- The near-Earth equation \(mg\Delta h\) is an approximation for small height changes in an almost uniform field.
