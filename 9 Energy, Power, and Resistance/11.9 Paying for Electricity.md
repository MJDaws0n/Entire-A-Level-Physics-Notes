### The Cost of Electricity

*Source slide 3.*

- There are 6 large energy providers in the UK and 81% of people buy their electrical energy from one of these providers.
- These companies have been investigated by Ofgen and the Competition and Market Authority (CMA).
- CMA discovered that customers have been paying £1.4 billion a year more than they would have been if the market had been fully competitive.
- This is because companies may price fix and also keep customers on more expensive base tariff deals than cheaper deals that they offer.
- Therefore, it is important for you to understand how to determine the cost of energy.

### Energy Transferred

*Source slide 4.*

- The energy transferred to a device depends upon the [[Glossary#Power|power]] rating of the device used and the time that device is used for.

### Energy and kilowatt-hour

*Source slide 5.*

- The SI unit for energy is the Joule (J).
- Yet as this is a relatively small amount of energy when compared to the energy transferred by household electrical devices.
- Therefore, we define the kilowatt-hour as a unit of energy to deal with this issue.

### Converting from kWh to J

*Source slide 6.*

- You need to be able to convert from kilowatt-hours into J.
- To do this we change the kW into W and the hours into seconds.

### Energy and Price

*Source slide 8.*

- The price charged by the electric companies depends upon the power rating of the device, the time the device is used for and the cost per unit of energy charged by the company.

### The Price of a kilowatt-hour

*Source slide 9.*

- The typical price of a unit of electricity is between 6 – 20 p for each kilowatt-hour.
- Certain companies charge less for each unit of energy at night when demand on the national infrastructure is low.
- This is due to engineering issues we face with efficiently storing electricity and altering the output of power stations to meet demand.

## Method

1. Choose the power form that uses known quantities.
2. For billing, convert power to kilowatts and time to hours.
3. For SI energy in joules, use power in watts and time in seconds.

## Common mistakes

- A kilowatt-hour is a unit of energy, not power.
