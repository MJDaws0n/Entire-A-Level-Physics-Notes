## Core notes

### Definition

*Source slide 4.*

- One ohm is defined as the [[Glossary#Resistance|resistance]] of a component when
- a [[Glossary#Potential Difference|potential difference]] of 1 V is produced

### What is resistance?

*Source slide 6.*

- All electrical components possess a quantity called resistance.
- Each component will resist charge that flow through it.
- It requires [[Glossary#Energy|energy]] to move a charge carrier through a component.
- The higher the resistance the more energy is required.
- We can use a gravitational energy analogy to explain electrical resistance.

### Resistance and Metal Conductors

*Source slide 7.*

- Increasing the temperature increases vibrations in the lattice.
- Increased vibrations leads to more collisions between the positive ions and the charge carries.
- This reduces the mean [[Glossary#Drift Velocity|drift velocity]] of the charge carries.
- This results in a greater resistance.
- Metals comprise of a regular lattice arrangement of positively charged ions.

### Resistance and Graphs

*Source slides 11–12.*

- We can rearrange our equation for ohm’s law to make current, I, the subject.
- We can create a graph of I against V.
- We can see that the gradient is the inverse of the resistance.
- Therefore, there is no y offset and the line will go through the origin (0,0).
- Components that have this relationship are called ohmic device.
- As we increases the temperature of a metallic conductor the resistance increases.
- As the resistance increases the gradient will decrease.

### Express an ohm in base SI units.

*Source slide 17.*

- The volt is not a base SI unit.
- The joule is not a base SI unit.
- The coulomb is not a base SI unit.

## Equations

### Resistance and resistivity

$$R=\frac{V}{I}$$

Resistance at a particular operating point.

$$R=\frac{\rho L}{A}$$

Resistance of a uniform conductor.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. For an ohmic conductor, use the gradient of a \(V\)-against-\(I\) graph.
2. In resistivity work, measure \(L\) and diameter carefully and use \(A=\pi d^2/4\).

## Common mistakes

- The reciprocal gradient is required if the graph plots \(I\) against \(V\).
- Resistivity is a material property; resistance also depends on dimensions.
