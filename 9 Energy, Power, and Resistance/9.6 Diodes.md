### The Diode

*Source slide 3.*

- We see light emitting diodes (LEDs) more and more in society.
- In recent years (2010’s) car lights have been changing from the old filament lamps to LEDs.
- The reason for this is that LEDs are more efficient as they do not get nearly as hot as their counterparts.
- They can transfer electrical [[Glossary#Energy|energy]] into light with less energy being ‘lost’.

### Diodes

*Source slide 4.*

- Diodes are ubiquitous in electrical devices.
- They are used in electrical circuits to regulate the direction of current.

### Polarity and Current

*Source slide 5.*

- No current can flow
- Change direction of diode.
- Change the polarity of the cell.

### Diodes Key Points

*Source slide 6.*

- Diodes are made of a semiconductor material.
- They only allow current to flow in one direction.
- Some diodes emit light when they conduct.
- These are called light emitting diodes (LEDs).

### Diode I-V Characteristic Curve

*Source slide 7.*

- At point C the diode has very little resistance.
- At point B the resistance begins to drop and the diode begins to conduct.
- This is known as the threshold p.d.
- This is approximately around 0.7 V.
- At point A the resistance is very high – practically infinite for practical purposes – and therefore does not conduct.

## Method

1. Identify the component and note whether its characteristic is linear and symmetric.
2. For a filament lamp, increasing temperature raises resistance.
3. A diode conducts strongly only in its forward direction after its characteristic begins to rise.

## Common mistakes

- A curved \(I\)-\(V\) graph does not have one constant resistance.
