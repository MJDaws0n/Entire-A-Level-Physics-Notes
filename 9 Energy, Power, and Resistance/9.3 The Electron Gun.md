## Core notes

### 9.3 The Electron Gun

*Source slide 1.*

- [[Glossary#Electromotive Force|Electromotive force]] is a term for when the charge carriers gain [[Glossary#Energy|energy]] while [[Glossary#Potential Difference|potential difference]] is when the charge carriers do work (losing energy).
- Both are measured with Voltmeters and have units volts (V).

### J.J. Thomson

*Source slide 3.*

- The electron gun (cathode ray tube) was used by JJ Thomson in 1897 for the discovery of the electron.
- This discovery was pivotal in our understanding of the universe.
- No longer were atoms solid indestructible spheres they were composite particles made of subatomic particles.
- Thomson was awarded the Nobel Prize for physics in 1906 for this work.

### The Electron Gun

*Source slides 4–5.*

- An electron gun is a device used to produce a narrow beam of electrons with precise kinetic energies.
- Electron guns are used as scientific instruments, such as, electron microscopes, spectrometers, and oscilloscopes.
- They were also previously used in old T.V sets and computer monitors – called CRT monitors.

### How does the electron gun work?

*Source slides 6–7.*

- Some electrons gain enough kinetic energy to escape the surface of the metal.
- The source of electrons is a small metal filament.
- The electrons can escape, forming a narrow beam, through a hole in the anode.
- Heating the filament gives the electrons energy.
- The electrons then enter a vacuum with a high p.d. applied between the filament and the anode.

## Equations

### Accelerating charged particles

$$\Delta E_k=qV$$

Kinetic-energy gain across a potential difference.

$$\frac{1}{2}mv^2=qV$$

Non-relativistic speed after acceleration from rest.

$$1\,\mathrm{eV}=1.60\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J}$$

Electronvolt conversion.

### Potential difference and electromotive force

$$V=\frac{W}{Q}$$

Potential difference: energy transferred per unit charge.

$$\mathcal{E}=\frac{W_{\mathrm{source}}}{Q}$$

EMF: energy supplied per unit charge.

$$W=VQ=VIt$$

Electrical energy transferred.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Use the magnitude of charge when finding the energy gained.
2. Convert electronvolts to joules before using SI mechanical equations.
3. Identify whether a component supplies energy or transfers it away from the charges.
4. Use joules per coulomb to interpret both volts and EMF.

## Common mistakes

- The electronvolt is a unit of energy, not potential difference.
- EMF is measured in volts; despite its name, it is not a force.
