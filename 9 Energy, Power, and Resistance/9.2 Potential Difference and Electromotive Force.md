## Core notes

### Nikola Tesla and the Coil

*Source slide 3.*

- He also invented a device called the Tesla coil which uses extremely large potential differences (sometimes called voltage) to produce a spectacular sight!

### Potential Difference and the Volt

*Source slide 4.*

- [[Glossary#Potential Difference|Potential difference]] (pd) is measured in volts (V).
- The unit, volt, has the namesake after Alessandro Volta who invented the first battery.

### The Voltmeter

*Source slide 5.*

- A voltmeter is used to measure the potential difference between two points in a circuit.
- Therefore, they are connected in parallel to the circuit.
- An ideal voltmeter would have an infinite [[Glossary#Resistance|resistance]] to ensure that no current passes through the voltmeter itself.

### Pd and Electromotive Force

*Source slide 6.*

- [[Glossary#Electromotive Force|Electromotive force]] is also measured in volts (V) with a voltmeter.
- Potential difference (pd) is when the charge carrier loses [[Glossary#Energy|energy]].
- Electromotive [[Glossary#Force|force]] (emf) is when the charge carrier gains energy.
- Charge carriers gain energy from cells, batteries, and [[Glossary#Power|power]] sources.
- They can also gain energy from solar cells, dynamos, and thermocouples.

## Equations

### Potential difference and electromotive force

$$V=\frac{W}{Q}$$

Potential difference: energy transferred per unit charge.

$$\mathcal{E}=\frac{W_{\mathrm{source}}}{Q}$$

EMF: energy supplied per unit charge.

$$W=VQ=VIt$$

Electrical energy transferred.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Identify whether a component supplies energy or transfers it away from the charges.
2. Use joules per coulomb to interpret both volts and EMF.

## Common mistakes

- EMF is measured in volts; despite its name, it is not a force.
