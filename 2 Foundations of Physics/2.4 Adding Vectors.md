## Core notes

### Chasing Cats

*Source slide 3.*

- A dog finds itself in a river chasing a cat.
- The dog swims directly towards the cat.
- Yet the dog misses the bank and drowns in the river.
- Dogs cannot do [[Glossary#Vector|vector]] addition.
- In this lesson you will become more accomplished at physics than ‘man’s best friend’.

### Vectors and Arrows

*Source slide 4.*

- Vectors can be represented by arrows.
- The length represents the magnitude of the vector.
- The direction represents the direction of the vector.
- Vectors can be parallel.
- Vectors can be anti-parallel.

### Resultant Vector

*Source slide 5.*

- The resultant vector is the result of adding together two or more vectors.

### Vectors in 2 dimensions

*Source slide 11.*

- You can find the resultant with scaled drawings or by using Pythagoras’ theorem.

### Individual Book Work

*Source slides 12–19.*

- Add multiple vectors in 2 dimensions.
- Vector right 5 N, left 6 N, left 2 N add right 3 N.
- Vector up 3 N add vector right 4 N.
- Vector up 3 N add vector left 4 N.
- Vector down 5 N add right 3 N.
- Vector right 5, up 6, left 2 and then down 2.

### Direction of Vectors

*Source slide 18.*

- Vectors have a magnitude and direction.
- We can describe the direction of the resultant vector with an angle.

## Equations

### Vectors and components

$$F_x=F\cos\theta$$

Component adjacent to the stated angle.

$$F_y=F\sin\theta$$

Component opposite the stated angle.

$$R=\sqrt{R_x^2+R_y^2}$$

Magnitude from perpendicular components.

$$\theta=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{R_y}{R_x}\right)$$

Direction from perpendicular components; check the quadrant.

### Vector geometry

$$a^2=b^2+c^2$$

Pythagoras for a right-angled vector triangle.

$$a^2=b^2+c^2-2bc\cos A$$

Cosine rule.

$$\frac{a}{\sin A}=\frac{b}{\sin B}=\frac{c}{\sin C}$$

Sine rule.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Choose positive directions and draw a labelled sketch.
2. Resolve every vector along the same pair of perpendicular axes.
3. Add components with signs, then find the magnitude and direction of the resultant.
4. Draw vectors head-to-tail and label every side and angle.
5. Use Pythagoras for perpendicular vectors; otherwise use the sine or cosine rule.
6. Give the direction relative to a named axis or compass direction.

## Common mistakes

- A negative component means the vector points opposite to the chosen positive direction.
- State directions with the final magnitude; a magnitude alone is not a complete vector answer.
- The internal angle in the triangle may be \(180^{\circ}\) minus the angle shown between forces.
