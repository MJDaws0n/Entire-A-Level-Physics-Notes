*Source slide 17.*

### Prefixes

*Source slide 20.*

- SI uses prefixes where pre means before.
- For example, km stands for kilometre where the prefix is kilo.
- You need to be able to convert between a prefixed unit to base SI.
- You need to learn all these prefixes.

## Equations

### SI units, prefixes and conversions

$$Q_{\mathrm{base}} = Q_{\mathrm{prefixed}}\times 10^n$$

Replace the prefix by its power of ten.

$$T\,(\mathrm{K}) = \theta\,({}^{\circ}\mathrm{C}) + 273.15$$

Convert Celsius temperature to kelvin.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Write the prefix as a power of ten before doing the calculation.
2. Convert every value to coherent SI units, then substitute.
3. Carry extra calculator digits and round only the final answer.

## Common mistakes

- A capital letter can change a prefix: \(\mathrm{m}\) is milli but \(\mathrm{M}\) is mega.
- For squared or cubed units, the conversion factor must also be squared or cubed.

> [!note]- Slide 16: A Problem of Units
> - As you may be aware there are different units that represent the same quantities.
> - For example, for length we have cm, m, km, inches, feet, and miles.
> - It is important that scientists have a common language and understand which units are using or it could be disastrous.
> - In 1999 NASAs £195 million Mars Climate Orbiter burnt up in Mars’ upper atmosphere.
> - A piece of the orbiter was made by Lockheed Martin and they used imperial units not SI!
