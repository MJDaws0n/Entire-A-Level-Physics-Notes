## Equations

### Distance and speed

$$\text{speed}=\frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}$$

Scalar rate of change of distance.

### Displacement and velocity

$$\bar v=\frac{\Delta s}{\Delta t}$$

Average velocity from displacement.

### Acceleration

$$a=\frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$$

Acceleration.

### Motion graphs

$$\text{gradient of }s\text{-}t=v$$

Velocity is the displacement-time gradient.

$$\text{gradient of }v\text{-}t=a$$

Acceleration is the velocity-time gradient.

$$\Delta s=\int v\,dt$$

Displacement is signed area under a velocity-time graph.

### Uniform acceleration equations

$$v=u+at$$

Final velocity after time \(t\).

$$s=\frac{1}{2}(u+v)t$$

Displacement from mean velocity.

$$s=ut+\frac{1}{2}at^2$$

Displacement without final velocity.

$$v^2=u^2+2as$$

Motion without time.

## Method

1. Identify the total distance travelled and the corresponding elapsed time.
2. Convert times and distances to consistent units before dividing.
3. For a changing speed, distinguish the average speed over an interval from the instantaneous speed at one moment.
4. Choose and state a positive direction before using signed displacement or velocity.
5. Use displacement, not distance, when calculating average velocity.
6. For instantaneous velocity, find the gradient of a tangent to the displacement-time graph.
7. Choose and state a positive direction before using signed velocities.
8. Calculate the signed velocity change before dividing by the time interval.
9. Use the gradient of a velocity-time graph to find acceleration.
10. For an instantaneous gradient, draw a tangent at the required point.
11. For area, divide the region into simple shapes or count signed squares.
12. Area below the time axis is negative displacement.

## Common mistakes

- Average speed is total distance divided by total time; it is not normally the mean of two speeds.
- Distance is scalar; displacement and velocity include direction.
- Negative acceleration does not always mean slowing down; compare the signs of velocity and acceleration.
- The area under a displacement-time graph is not a standard motion quantity.
- A horizontal line on a velocity-time graph means constant velocity, not rest unless the line is at zero.
- Do not automatically make \(g\) negative; its sign follows the chosen positive direction.
