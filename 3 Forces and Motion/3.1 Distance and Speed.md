## Core notes

### The Speed Equation

*Source slide 5.*

- Speed is a [[Glossary#Scalar|scalar]] quantity.

### Distance Time Graphs

*Source slide 7.*

- These graphs are used to represent the motion of objects.
- Time is on the x axis.
- Distance is placed on the y axis.
- Stationary objects are represented by a horizontal line.
- Objects moving at constant speed are represented by a sloping straight line.

### Determining the gradient

*Source slide 8.*

- The gradient of a line is how steep it is.

### Average and Instantaneous Speed

*Source slide 14.*

- Instantaneous speed is the speed at that instant of time.
- To calculate the instantaneous speed we draw a tangent to the curve at the time we want to calculate the speed then we determine the gradient of the tangent line.
- A tangent is the limiting line through two points that approach one another on a curve.

## Equations

### Distance and speed

$$\text{speed}=\frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}$$

Scalar rate of change of distance.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Identify the total distance travelled and the corresponding elapsed time.
2. Convert times and distances to consistent units before dividing.
3. For a changing speed, distinguish the average speed over an interval from the instantaneous speed at one moment.

## Common mistakes

- Average speed is total distance divided by total time; it is not normally the mean of two speeds.
