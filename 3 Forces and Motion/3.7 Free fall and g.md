## Core notes

### Acceleration Under Free Fall

*Source slides 3–4.*

- Objects with mass exert a [[Glossary#Force|force]] upon other objects with mass.
- This is the gravitational force.
- Kids, chickens, and cats are massive.
- They will apply a force on Earth to their centre.
- The Earth is massive.
- An object accelerating under the gravitational force, with no other forces acting on it, is said to be in free-fall.
- [[Glossary#Acceleration|Acceleration]] due to free-fall is denoted by the letter g.

### Variation in the value of g

*Source slide 5.*

- The value of g varies slightly around the Earth.
- This is due to your longitude, latitude, and the local geology.

### The Electromagnetic Method

*Source slide 9.*

- We have an electromagnet which is holding a metal ball.
- This is placed a known height above a trap door.
- A signal will simultaneously start a timer and release the metal ball from the electromagnet.
- When the ball passes through the trap door a second signal is sent to stop the timer.

### Improving the Trap Door Method

*Source slide 11.*

- The trap door method can introduce minor errors due to the time delay between the ball hitting the trap door and the signal being sent.
- Therefore we could use a light gate instead.
- These devices send a beam of light and when this beam is broken the signal is sent.

### Stroboscope Method

*Source slides 12–20.*

- We can also use a camera (or video camera) to determine g.
- The camera can be set to a long exposure with a strobe light flashing at a set time interval.
- We can see that the distance between the intervals increases as the time increases.
- This is because the ball is accelerating (speeding up) .
- The method works as we measure distance, s.
- We know the time interval, t, of the strobe.
- We can then use our equations for constant acceleration (suvat equations).

## Equations

### Uniform acceleration equations

$$v=u+at$$

Final velocity after time \(t\).

$$s=\frac{1}{2}(u+v)t$$

Displacement from mean velocity.

$$s=ut+\frac{1}{2}at^2$$

Displacement without final velocity.

$$v^2=u^2+2as$$

Motion without time.

### Forces and Newton's laws

$$\sum F=ma$$

Resultant force causes acceleration.

$$W=mg$$

Weight in a gravitational field.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Write down \(u,v,a,s,t\) with signs and units.
2. Select an equation containing the known quantities and the one unknown.
3. These equations apply only while acceleration is constant.
4. Isolate the object and draw only forces acting on it.
5. Choose axes, resolve forces if needed, then write \(\sum F=ma\) along each axis.
6. For equilibrium or constant velocity, set the resultant force to zero.

## Common mistakes

- Do not automatically make \(g\) negative; its sign follows the chosen positive direction.
- Newton's third-law pair acts on different objects, so the two forces do not cancel on one free-body diagram.
- Mass is measured in kilograms; weight is a force measured in newtons.
