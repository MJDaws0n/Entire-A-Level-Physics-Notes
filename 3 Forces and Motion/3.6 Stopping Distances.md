## Core notes

### Stopping Distances

*Source slide 3.*

- Your speed and reaction time affect the thinking distance.

### Factors affecting braking distance

*Source slide 4.*

- The condition of the brakes.
- The conditions of the tyres.
- The condition of the road surface.
- The speed of the car.
- The driver does not affect the braking distance.

### Thinking Distance

*Source slide 5.*

- Thinking distance is affected by your reaction time (the time it takes you from seeing the stimuli to pressing the brake) and your speed.

## Equations

### Distance and speed

$$\text{speed}=\frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}$$

Scalar rate of change of distance.

### Uniform acceleration equations

$$v=u+at$$

Final velocity after time \(t\).

$$s=\frac{1}{2}(u+v)t$$

Displacement from mean velocity.

$$s=ut+\frac{1}{2}at^2$$

Displacement without final velocity.

$$v^2=u^2+2as$$

Motion without time.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Identify the total distance travelled and the corresponding elapsed time.
2. Convert times and distances to consistent units before dividing.
3. For a changing speed, distinguish the average speed over an interval from the instantaneous speed at one moment.
4. Write down \(u,v,a,s,t\) with signs and units.
5. Select an equation containing the known quantities and the one unknown.
6. These equations apply only while acceleration is constant.

## Common mistakes

- Average speed is total distance divided by total time; it is not normally the mean of two speeds.
- Do not automatically make \(g\) negative; its sign follows the chosen positive direction.
