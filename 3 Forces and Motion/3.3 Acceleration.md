## Core notes

### Acceleration

*Source slide 4.*

- [[Glossary#Acceleration|Acceleration]] is a [[Glossary#Vector|vector]] quantity.
- This is because acceleration is the rate of change of a vector quantity, [[Glossary#Velocity|velocity]].

### Velocity-Time Graphs and Acceleration

*Source slide 8.*

- A: Constant velocity – zero gradient – zero acceleration.
- B: Increasing velocity – positive gradient – constant increasing acceleration.
- C: Varying velocity – changing gradient – changing (non-constant) acceleration.
- D: Decreasing velocity – negative gradient – constant decreasing acceleration.

## Equations

### Acceleration

$$a=\frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$$

Acceleration.

### Motion graphs

$$\text{gradient of }s\text{-}t=v$$

Velocity is the displacement-time gradient.

$$\text{gradient of }v\text{-}t=a$$

Acceleration is the velocity-time gradient.

$$\Delta s=\int v\,dt$$

Displacement is signed area under a velocity-time graph.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Choose and state a positive direction before using signed velocities.
2. Calculate the signed velocity change before dividing by the time interval.
3. Use the gradient of a velocity-time graph to find acceleration.
4. For an instantaneous gradient, draw a tangent at the required point.
5. For area, divide the region into simple shapes or count signed squares.
6. Area below the time axis is negative displacement.

## Common mistakes

- Negative acceleration does not always mean slowing down; compare the signs of velocity and acceleration.
- The area under a displacement-time graph is not a standard motion quantity.
- A horizontal line on a velocity-time graph means constant velocity, not rest unless the line is at zero.
