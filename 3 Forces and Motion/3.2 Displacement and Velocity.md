## Core notes

### Distance and Displacement

*Source slide 5.*

- Distance travelled is a [[Glossary#Scalar|scalar]] while [[Glossary#Displacement|displacement]] is a [[Glossary#Vector|vector]] quantity.

### Velocity

*Source slide 6.*

- Average [[Glossary#Velocity|velocity]] can be calculated by using the displacement in a given time.

### Blown from here to there

*Source slides 8–9.*

- A rubber duck is left in a swimming pool where it is blown backwards and forwards.
- Over the period of two days it completes two lengths of the pool.
- i) Determine the duck’s velocity.
- The duck’s velocity is zero.
- The duck ended in its starting position therefore displacement equals zero.
- As velocity is the change in displacement divided by the time taken then velocity must equal zero.

### Displacement-Time Graphs

*Source slide 11.*

- These graphs are used to represent the motion of objects.
- Time is on the x axis.
- Displacement is placed on the y axis.
- Stationary objects are represented by a horizontal line.
- Objects moving at constant velocity are represented by a sloping straight line.

### Determining the gradient

*Source slide 13.*

- The gradient of a line is how steep it is.

## Equations

### Displacement and velocity

$$\bar v=\frac{\Delta s}{\Delta t}$$

Average velocity from displacement.

### Motion graphs

$$\text{gradient of }s\text{-}t=v$$

Velocity is the displacement-time gradient.

$$\text{gradient of }v\text{-}t=a$$

Acceleration is the velocity-time gradient.

$$\Delta s=\int v\,dt$$

Displacement is signed area under a velocity-time graph.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Choose and state a positive direction before using signed displacement or velocity.
2. Use displacement, not distance, when calculating average velocity.
3. For instantaneous velocity, find the gradient of a tangent to the displacement-time graph.
4. For an instantaneous gradient, draw a tangent at the required point.
5. For area, divide the region into simple shapes or count signed squares.
6. Area below the time axis is negative displacement.

## Common mistakes

- Distance is scalar; displacement and velocity include direction.
- The area under a displacement-time graph is not a standard motion quantity.
- A horizontal line on a velocity-time graph means constant velocity, not rest unless the line is at zero.
