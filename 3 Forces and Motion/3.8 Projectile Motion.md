## Core notes

### 3.8 Projectile Motion

*Source slide 1.*

- Data from an experiment where a ball is dropped a known height and the time taken is recorded in a distance vs time2 graph.

### Cannons and Projectiles

*Source slide 3.*

- A cannon fires a projectile, a cannon ball.
- In the past cannons where positioned on clifftops or castle walls to increase their range.
- For A level Physics we presume objects are in free fall, ignore air [[Glossary#Resistance|resistance]], range only depends only upon the height, s, of the cannon and the initial [[Glossary#Velocity|velocity]], u, of the cannon ball when fired.

### Independent Motion

*Source slides 4–7.*

- Let’s take two balls, one dropped vertically while the other is thrown horizontally.
- You will notice that both balls fall at the same rate.
- They are at the same height at the same time.
- It does not matter if a projectile is moving horizontally.
- The projectile’s velocity only changes due to the gravitational [[Glossary#Force|force]] ([[Glossary#Acceleration|acceleration]] of free fall, g).
- The vertical velocity changes due to acceleration of free fall.
- The vertical [[Glossary#Displacement|displacement]] and time of flight can be calculated using the equations of motion

### Firing Upwards

*Source slides 15–16.*

- We will need to solve problems with projectiles being fired into the air.
- To solve these we need to think about the problem physically.
- This will save us much time.
- The vertical velocity component equals zero at the top of the parabola.
- As this is a turning moment.
- We will need to also split the total velocity, v, into its independent velocities, vH and vv.
- These relationship are give in the OCR physics data sheet.

## Equations

### Projectile motion

$$u_x=u\cos\theta,\qquad u_y=u\sin\theta$$

Resolve the launch velocity.

$$a_x=0,\qquad a_y=-g$$

Ignoring air resistance and taking upward as positive.

### Uniform acceleration equations

$$v=u+at$$

Final velocity after time \(t\).

$$s=\frac{1}{2}(u+v)t$$

Displacement from mean velocity.

$$s=ut+\frac{1}{2}at^2$$

Displacement without final velocity.

$$v^2=u^2+2as$$

Motion without time.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Treat horizontal and vertical motion separately but use the same time.
2. Use constant horizontal velocity to find range or time.
3. Use a constant-acceleration equation vertically with the correct sign for \(g\).
4. Write down \(u,v,a,s,t\) with signs and units.
5. Select an equation containing the known quantities and the one unknown.
6. These equations apply only while acceleration is constant.

## Common mistakes

- At the highest point, vertical velocity is zero but horizontal velocity is not.
- Do not automatically make \(g\) negative; its sign follows the chosen positive direction.
