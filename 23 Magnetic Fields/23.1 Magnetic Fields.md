## Core notes

### 23.1 Magnetic Fields

*Source slide 1.*

- Michael Faraday introduced magnetic fields lines as a visual representation of magnetic fields.
- Faraday called magnetic field lines “lines of [[Glossary#Force|force]].”

### Magnets

*Source slide 3.*

- All magnets have a north and south pole.
- Magnets attract ferrous (contains iron) materials.
- Magnets can attract and repel other magnets.

### Magnetic Field Lines

*Source slide 5.*

- All magnets produce magnetic field lines.
- We observe magnetic field lines with iron filings or a compass.

### Rules: Magnetic Field Line

*Source slide 6.*

### Interacting Magnetic Fields

*Source slides 8–9.*

- These two magnets are going to attract towards each other.
- Magnets move in the direction to cause the magnetic field lines to shorten.

- Denser field lines means a greater force.

### The Earth

*Source slide 10.*

- A compass needle points in line with magnetic field lines.
- On Earth, a compass needle points towards magnetic north.
- Above the equator the compass needle dips downwards.
- This tells us the Earth has
- Earth is a magnet.

### Current and Magnetism

*Source slide 11.*

- Current is the net flow of charged particles.
- Current creates a magnetic field.

### Direction of the Magnetic Field

*Source slide 14.*

- The magnetic field around a current carrying conductor can be clockwise or anticlockwise.

### Right Hand Corkscrew Rule

*Source slide 15.*

- We can identify the direction of the magnetic field with the right hand corkscrew rule.
- Raise your right hand in the air.
- Your thumb points in the direction of conventional current.
- Your fingers show the direction of the magnetic field.
- Point your thumb towards your face – anti-clockwise.

### Direction of Magnetic Field

### Medical Imaging

*Source slide 22.*

- Large magnetic fields are used in medical imaging.
- Magnetic [[Glossary#Resonance|resonance]] imaging (MRI) is non-invasive and produces excellent soft tissue contrast without harmful ionizing radiation.

### MRIs and Solenoids

*Source slide 25.*

- We need to produce large magnetic fields to produce an MRI image.
- To do so we need more magnetic field lines.
- A solenoid is a current carrying conductor that loops many times.
- Denser (closer) field lines means a greater force.

## Equations

### Magnetic forces

$$F=BIL\sin\theta$$

Force on a current-carrying conductor.

$$F=BQv\sin\theta$$

Force on a moving charge.

$$r=\frac{mv}{BQ}=\frac{p}{BQ}$$

Radius for perpendicular motion in a uniform magnetic field.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Identify field, conventional current or positive-charge velocity, and force as mutually perpendicular directions.
2. Reverse the force for a negative charge.
3. A magnetic force does no work because it is perpendicular to velocity.

## Common mistakes

- Use the component of current/velocity perpendicular to the field.
