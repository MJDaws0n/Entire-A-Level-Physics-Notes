## Core notes

### 23.5 Faraday’s Law and Lenz’s Law

*Source slide 1.*

- Increase the magnetic field strength.
- Increase the number of coils in the conductor.
- Increase the relative speed.

### Lenz’s Law

*Source slides 5–7.*

- The direction of the induced e.m.f. (or current) always opposes the change producing it.
- Moving a magnet towards a coil of conductor induces an e.m.f.
- When we have a complete circuit and an e.m.f. we have a current in the coil.
- A current produces its own magnetic field.
- The induced field opposes the change producing it.
- Reversing the direction of the relative motion causes the current to change direction and changes the direction of the magnetic field.

### Generators

*Source slide 10.*

- Mains electricity is produced by an alternator.
- An alternator can produce A.C. (alternating current) at a [[Glossary#Frequency|frequency]] of 50 Hz with slip-rings and brushes.

### Faraday’s law

*Source slide 13.*

- Faraday’s law states the induced e.m.f. is proportional to the rate of change in [[Glossary#Magnetic Flux|magnetic flux]] linkage.
- Rate of change means the e.m.f. is related to the gradient of the [[Glossary#Flux Linkage|flux linkage]] with respect to time.

## Equations

### Electromagnetic induction

$$\Phi=BA\cos\theta$$

Magnetic flux; \(\theta\) is between field and area normal.

$$\mathcal{E}=-\frac{\Delta(N\Phi)}{\Delta t}$$

Faraday's and Lenz's laws.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Find the change in flux linkage \(N\Phi\), not just the final flux.
2. Use the minus sign/Lenz's law to state that the induced effect opposes the change causing it.
3. Increase EMF by increasing turns, field, area, or rate of change.

## Common mistakes

- Flux uses the angle to the normal; using the angle to the plane swaps sine and cosine.
