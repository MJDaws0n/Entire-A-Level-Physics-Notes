## Core notes

### Aurora borealis

*Source slide 3.*

- The Sun bombards Earth with charged particles.
- The aurora borealis is created when charged particles from the Sun are accelerated in the Earth’s magnetic field.
- As the magnetic fields strength is stronger at the poles, this is where you are most likely to be able to see this spectacular event.

### Charged Particles with a Magnetic Field

*Source slides 4–12.*

- Charged particles moving in a magnetic field experience a [[Glossary#Force|force]].
- The direction of the force can be predicted with the use of Fleming's left-hand rule.
- Moving charged particles will trace out (describe) a circular path when placed inside a uniformed magnetic field.

### Radius in a Uniformed Magnetic Field

*Source slide 14.*

- More massive particle have a larger radius.
- Faster moving particles have a larger radius.
- Stronger magnetic fields reduce the radius.
- Particles with greater charge have a smaller radius.

### Useful Equations

*Source slide 15.*

- These equations are useful when solving problems with moving charged particles in uniformed magnetic fields.

### Velocity Selector

*Source slide 17.*

- A [[Glossary#Velocity|velocity]] selector is a device that uses electric and magnetic fields to select charged particles of a specific velocity.

### Hall Probe

*Source slide 20.*

- A Hall probe can be used to measure the [[Glossary#Magnetic Flux|magnetic flux]] [[Glossary#Density|density]] between two magnets.
- The flat surface of the probe must be directed between the magnets so the magnetic field lines pass completely perpendicular to this surface.

### Measuring Magnetic Fields

*Source slide 21.*

- The Hall probe is connected to a voltmeter to measure the Hall voltage.
- If the probe is not held in the correct orientation (perpendicular to the field lines), the voltmeter reading will be reduced.
- The Hall voltage is directly proportional to the magnetic flux density so can be obtained.
- A Hall probe is sensitive enough to measure even the Earth’s magnetic flux density.

## Equations

### Magnetic forces

$$F=BIL\sin\theta$$

Force on a current-carrying conductor.

$$F=BQv\sin\theta$$

Force on a moving charge.

$$r=\frac{mv}{BQ}=\frac{p}{BQ}$$

Radius for perpendicular motion in a uniform magnetic field.

### Centripetal acceleration and force

$$a_c=\frac{v^2}{r}=\omega^2r$$

Centripetal acceleration.

$$F_c=\frac{mv^2}{r}=m\omega^2r$$

Resultant inward force.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Identify field, conventional current or positive-charge velocity, and force as mutually perpendicular directions.
2. Reverse the force for a negative charge.
3. A magnetic force does no work because it is perpendicular to velocity.
4. Identify the real forces, then set their inward resultant equal to \(mv^2/r\).
5. Centripetal force is a role played by real forces, not an extra force.

## Common mistakes

- Use the component of current/velocity perpendicular to the field.
- Velocity is tangential while acceleration and resultant force point toward the centre.
