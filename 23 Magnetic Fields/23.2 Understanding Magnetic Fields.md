## Core notes

### Interacting Magnetic Fields

*Source slides 4–5.*

- So far we have seen that permanent magnets produce magnetic fields.
- Also, current carrying conductors produce magnetic fields.
- Outside a magnet, magnetic field lines are drawn from north to south.
- Denser (closer) field lines means a greater [[Glossary#Force|force]]

### Direction of Force

*Source slides 6–7.*

- Reversing the permanent magnet's polarity reverses the force direction.
- When we change the direction of the current the direction of the force changes.

### Determining the Direction

*Source slide 9.*

- We determine the direction of the force on the current carrying conductor by the use of Fleming’s left hand rule.

### Increasing the Magnitude of Force

*Source slide 16.*

- Length of wire in the magnetic field.
- The magnetic field strength of the permanent magnet.

### Magnetic Field

*Source slide 19.*

- The quantity \(B\) describing a magnetic field is [[Glossary#Magnetic Flux|magnetic flux]] [[Glossary#Density|density]], measured in tesla.
- In a field-line diagram, closer spacing represents a stronger field; the lines themselves are a model.
- The units for magnetic flux density are tesla (T).

### Determining Magnetic Flux Density

*Source slide 23.*

- We can experimentally determine the magnetic flux density of a set of magnets.
- The magnets are placed on a top-pan balance with a current carrying conductor placed between them.
- The length of the wire in the magnetic field is measured with a ruler.
- The current will be measured with an ammeter in series.

## Equations

### Magnetic forces

$$F=BIL\sin\theta$$

Force on a current-carrying conductor.

$$F=BQv\sin\theta$$

Force on a moving charge.

$$r=\frac{mv}{BQ}=\frac{p}{BQ}$$

Radius for perpendicular motion in a uniform magnetic field.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Identify field, conventional current or positive-charge velocity, and force as mutually perpendicular directions.
2. Reverse the force for a negative charge.
3. A magnetic force does no work because it is perpendicular to velocity.

## Common mistakes

- Use the component of current/velocity perpendicular to the field.
