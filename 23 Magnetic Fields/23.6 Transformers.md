## Core notes

### 23.6 Transformers

*Source slide 1.*

- Faraday’s and Lenz’s law relate the induced e.m.f to the concept of [[Glossary#Magnetic Flux|magnetic flux]] linkage.
- Lenz’s law: The direction of the induced e.m.f. (or current) always opposes the change producing it.

### Transformers and Efficiency

*Source slide 6.*

- In step-up transformers the [[Glossary#Potential Difference|potential difference]] increases and the current decreases.
- In step-down transformers the potential difference decreases and the current increases.
- Current causes heating.
- By using step-up transformers we increase [[Glossary#Efficiency|efficiency]] as less [[Glossary#Energy|energy]] is lost to the environment.

### National Grid and Transformers

*Source slide 23.*

- If you increase the potential difference you decrease the current while maintaining the same power transfer.
- Reducing current allows us to reduce the losses due to heating.
- Also using soft laminated iron reduces eddy currents and allows for easy magnetisation and demagnetisation.

## Equations

### Transformers

$$\frac{N_s}{N_p}=\frac{V_s}{V_p}$$

Turns and voltage ratio for an ideal transformer.

$$V_pI_p=V_sI_s$$

Power conservation for an ideal transformer.

$$\frac{N_s}{N_p}=\frac{V_s}{V_p}=\frac{I_p}{I_s}$$

Combined ideal ratios.

### Electromagnetic induction

$$\Phi=BA\cos\theta$$

Magnetic flux; \(\theta\) is between field and area normal.

$$\mathcal{E}=-\frac{\Delta(N\Phi)}{\Delta t}$$

Faraday's and Lenz's laws.

### Electrical energy and power

$$P=VI=I^2R=\frac{V^2}{R}$$

Equivalent power relationships.

$$W=Pt=VIt$$

Electrical energy transferred.

$$\text{cost}=\text{energy in kWh}\times\text{price per kWh}$$

Domestic electricity cost.

## Method

1. Use rms alternating voltages and identify primary/secondary quantities clearly.
2. For a real transformer, use efficiency to relate input and output powers.
3. High transmission voltage lowers current and therefore \(I^2R\) losses.
4. Find the change in flux linkage \(N\Phi\), not just the final flux.
5. Use the minus sign/Lenz's law to state that the induced effect opposes the change causing it.
6. Increase EMF by increasing turns, field, area, or rate of change.
7. Choose the power form that uses known quantities.
8. For billing, convert power to kilowatts and time to hours.
9. For SI energy in joules, use power in watts and time in seconds.

## Common mistakes

- A transformer requires changing magnetic flux, so it does not operate from steady DC.
- Flux uses the angle to the normal; using the angle to the plane swaps sine and cosine.
- A kilowatt-hour is a unit of energy, not power.
