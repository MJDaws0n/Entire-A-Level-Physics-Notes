## Core notes

### 22.2 Coulomb’s Law

*Source slide 1.*

- A positive point charge
- A uniformly negatively charged object.

### Electrostatic Forces

*Source slide 3.*

- He experimentally showed, that the [[Glossary#Force|force]] caused from the interaction of two charged particles is proportional to the product of the charge.
- Also, Coulomb demonstrated that the force is inversely proportional to the square of the distance between the two charged particles.

### Investigating Coulomb's Law

*Source slides 9–11.*

- To investigate the force produced between two-point charges with the same charge, we need to measure the force and distance of separation.
- As the point charges get closer together the reading on the top-pan balance will increase.
- We saw last lesson that uniformly charged spheres can be treated as point like charges.

### Newton’s and Coulomb's law

*Source slide 17.*

- Coulomb's law is an analogue to Newton’s law of gravitation but for charged particles.
- There are some obvious similarities and some differences.
- Newton’s law of gravitation – mass and only attractive
- Coulomb's law – electric charge and is attractive and repulsive.
- The following slides compare the two laws for point masses and point charges.

## Equations

### Coulomb's law and radial fields

$$F=\frac{Qq}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2}$$

Coulomb force in vacuum.

$$E=\frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2}$$

Radial field of a point charge.

### Electric field strength

$$E=\frac{F}{Q}$$

Electric field strength.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Measure separation between the charge centres.
2. Find magnitude first, then determine attraction/repulsion and direction from charge signs.
3. Define field direction as the force direction on a positive test charge.
4. Use superposition to add electric fields as vectors.

## Common mistakes

- Field magnitude follows an inverse-square relationship.
- A negative charge experiences force opposite to the electric-field direction.
