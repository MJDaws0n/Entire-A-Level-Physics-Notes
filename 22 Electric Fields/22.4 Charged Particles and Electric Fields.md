## Core notes

### Particle Accelerators

*Source slide 3.*

- Charged particles, such as electrons and protons, can be accelerated by electric fields.
- As we just saw in the starter question even a 1.5 V cell can produce large accelerations for electrons.
- Particle accelerators use electric fields to accelerate protons close to the speed of light.

### Particle Acceleration

*Source slides 6–7.*

- When an electron is placed between two charged horizontal plates it will move towards the positive plate.
- The electron experiences a constant electrostatic [[Glossary#Force|force]] due to the uniformed electric field, so it experience constant [[Glossary#Acceleration|acceleration]].
- When the electron is initially traveling in the direction of the electric field it experiences a deceleration.
- If the field is strong enough the electron changes direction and accelerates towards the positive plate.

### Charged Particles at right angles to E-field

*Source slides 12–13.*

- A charged particle that moves at right angles in an electric field will trace out a curve.
- You analyse the motion of the horizontal and vertical components separately.
- The horizontal motion is unaffected.

## Equations

### Uniform electric fields

$$E=\frac{V}{d}$$

Uniform field between parallel plates.

$$F=qE,\qquad a=\frac{qE}{m}$$

Force and acceleration of a charged particle.

### Uniform acceleration equations

$$v=u+at$$

Final velocity after time \(t\).

$$s=\frac{1}{2}(u+v)t$$

Displacement from mean velocity.

$$s=ut+\frac{1}{2}at^2$$

Displacement without final velocity.

$$v^2=u^2+2as$$

Motion without time.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. For motion between plates, calculate \(E\), then \(F=qE\), then \(a=F/m\).
2. Treat motion parallel and perpendicular to the field independently when required.
3. Write down \(u,v,a,s,t\) with signs and units.
4. Select an equation containing the known quantities and the one unknown.
5. These equations apply only while acceleration is constant.

## Common mistakes

- A negative charge accelerates opposite to the electric-field direction.
- Do not automatically make \(g\) negative; its sign follows the chosen positive direction.
