## SI units, prefixes and conversions

$$Q_{\mathrm{base}} = Q_{\mathrm{prefixed}}\times 10^n$$

Replace the prefix by its power of ten.

$$T\,(\mathrm{K}) = \theta\,({}^{\circ}\mathrm{C}) + 273.15$$

Convert Celsius temperature to kelvin.

## Base and derived units

$$1\,\mathrm{N}=1\,\mathrm{kg\,m\,s^{-2}}$$

The newton in SI base units.

$$1\,\mathrm{J}=1\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-2}}$$

The joule in SI base units.

$$1\,\mathrm{W}=1\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-3}}$$

The watt in SI base units.

$$1\,\mathrm{Pa}=1\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$$

The pascal in SI base units.

## Mathematical tools used throughout the course

$$s=r\theta$$

Arc length, with the angle in radians.

$$\text{circumference}=2\pi r,\qquad A_{\mathrm{circle}}=\pi r^2$$

Circle relationships.

$$A_{\mathrm{triangle}}=\frac{1}{2}bh$$

Area of a triangle.

$$A_{\mathrm{trapezium}}=\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$$

Area of a trapezium.

$$A_{\mathrm{sphere}}=4\pi r^2,\qquad V_{\mathrm{sphere}}=\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$$

Sphere relationships.

$$V_{\mathrm{cylinder}}=\pi r^2h$$

Volume of a cylinder.

$$\sin\theta\approx\tan\theta\approx\theta,\qquad \cos\theta\approx1$$

Small-angle approximations when \(\theta\) is in radians.

$$\log(xy)=\log x+\log y,\qquad \log(x^n)=n\log x$$

Logarithm rules; the same forms apply to natural logarithms.

$$\ln(e^x)=x,\qquad e^{\ln x}=x$$

Inverse relationship between \(e^x\) and \(\ln x\).

## Vectors and components

$$F_x=F\cos\theta$$

Component adjacent to the stated angle.

$$F_y=F\sin\theta$$

Component opposite the stated angle.

$$R=\sqrt{R_x^2+R_y^2}$$

Magnitude from perpendicular components.

$$\theta=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{R_y}{R_x}\right)$$

Direction from perpendicular components; check the quadrant.

## Vector geometry

$$a^2=b^2+c^2$$

Pythagoras for a right-angled vector triangle.

$$a^2=b^2+c^2-2bc\cos A$$

Cosine rule.

$$\frac{a}{\sin A}=\frac{b}{\sin B}=\frac{c}{\sin C}$$

Sine rule.

## Distance and speed

$$\text{speed}=\frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}$$

Scalar rate of change of distance.

## Displacement and velocity

$$\bar v=\frac{\Delta s}{\Delta t}$$

Average velocity from displacement.

## Acceleration

$$a=\frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$$

Acceleration.

## Motion graphs

$$\text{gradient of }s\text{-}t=v$$

Velocity is the displacement-time gradient.

$$\text{gradient of }v\text{-}t=a$$

Acceleration is the velocity-time gradient.

$$\Delta s=\int v\,dt$$

Displacement is signed area under a velocity-time graph.

## Uniform acceleration equations

$$v=u+at$$

Final velocity after time \(t\).

$$s=\frac{1}{2}(u+v)t$$

Displacement from mean velocity.

$$s=ut+\frac{1}{2}at^2$$

Displacement without final velocity.

$$v^2=u^2+2as$$

Motion without time.

## Projectile motion

$$u_x=u\cos\theta,\qquad u_y=u\sin\theta$$

Resolve the launch velocity.

$$a_x=0,\qquad a_y=-g$$

Ignoring air resistance and taking upward as positive.

## Forces and Newton's laws

$$\sum F=ma$$

Resultant force causes acceleration.

$$W=mg$$

Weight in a gravitational field.

## Drag and terminal velocity

$$F_{\mathrm{drag}}\propto v$$

A possible low-speed model.

$$F_{\mathrm{drag}}\propto v^2$$

A common turbulent/high-speed model.

$$F_{\mathrm{drag}}=W\quad\text{at terminal velocity}$$

Zero resultant force, so zero acceleration.

## Moments, couples and equilibrium

$$M=Fx$$

Moment equals force times perpendicular distance from the pivot.

$$\tau=Fd$$

Torque of a couple; \(d\) is the perpendicular separation of the forces.

$$\sum M_{\mathrm{clockwise}}=\sum M_{\mathrm{anticlockwise}}$$

Rotational equilibrium.

## Density and pressure

$$\rho=\frac{m}{V}$$

Density.

$$p=\frac{F}{A}$$

Pressure caused by a normal force.

$$\Delta p=h\rho g$$

Pressure difference in a fluid column.

## Upthrust and Archimedes' principle

$$U=\rho_{\mathrm{fluid}}V_{\mathrm{displaced}}g$$

Upthrust equals the weight of displaced fluid.

$$U=W\quad\text{for a floating object in equilibrium}$$

Balanced vertical forces.

## Work and mechanical energy

$$W=Fx\cos\theta$$

Work done by a constant force; \(\theta\) is between force and displacement.

$$E_k=\frac{1}{2}mv^2$$

Kinetic energy.

$$\Delta E_p=mg\Delta h$$

Change in gravitational potential energy near Earth's surface.

## Power and efficiency

$$P=\frac{W}{t}=\frac{\Delta E}{t}$$

Rate of work or energy transfer.

$$P=Fv$$

Power for force parallel to constant velocity.

$$\eta=\frac{\text{useful output}}{\text{total input}}$$

Efficiency as a decimal; multiply by \(100\%\) for percent.

## Springs and elastic energy

$$F=kx$$

Hooke's law within the limit of proportionality.

$$E_{\mathrm{elastic}}=\frac{1}{2}Fx=\frac{1}{2}kx^2$$

Area under a linear force-extension graph.

$$k_{\mathrm{parallel}}=k_1+k_2+\cdots$$

Parallel springs have the same extension and their forces add.

$$\frac{1}{k_{\mathrm{series}}}=\frac{1}{k_1}+\frac{1}{k_2}+\cdots$$

Series springs carry the same force and their extensions add.

## Stress, strain and Young modulus

$$\sigma=\frac{F}{A}$$

Tensile stress.

$$\varepsilon=\frac{\Delta L}{L}$$

Tensile strain.

$$E=\frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon}$$

Young modulus in the linear elastic region.

## Momentum and impulse

$$p=mv$$

Linear momentum.

$$F=\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$$

Mean resultant force.

$$J=F\Delta t=\Delta p$$

Impulse for a constant/mean force.

$$\sum p_{\mathrm{before}}=\sum p_{\mathrm{after}}$$

Momentum conservation in an isolated system.

## Charge and current

$$\Delta Q=I\Delta t$$

Charge passing a point.

$$Q=Ne$$

Charge is quantised in multiples of elementary charge.

$$I=Anev_d$$

Mean drift velocity in a conductor.

## Kirchhoff's laws

$$\sum I_{\mathrm{in}}=\sum I_{\mathrm{out}}$$

First law: conservation of charge at a junction.

$$\sum \mathcal{E}=\sum V$$

Second law around a complete loop: conservation of energy per unit charge.

## Potential difference and electromotive force

$$V=\frac{W}{Q}$$

Potential difference: energy transferred per unit charge.

$$\mathcal{E}=\frac{W_{\mathrm{source}}}{Q}$$

EMF: energy supplied per unit charge.

$$W=VQ=VIt$$

Electrical energy transferred.

## Accelerating charged particles

$$\Delta E_k=qV$$

Kinetic-energy gain across a potential difference.

$$\frac{1}{2}mv^2=qV$$

Non-relativistic speed after acceleration from rest.

$$1\,\mathrm{eV}=1.60\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J}$$

Electronvolt conversion.

## Resistance and resistivity

$$R=\frac{V}{I}$$

Resistance at a particular operating point.

$$R=\frac{\rho L}{A}$$

Resistance of a uniform conductor.

## Current-voltage characteristics

$$R_{\mathrm{static}}=\frac{V}{I}$$

Resistance at one point on a non-linear characteristic.

$$r_{\mathrm{dynamic}}=\frac{\Delta V}{\Delta I}$$

Local/dynamic resistance from a tangent where required.

## Resistors in series and parallel

$$R_{\mathrm{series}}=R_1+R_2+\cdots$$

Series combination.

$$\frac{1}{R_{\mathrm{parallel}}}=\frac{1}{R_1}+\frac{1}{R_2}+\cdots$$

Parallel combination.

## Electrical energy and power

$$P=VI=I^2R=\frac{V^2}{R}$$

Equivalent power relationships.

$$W=Pt=VIt$$

Electrical energy transferred.

$$\text{cost}=\text{energy in kWh}\times\text{price per kWh}$$

Domestic electricity cost.

## Internal resistance

$$\mathcal{E}=I(R+r)$$

EMF for external resistance \(R\) and internal resistance \(r\).

$$\mathcal{E}=V+Ir$$

Terminal potential difference while supplying current.

$$V=\mathcal{E}-Ir$$

Straight-line form for a \(V\)-against-\(I\) graph.

## Potential dividers and sensing circuits

$$V_{\mathrm{out}}=\frac{R_2}{R_1+R_2}V_{\mathrm{in}}$$

Output across \(R_2\) for an unloaded divider.

$$\frac{V_1}{V_2}=\frac{R_1}{R_2}$$

Voltage ratio in a series pair.

## Progressive waves

$$v=f\lambda$$

Wave speed.

$$f=\frac{1}{T}$$

Frequency and period.

$$\Delta\phi=2\pi\frac{\Delta x}{\lambda}=2\pi\frac{\Delta t}{T}$$

Phase difference in radians.

## Refraction and total internal reflection

$$n=\frac{c}{v}$$

Absolute refractive index.

$$n_1\sin\theta_1=n_2\sin\theta_2$$

Snell's law.

$$\sin C=\frac{n_2}{n_1}$$

Critical angle from higher index \(n_1\) to lower index \(n_2\).

## Diffraction and polarisation

$$I=I_0\cos^2\theta$$

Malus' law where the course/question requires it.

## Wave intensity

$$I=\frac{P}{A}$$

Power per unit area.

$$I=\frac{P}{4\pi r^2}$$

Intensity from an isotropic point source.

$$I\propto A^2$$

Intensity is proportional to amplitude squared.

## Superposition and interference

$$\Delta x=n\lambda$$

Constructive interference.

$$\Delta x=\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\lambda$$

Destructive interference.

$$\lambda=\frac{ax}{D}$$

Double-slit relationship for small angles.

## Stationary waves and harmonics

$$f_n=\frac{nv}{2L}\quad(n=1,2,3,\ldots)$$

String or open-open air column.

$$f_n=\frac{nv}{4L}\quad(n=1,3,5,\ldots)$$

Closed-open air column.

## Photons and wave-particle duality

$$E=hf=\frac{hc}{\lambda}$$

Photon energy.

$$p=\frac{h}{\lambda}$$

Photon momentum and de Broglie relationship.

$$\lambda=\frac{h}{p}$$

de Broglie wavelength of a particle.

## The photoelectric effect

$$hf=\phi+E_{k,\max}$$

Einstein's photoelectric equation.

$$E_{k,\max}=eV_s=\frac{1}{2}mv_{\max}^2$$

Maximum kinetic energy and stopping potential.

$$f_0=\frac{\phi}{h}$$

Threshold frequency.

## Temperature, states and internal energy

$$T\,(\mathrm{K})=\theta\,({}^{\circ}\mathrm{C})+273.15$$

Absolute temperature.

## Specific heat capacity

$$\Delta E=mc\Delta\theta$$

Heating with no change of state.

## Specific latent heat

$$\Delta E=mL$$

Energy for a change of state at constant temperature.

## Ideal gases and kinetic theory

$$pV=NkT=nRT$$

Ideal gas equation.

$$pV=\frac{1}{3}Nm\langle c^2\rangle$$

Kinetic-theory pressure relationship.

$$\frac{1}{2}m\langle c^2\rangle=\frac{3}{2}kT$$

Mean translational kinetic energy.

$$c_{\mathrm{rms}}=\sqrt{\langle c^2\rangle}=\sqrt{\frac{3kT}{m}}$$

Root-mean-square molecular speed.

## Radians and angular motion

$$s=r\theta$$

Arc length; \(\theta\) must be in radians.

$$\omega=\frac{\Delta\theta}{\Delta t}=\frac{2\pi}{T}=2\pi f$$

Angular velocity.

$$v=\omega r$$

Linear speed.

## Centripetal acceleration and force

$$a_c=\frac{v^2}{r}=\omega^2r$$

Centripetal acceleration.

$$F_c=\frac{mv^2}{r}=m\omega^2r$$

Resultant inward force.

## Simple harmonic motion

$$a=-\omega^2x$$

Defining SHM relationship.

$$x=A\cos\omega t\quad\text{or}\quad x=A\sin\omega t$$

Displacement with a chosen phase origin.

$$v=\pm\omega\sqrt{A^2-x^2}$$

Speed-displacement relationship.

$$\omega=\frac{2\pi}{T}=2\pi f$$

Angular frequency.

## Energy in simple harmonic motion

$$E_{\mathrm{total}}=\frac{1}{2}m\omega^2A^2$$

Total energy of ideal SHM.

$$E_k=\frac{1}{2}m\omega^2(A^2-x^2)$$

Kinetic energy at displacement \(x\).

$$E_p=\frac{1}{2}m\omega^2x^2$$

Potential energy in an ideal SHM model.

## Gravitational fields

$$g=\frac{F}{m}$$

Gravitational field strength: force per unit mass.

## Newton's law of gravitation

$$F=\frac{GMm}{r^2}$$

Magnitude of the attractive force between spherical/point masses.

## Radial gravitational field strength

$$g=\frac{GM}{r^2}$$

Magnitude outside a spherical mass.

## Kepler's laws and circular orbits

$$T^2=\frac{4\pi^2}{GM}r^3$$

Circular orbit/Kepler's third law.

$$v=\sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}$$

Speed in a circular gravitational orbit.

## Gravitational potential

$$V_g=-\frac{GM}{r}$$

Potential with zero at infinity.

$$\Delta W=m\Delta V_g$$

Work/energy change for a mass moved between potentials.

## Gravitational potential energy

$$E_p=mV_g=-\frac{GMm}{r}$$

Potential energy with zero at infinity.

## Atomic energy levels and spectra

$$hf=\Delta E=\frac{hc}{\lambda}$$

Photon emitted or absorbed in an energy-level transition.

## Diffraction gratings

$$d\sin\theta=n\lambda$$

Diffraction grating.

## Black-body radiation and temperature

$$\lambda_{\max}T=b$$

Wien's displacement law.

## Stellar luminosity and flux

$$L=4\pi R^2\sigma T^4$$

Stefan-Boltzmann luminosity.

$$F=\frac{L}{4\pi d^2}$$

Radiant flux/intensity at distance \(d\).

## Astronomical distance methods

$$d\,(\mathrm{pc})=\frac{1}{p\,(\mathrm{arcsec})}$$

Parallax distance.

$$1\,\mathrm{ly}\approx9.5\times10^{15}\,\mathrm{m}$$

Light-year conversion.

$$1\,\mathrm{pc}\approx3.1\times10^{16}\,\mathrm{m}$$

Parsec conversion.

## Doppler shift and redshift

$$\frac{\Delta\lambda}{\lambda}\approx\frac{v}{c}$$

Non-relativistic Doppler redshift.

## Hubble's law and the Hubble time

$$v=H_0d$$

Hubble's law.

$$t_{\mathrm{H}}\approx H_0^{-1}$$

Hubble-time estimate after converting \(H_0\) to \(\mathrm{s^{-1}}\).

## Capacitance

$$C=\frac{Q}{V}$$

Capacitance.

$$C=\frac{\varepsilon_0A}{d}$$

Parallel plates in vacuum or air, neglecting edge effects.

$$C=4\pi\varepsilon_0R$$

Capacitance of an isolated conducting sphere of radius \(R\).

## Capacitors in series and parallel

$$C_{\mathrm{parallel}}=C_1+C_2+\cdots$$

Capacitors in parallel.

$$\frac{1}{C_{\mathrm{series}}}=\frac{1}{C_1}+\frac{1}{C_2}+\cdots$$

Capacitors in series.

## Energy stored by a capacitor

$$W=\frac{1}{2}QV=\frac{Q^2}{2C}=\frac{1}{2}CV^2$$

Energy stored.

## Capacitor charging and discharging

$$\tau=CR$$

Time constant.

$$x=x_0e^{-t/CR}$$

Discharge of \(Q,V\), or \(I\) magnitude.

$$x=x_0\left(1-e^{-t/CR}\right)$$

Charging rise for \(Q\) or capacitor voltage.

## Electric field strength

$$E=\frac{F}{Q}$$

Electric field strength.

## Coulomb's law and radial fields

$$F=\frac{Qq}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2}$$

Coulomb force in vacuum.

$$E=\frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2}$$

Radial field of a point charge.

## Uniform electric fields

$$E=\frac{V}{d}$$

Uniform field between parallel plates.

$$F=qE,\qquad a=\frac{qE}{m}$$

Force and acceleration of a charged particle.

## Electric potential and potential energy

$$V=\frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r}$$

Potential of a point charge.

$$E_p=qV=\frac{Qq}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r}$$

Electric potential energy.

## Magnetic forces

$$F=BIL\sin\theta$$

Force on a current-carrying conductor.

$$F=BQv\sin\theta$$

Force on a moving charge.

$$r=\frac{mv}{BQ}=\frac{p}{BQ}$$

Radius for perpendicular motion in a uniform magnetic field.

## Electromagnetic induction

$$\Phi=BA\cos\theta$$

Magnetic flux; \(\theta\) is between field and area normal.

$$\mathcal{E}=-\frac{\Delta(N\Phi)}{\Delta t}$$

Faraday's and Lenz's laws.

## Transformers

$$\frac{N_s}{N_p}=\frac{V_s}{V_p}$$

Turns and voltage ratio for an ideal transformer.

$$V_pI_p=V_sI_s$$

Power conservation for an ideal transformer.

$$\frac{N_s}{N_p}=\frac{V_s}{V_p}=\frac{I_p}{I_s}$$

Combined ideal ratios.

## Nuclear and particle structure

$$R=r_0A^{1/3}$$

Nuclear radius model.

$$A=Z+N$$

Mass number equals proton plus neutron number.

$$Q_{\mathrm{before}}=Q_{\mathrm{after}}$$

Charge conservation in particle reactions.

## Particle families and quarks

$$Q_u=+\frac{2}{3}e,\qquad Q_d=Q_s=-\frac{1}{3}e$$

Quark charges.

$$p=uud,\qquad n=udd$$

Proton and neutron quark compositions.

## Particle and nuclear decay equations

$${}^{A}_{Z}X\rightarrow{}^{A-4}_{Z-2}Y+{}^{4}_{2}\alpha$$

Alpha decay.

$$n\rightarrow p+e^-+\bar{\nu}_e$$

Beta-minus decay; quark change \(d\rightarrow u\).

$$p\rightarrow n+e^++\nu_e$$

Beta-plus decay; quark change \(u\rightarrow d\).

$$e^-+e^+\rightarrow2\gamma$$

Electron-positron annihilation.

## Radioactive decay

$$A=\lambda N$$

Activity.

$$\frac{\Delta N}{\Delta t}=-\lambda N$$

Decay rate.

$$N=N_0e^{-\lambda t},\qquad A=A_0e^{-\lambda t}$$

Exponential decay.

$$\lambda t_{1/2}=\ln 2$$

Half-life relationship.

## Mass-energy and binding energy

$$\Delta E=\Delta mc^2$$

Energy released from a mass decrease.

$$E_b=\Delta mc^2$$

Nuclear binding energy from mass defect.

$$1\,\mathrm{u}=1.661\times10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$$

Atomic mass unit conversion.

$$1\,\mathrm{eV}=1.60\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J}$$

Electronvolt conversion.

## X-rays and attenuation

$$E_{\max}=eV=\frac{hc}{\lambda_{\min}}$$

Maximum X-ray photon energy and minimum wavelength.

$$I=I_0e^{-\mu x}$$

Attenuation in a material.

$$x_{1/2}=\frac{\ln2}{\mu}$$

Half-value thickness.

## Nuclear medical imaging

$$A=\lambda N$$

Tracer activity.

$$N=N_0e^{-\lambda t}$$

Tracer decay.

$$e^-+e^+\rightarrow2\gamma$$

Positron-electron annihilation; photons travel approximately opposite directions.

## Ultrasound pulse-echo imaging

$$v=f\lambda$$

Wave speed.

$$d=\frac{ct}{2}$$

Pulse-echo depth.

## Acoustic impedance and reflection

$$Z=\rho c$$

Acoustic impedance.

$$\frac{I_r}{I_0}=\left(\frac{Z_2-Z_1}{Z_2+Z_1}\right)^2$$

Normal-incidence intensity reflection coefficient.

## Doppler ultrasound

$$\frac{\Delta f}{f}=\frac{2v\cos\theta}{c}$$

Doppler ultrasound for reflected waves.
