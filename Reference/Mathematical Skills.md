# Mathematical Skills

[[00 Course Index|Course index]] · [[Formula Sheet]] · [[Radians]] · [[Graphs, Gradients and Data Skills]]

These are the mathematical relationships and habits used throughout OCR Physics A, including the mathematical section of the examination data booklet.

## Relationships

$$s=r\theta$$

Arc length, with the angle in radians.

$$\text{circumference}=2\pi r,\qquad A_{\mathrm{circle}}=\pi r^2$$

Circle relationships.

$$A_{\mathrm{triangle}}=\frac{1}{2}bh$$

Area of a triangle.

$$A_{\mathrm{trapezium}}=\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h$$

Area of a trapezium.

$$A_{\mathrm{sphere}}=4\pi r^2,\qquad V_{\mathrm{sphere}}=\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$$

Sphere relationships.

$$V_{\mathrm{cylinder}}=\pi r^2h$$

Volume of a cylinder.

$$\sin\theta\approx\tan\theta\approx\theta,\qquad \cos\theta\approx1$$

Small-angle approximations when \(\theta\) is in radians.

$$\log(xy)=\log x+\log y,\qquad \log(x^n)=n\log x$$

Logarithm rules; the same forms apply to natural logarithms.

$$\ln(e^x)=x,\qquad e^{\ln x}=x$$

Inverse relationship between \(e^x\) and \(\ln x\).

## Method

1. Convert every trigonometric angle to the mode required by the equation before calculating.
2. Use radians in angular-motion, arc-length and small-angle relationships.
3. When linearising an exponential, take logarithms of the complete dimensionless ratio where possible.

## Common mistakes

- Small-angle approximations are not valid for large angles and require radians.
- Taking a logarithm does not remove units physically; write the model as a ratio to a reference value when interpreting it rigorously.

## Calculator checks

- Check whether the calculator is in degrees or radians before using trigonometry.
- Keep guard digits during working and round only the final answer.
- For a value written as \(a\times10^n\), enter the exponent with the calculator's exponent key rather than multiplying by ten repeatedly.
- A logarithm can be used to linearise an exponential: \(y=y_0e^{kx}\) gives \(\ln(y/y_0)=kx\).
