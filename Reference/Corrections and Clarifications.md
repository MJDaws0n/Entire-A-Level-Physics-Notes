# Corrections and Clarifications

The lesson notes use checked physics rather than copying every slide literally. Repeated classroom prompts were condensed, and equation or unit errors were corrected. Notable source corrections include:

- Mixed-unit addition: \(100\,\mathrm{g}+0.500\,\mathrm{kg}=600\,\mathrm{g}\), not \(100.5\,\mathrm{g}\).
- Prefix conversions use the prefix on the unit: \(1\,\mathrm{km}=10^3\,\mathrm{m}\), \(1\,\mathrm{Mm}=10^6\,\mathrm{m}\), \(1\,\mathrm{\mu m}=10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}\) and \(1\,\mathrm{nm}=10^{-9}\,\mathrm{m}\). Several reversed “add a prefix” lines in the slides were not retained.
- Vector components retain the unit of the original vector; a force component is in newtons, not \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\).
- Copper density is approximately \(8.96\times10^3\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}\), not \(8.96\times10^4\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}\).
- \(1\,\mathrm{cm^3}=10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m^3}\); displaced-fluid examples must retain this exponent.
- In circular motion, angular velocity is \(\omega=\Delta\theta/\Delta t\); angular acceleration is \(\alpha=\Delta\omega/\Delta t\).
- In gravitational potential, the reference zero is at infinity and \(V_g=-GM/r\).
- In capacitor charging, charge and capacitor voltage rise as \(x=x_0(1-e^{-t/RC})\), while charging current decays.
- For springs, \(k_{\mathrm{parallel}}=k_1+k_2+\cdots\), while \(1/k_{\mathrm{series}}=1/k_1+1/k_2+\cdots\). A slide labelled the parallel sum as a series rule; the lesson uses the corrected relationship.

Where a numerical slide appeared inconsistent, the notes retain the general method and use the checked equation rather than reproducing the faulty number.
