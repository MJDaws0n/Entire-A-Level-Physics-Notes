## Core notes

### Graphene

*Source slides 3–4.*

- Graphene was discovered by two physicists, Prof Andre Geim and Prof Kostya Novoselov at the University of Manchester in 2004.
- Fascination with this material stems from its remarkable physical properties.
- It is 200 times stronger than steel yet incredibly lightweight and flexible.
- It is electrically and thermally conductive but also transparent.
- It is the world's first 2D material and is one million times smaller than the diameter of a single human hair.
- Graphene is extremely electrically conductive.
- This is because of the [[Glossary#Density|density]] of free electrons present in the material.

### Number Density

*Source slides 5–7.*

- Number density, n, equals the number of electrons per meter cubed.
- ne - Number of electrons (no units).
- Conductors, insulators and semi-conductors have a different number of charge carriers in a given volume.
- Number density, n, tells us how many charge carriers a material has per cubic meter (m-3)

### How fast do carriers move?

*Source slide 9.*

- Some people believe that electrons move at the speed of light as when you turn on the light switch the lamp comes on instantaneously.
- Actually the electrons drift along the wire.
- The lamp responds quickly because the electric field is established through the circuit at a large fraction of the speed of light; individual electrons drift slowly.

- We want to maintain the magnitude of the current
- yet we decreases the number of free charge carriers.
- To maintain the current, when the number of free charge carriers is reduced, we would need to increase the velocity of the charge carriers.
