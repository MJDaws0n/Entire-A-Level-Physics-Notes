## Core notes

### Electrical Currents

*Source slide 3.*

- Electric currents are everywhere not just found in metal conductors.
- Electric currents are found in the atmosphere, the earth core, and in human cells.
- Electrical currents are caused by electrically charged particles moving in a net (overall) direction.

### Ions: Relative Charge and Absolute Charge

*Source slide 4.*

- Ions are electrically charged particles.
- A hydrogen ion, H+, has lost an electron and therefore has a relative charge of +1.

### Current in Metal Conductors

*Source slide 5.*

- Metals comprise of a regular lattice arrangement of positively charged ions.
- The electrons form a sea of free electrons.
- The free electrons are free to move when a [[Glossary#Potential Difference|potential difference]] is applied.
- The ions do not move but vibrate around fixed positions.

### Polarity and Current

*Source slide 7.*

- Cell and batteries provide the potential difference and have a positive and negative terminal.
- Current is the rate of flow of charged particles.
- The direction of conventional current is the direction positive charge would flow.
- This is opposite to electron flow.

### Measuring Current

*Source slide 11.*

- To measure current we use an ammeter.
- The ammeter is placed in line with the conductor.
- This is called in series.
- The ammeter ‘counts’ the charged particles and the units are given in amps (A).
- A perfect ammeter would have zero [[Glossary#Resistance|resistance]].

### Electric Current in Electrolytes

*Source slides 12–13.*

- Liquids can conduct electricity.
- Liquids that can conduct an electrical current are called electrolytes.
- Although pure water is an excellent electrical insulator water from rivers and the tap have dissolved ions present in them.
- Therefore, impure water can carry an electrical current and is called an ionic solution.
- A common example of an ionic solution is salt (sodium chloride, Na+ Cl-) water.
- The salt separates into positively charged sodium ions Na+ and negatively charged chlorine ions Cl-
- The net flow of ions causes a current.

### Electrolysis Equipment

*Source slide 14.*

- Electrolyte – contains ions in solution

### Cathode

*Source slide 18.*

- At the electrodes electrons are transferred to and from the ions.
- Negative Electron Flow
- The negative electrons flow into an electrode.
- The electrode becomes negatively charged we call this a cathode.
- The positive ions are attracted to the negative cathode.

### Anodes

*Source slide 19.*

- The negative electrons flow away from the electrode.
- The electrode becomes positively charged we call this an anode.
- The negative ions are attracted to the positive anode.
- Electrons are transferred.
- The negative ions are called anions.

## Equations

### Charge and current

$$\Delta Q=I\Delta t$$

Charge passing a point.

$$Q=Ne$$

Charge is quantised in multiples of elementary charge.

$$I=Anev_d$$

Mean drift velocity in a conductor.

## Method

1. Use conventional current direction unless the question explicitly asks for electron flow.
2. In drift-speed calculations, convert number density and cross-sectional area to SI units.

## Common mistakes

- Electrons drift opposite to conventional current.
