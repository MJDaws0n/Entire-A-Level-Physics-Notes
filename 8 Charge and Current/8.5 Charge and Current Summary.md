## Equations

### Charge and current

$$\Delta Q=I\Delta t$$

Charge passing a point.

$$Q=Ne$$

Charge is quantised in multiples of elementary charge.

$$I=Anev_d$$

Mean drift velocity in a conductor.

### Kirchhoff's laws

$$\sum I_{\mathrm{in}}=\sum I_{\mathrm{out}}$$

First law: conservation of charge at a junction.

$$\sum \mathcal{E}=\sum V$$

Second law around a complete loop: conservation of energy per unit charge.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Use conventional current direction unless the question explicitly asks for electron flow.
2. In drift-speed calculations, convert number density and cross-sectional area to SI units.
3. Mark current directions; a negative answer means the true direction is opposite.
4. Apply the junction rule, then write one independent loop equation at a time.
5. Treat a potential rise and potential drop with consistent signs around each loop.

## Common mistakes

- Electrons drift opposite to conventional current.
- Current is not used up by a component; energy is transferred from each coulomb of charge.
