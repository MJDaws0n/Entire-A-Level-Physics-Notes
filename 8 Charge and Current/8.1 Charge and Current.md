## Core notes

### Electric Charge

*Source slide 4.*

- Electric charge is a physical property similar to mass, volume, or temperature.
- Charge is measured in Coulombs (C).
- Particles can have a positive charge.
- They can have a negative charge or particles can have no charge, neutral.
- Neutral particles do not interact with charged particles.

### Electric Current

*Source slide 5.*

- Current is the rate of flow of charged particles.
- In metal wires the charged particles which flow are electrons.
- [[Glossary#Potential Difference|Potential Difference]] (cell, battery, capacitor).

### Definition of Electric Current

*Source slide 6.*

- Electric current is defined as the rate of flow of charge.
- This is the amount of charge passing a point per second.

### Charge is quantised

*Source slide 8.*

- Observable isolated charge is quantised in integer multiples of the elementary charge; quarks carry fractional charge but are not observed in isolation.
- This value is the magnitude (size) of the charge of an electron and proton.

## Method

1. Use conventional current direction unless the question explicitly asks for electron flow.
2. In drift-speed calculations, convert number density and cross-sectional area to SI units.

## Common mistakes

- Electrons drift opposite to conventional current.
