### The Characteristics of Stars

*Source slide 4.*

- There are an enormous variety of stars in the Universe.
- Stars vary in their mass, diameter, surface temperature, colour, and [[Glossary#Luminosity|luminosity]].
- Astrophysicists use many different methods to classify stars.
- One of the most useful methods is to compare luminosity against surface temperature - Hertzsprung-Russell diagram.

### Luminosity

*Source slide 5.*

- Hertzsprung-Russell (HR) diagram compares luminosity against surface temperature.
- Luminosity is the total radiant [[Glossary#Power|power]] output by a star.
- For a HR diagram luminosity is relative to the Sun’s (L/L☉).
- As temperature and luminosity of stars vary greatly the axis are plotted logarithmically.

### During the evolution of stars, they move across the HR diagram.

*Source slide 8.*

- Sun like stars start in the main sequence and move to become red giants before forming into white dwarfs.

### Higher mass stars star very hot and luminous.

*Source slide 9.*

- They rapidly consume their fuel before swelling in size, cooling and go supernova.

## Method

1. Use mass as the main factor determining a star's life cycle.
2. Read a Hertzsprung-Russell diagram from luminosity and surface temperature; the temperature axis decreases to the right.

## Common mistakes

- A position on the Hertzsprung-Russell diagram is not a plot of a star's movement through space.
