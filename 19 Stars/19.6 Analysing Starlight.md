## Core notes

### Starlight

*Source slide 3.*

- The electromagnetic radiation emitted from stars inform us of the characteristics of the star.
- The temperature of the star determines the intensities of the frequencies of the light emitted
- Therefore, from starlight we can determine the type of star.
- To do this we need a method of separating different frequencies of light to analyse them.
- For this we will use a [[Glossary#Diffraction|diffraction]] grating.

### The Diffraction Grating

*Source slide 7.*

- The double slit experiment had two slits.
- A diffraction grating can have a thousand slits (lines) every millimetre.
- Compared to a double slit a diffraction grating produces a sharper distinction between the light and dark fringes.
- This means we can analyse light that is composed of many different frequencies.

## Equations

### Black-body radiation and temperature

$$\lambda_{\max}T=b$$

Wien's displacement law.

### Doppler shift and redshift

$$\frac{\Delta\lambda}{\lambda}\approx\frac{v}{c}$$

Non-relativistic Doppler redshift.

### Atomic energy levels and spectra

$$hf=\Delta E=\frac{hc}{\lambda}$$

Photon emitted or absorbed in an energy-level transition.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Use the peak wavelength of the continuous spectrum to estimate surface temperature.
2. A hotter black body has a shorter peak wavelength and a larger area under its spectrum.
3. For redshift, \(\Delta\lambda=\lambda_{\mathrm{observed}}-\lambda_{\mathrm{rest}}\).
4. Use the approximation only when speed is much smaller than the speed of light.
5. Use the magnitude of the energy-level difference for photon energy.
6. Emission gives bright lines; absorption removes matching wavelengths from a continuous spectrum.

## Common mistakes

- The visible colour is a mixture of wavelengths, not simply the peak wavelength.
- Redshift means observed wavelength is longer; blueshift means it is shorter.
- An electron may occupy an allowed energy level, not an energy between levels.

> - A minimum occurs when the path difference is an odd number of half wavelengths.
> - 1/2 λ, 2/3 λ… This is in antiphase.
> - A maximum occurs when the path difference is a multiple of the [[Glossary#Wavelength|wavelength]].
