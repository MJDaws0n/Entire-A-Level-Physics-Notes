## Core notes

### Energy Levels in Gas Atoms

*Source slide 10.*

- When electrons are bound to atoms in a gas, they can only exist in one of a set of discrete [[Glossary#Energy|energy]] levels.
- The lowest energy level is called the ground state.
- An electron with zero energy is free from the atom.
- Energy levels are negative because external energy is required to remove an electron from the atom.

### Changing Energy

*Source slide 12.*

- When electrons gain energy and increase in energy level they are excited.
- When electrons lose energy and decrease in energy level they relax.

### The electronvolt

*Source slide 14.*

- The electronvolt (eV) is a unit of energy.

### Photons

*Source slide 16.*

- Electrons increase or decrease in energy level when they absorb or emit a photon.
- A photon is a particle of light with a certain energy ([[Glossary#Frequency|frequency]]).

## Equations

### Atomic energy levels and spectra

$$hf=\Delta E=\frac{hc}{\lambda}$$

Photon emitted or absorbed in an energy-level transition.

### Photons and wave-particle duality

$$E=hf=\frac{hc}{\lambda}$$

Photon energy.

$$p=\frac{h}{\lambda}$$

Photon momentum and de Broglie relationship.

$$\lambda=\frac{h}{p}$$

de Broglie wavelength of a particle.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Use the magnitude of the energy-level difference for photon energy.
2. Emission gives bright lines; absorption removes matching wavelengths from a continuous spectrum.
3. Convert wavelength to metres before using \(hc/\lambda\).
4. For a massive non-relativistic particle, find momentum from \(p=mv\).

## Common mistakes

- An electron may occupy an allowed energy level, not an energy between levels.
- Intensity changes the number of photons per second; frequency changes the energy of each photon.
