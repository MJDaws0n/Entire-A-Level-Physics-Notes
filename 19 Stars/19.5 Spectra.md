## Core notes

### Electromagnetic Waves

*Source slide 4.*

- Electromagnetic waves are classified by their [[Glossary#Wavelength|wavelength]].
- You need to be able to recall the name and order of magnitude for the different types of EM waves.
- Reducing wavelength (values in m)
- X-rays and gamma overlap
- X-rays and gamma rays overlap because they are defined not by their wavelength but their origin.

### Spectroscopy

*Source slide 7.*

- Spectroscopy is the study of the absorption and emission of electromagnetic radiation by matter.
- Spectroscopy is used as a tool for studying the structures of atoms and molecules.
- Spectroscopy is used in astronomy
- but also, physical and analytical chemistry.
- continuous spectra, absorption line spectra and emission line spectra.

### Continuous Spectra

*Source slide 8.*

- A continuous spectrum contains an unbroken range of frequencies or wavelengths.
- Sources of continuous spectra are the Sun and filaments lamps.

### Absorption Spectra

*Source slides 11–13.*

- Different atoms absorb different wavelengths of electromagnetic radiation.
- The electromagnetic radiation that is absorbed depends upon the difference in [[Glossary#Energy|energy]] of the electron’s energy levels.
- We can use this fact to identify an element.
- Janssen travelled to India to measure the solar spectrum during a total eclipse.
- He observed a line in the spectra that couldn’t be explained.
- Janssen had discovered a new element.
- He called it helium derived from the Greek, 'helios' meaning sun.

### Emission Spectra

*Source slide 14.*

- Electrons absorb electromagnetic radiation to increase their energy level.
- This produces absorption spectra.
- Electrons emit electromagnetic radiation when the decrease their energy level.
- This produces emission spectra.

### Emission line spectra

*Source slide 15.*

- Emission line spectra – each element produces a unique emission line spectrum because of the unique set of energy levels for each element.

## Equations

### Atomic energy levels and spectra

$$hf=\Delta E=\frac{hc}{\lambda}$$

Photon emitted or absorbed in an energy-level transition.

### Diffraction gratings

$$d\sin\theta=n\lambda$$

Diffraction grating.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Use the magnitude of the energy-level difference for photon energy.
2. Emission gives bright lines; absorption removes matching wavelengths from a continuous spectrum.
3. Convert lines per metre to slit spacing with \(d=1/N\).
4. Measure diffraction angle from the central maximum.

## Common mistakes

- An electron may occupy an allowed energy level, not an energy between levels.
- An order exists only when \(|\sin\theta|\leq1\).
