## Core notes

### The Universe

*Source slide 3.*

- The universe is by definition everything that exists and contains countless amazing objects.

### Spherical objects

*Source slide 7.*

- The sphere is the shape that provides the minimum surface area to volume ratio.
- The sphere is the shape with the most volume for the least surface area.
- When the mass of an object is great enough it will form a sphere through the mutual attraction of the gravitational [[Glossary#Force|force]].
- This is why planets and stars are spherical.

### The birth of stars

*Source slides 9–11.*

- Nebulae are the birthplace of stars.
- Nebulae are formed over millions of years
- with particles of dust and gas
- attracted to each other through the gravitational force.
- As the dust and gas get closer together the gravitational force increases.
- The causes the dust and gas to accelerate.
- Small variations in the nebula cause denser regions to form.

### Stars During Main Sequence

*Source slide 16.*

- When a star has formed, it remains in a stable equilibrium with almost a constant size for most of its life.
- This is called the main sequence of the star’s life.
- Gravitational forces compress the star.
- gas [[Glossary#Pressure|pressure]] from the nuclei in the core push outwards.

## Method

1. Use mass as the main factor determining a star's life cycle.
2. Read a Hertzsprung-Russell diagram from luminosity and surface temperature; the temperature axis decreases to the right.
3. Use half the total apparent annual angular shift as the parallax angle when appropriate.
4. Match the distance unit to the scale and convert only when the calculation requires it.

## Common mistakes

- A position on the Hertzsprung-Russell diagram is not a plot of a star's movement through space.
- A light-year is a distance, not a time.
