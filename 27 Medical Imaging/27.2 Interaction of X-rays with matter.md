## Core notes

### Attenuation

*Source slide 3.*

- X-rays are attenuated when they travel through a material.
- This is because X-rays are absorbed and scattered from particles in the material.
- Increasing the distance the X-rays travel through a material or increasing the [[Glossary#Density|density]] of the material, increases the attenuation of the X-rays because more X-rays are absorbed and scattered from particles in the material.

### Attenuation Mechanisms

*Source slide 4.*

- The [[Glossary#Intensity|intensity]] of a collimated (parallel) beam of X-rays will decrease as it passes through matter.

### Simple Scatter

*Source slides 5–6.*

- Simple scattering of X-rays occurs when the photons have an [[Glossary#Energy|energy]] between 1 – 20 keV.
- The X-ray interacts with an electron in an atom but has less energy than is required to ionise (remove) the electron.
- The X-ray photon is scattered (bounces off) without any change to its energy.
- X-rays used in medical settings have an energy greater than 20 keV, and therefore, simple scattering is an unimportant consideration.

### Photoelectric Effect

*Source slide 7.*

- This photoelectric effect mechanism is important when X-ray photons have an energy below 100 keV.
- The X-ray is absorbed by an electron, and the electron is ionised (leaves the atom).
- For medical X-rays, the energy range is between 30 – 100 keV.
- Therefore, the photoelectric effect is a dominant attenuation mechanism in these procedures.

### Compton Scattering

*Source slide 8.*

- Compton scattering is significant when the X-ray photon has an energy range of 0.5 – 5 MeV.
- The X-ray ionises an electron, yet the X-ray isn’t completely absorbed.
- The X-ray continues with a reduced energy.
- Energy and [[Glossary#Momentum|momentum]] are conserved.

### Pair Production

*Source slide 9.*

- Pair production occurs when the X-ray photon has an energy equal to or greater than 1.02 MeV.
- The X-ray interacts with the nucleus.
- The X-ray is absorbed, and the energy creates an electron and a positron (the electron’s antiparticle).

*Source slide 12.*

- Different densities of materials result in different amounts of scattering and absorption.

### Contrast Medium

*Source slide 20.*

- X-rays are mainly used to image hard tissue.
- Hard tissue has a high attenuation coefficient therefore, you can achieve a good contrast image.
- Soft tissue has a low attenuation coefficient.
- Therefore, to get a good image of soft tissue using X-rays, you need to use a contrast medium that will increase the attenuation coefficient of the soft tissue.

### X-rays – Therapeutic Use

*Source slide 22.*

- High-energy X-rays can be used to kill cancerous cells.
- This is done via the Compton scattering and pair production mechanisms.
