## Core notes

### Reflected Waves

*Source slide 7.*

- The reflection of sound waves at boundaries is affected by the relative acoustic impedances of the two materials.
- When there is a large mismatch between the two acoustic impedances of the materials, there will be more reflection at the boundary.

### Acoustic Matching

*Source slide 12.*

- Due to the large acoustic-impedance difference between air and skin, almost all of the wave [[Glossary#Intensity|intensity]] would be reflected.
- We overcome this problem with an ultrasound scan by using a coupling gel.
- A coupling gel eliminates the air between the transducer and skin and reduces the [[Glossary#Acoustic Impedance|acoustic impedance]] mismatch to allow more of the wave to be transmitted.

## Equations

### Acoustic impedance and reflection

$$Z=\rho c$$

Acoustic impedance.

$$\frac{I_r}{I_0}=\left(\frac{Z_2-Z_1}{Z_2+Z_1}\right)^2$$

Normal-incidence intensity reflection coefficient.

### Ultrasound pulse-echo imaging

$$v=f\lambda$$

Wave speed.

$$d=\frac{ct}{2}$$

Pulse-echo depth.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Calculate each medium's impedance before forming the reflection ratio.
2. A larger impedance mismatch gives a stronger reflected pulse.
3. Use half the round-trip distance in pulse-echo calculations.
4. Coupling gel removes the air gap and reduces the impedance mismatch at the skin.

## Common mistakes

- The equation shown gives an intensity ratio, so the impedance ratio is squared.
- Do not forget the pulse travels to the boundary and back.
