### PET Scans

*Source slide 3.*

- A positron, a neutrino, and a gamma photon are emitted from fluorine-18.

### PET Scan

*Source slides 6–7.*

- A patient is injected with a medical tracer called fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG).
- Areas of high metabolic rate (such as tumours) in the patient will bind with the glucose more rapidly than others.
- Fluorine-18 in FDG will begin to decay and emit positrons.
- The positrons will encounter an electron in the patient and annihilate into two gamma photons with an [[Glossary#Energy|energy]] of 0.51 MeV.
- Gamma photons are detected with gamma detectors.
- Scintillators - producing visible light from the gamma photons
- Photomultiplier tubes - to produce an electrical signal (pd) from the visible photons.

### Advantages and Disadvantages of PET

*Source slide 10.*

- PET is non-invasive (no need for surgery).
- PET is used to detect different forms of cancer and disorders of the brain, such as Alzheimer’s disease.
- PET is very expensive as the medical tracers need to be produced on site.

### Alternative Radiopharmaceuticals

*Source slide 11.*

- Carbon monoxide can be used as a radiopharmaceutical for PET scans.
- A carbon-11 [[Glossary#Isotope|isotope]] can also be used for PET.
- Caron-11 decays with a [[Glossary#Half-Life|half-life]] of 20 minutes and emits a positron.
- Carbon monoxide binds with haemoglobin in the red blood cells.

## Method

1. Choose a tracer with suitable chemistry, gamma energy and half-life.
2. A gamma camera uses a collimator, scintillator, photomultipliers and position/energy processing.
3. PET locates near-simultaneous annihilation photons detected in coincidence.
4. Find mass defect using consistent particle or atomic masses.
5. Convert the mass difference to kilograms before using \(c^2\), unless using a stated \(\mathrm{MeV/u}\) conversion.
6. Compare binding energy per nucleon to explain energy release in fission or fusion.

## Common mistakes

- The gamma-camera collimator provides direction information but absorbs many photons, reducing sensitivity.
- Binding energy is the energy required to separate a nucleus; an increase in binding energy corresponds to energy released when the nucleus forms.
