## Core notes

### Doppler Red Shift

*Source slide 4.*

- Stars emit (give out) light.
- Light has a [[Glossary#Wavelength|wavelength]].
- When stars move away from us the light emitted appears to be red shifted.
- Red shift – increase wavelength
- and decreases [[Glossary#Frequency|frequency]].

### Doppler Effect

*Source slide 5.*

- The frequency of ultrasound waves changes when it is reflected by moving objects.
- When the object is moving away from the ultrasound wave, the frequency of the wave decreases.
- This increases the wavelength.
- When the object is moving towards the ultrasound wave, the frequency of the wave increases.
- This decreases the wavelength.

### Doppler Scans

*Source slide 7.*

- The Doppler effect can be utilised in ultrasound scans to image blood flow moving away and towards a transducer.

## Equations

### Doppler ultrasound

$$\frac{\Delta f}{f}=\frac{2v\cos\theta}{c}$$

Doppler ultrasound for reflected waves.

### Ultrasound pulse-echo imaging

$$v=f\lambda$$

Wave speed.

$$d=\frac{ct}{2}$$

Pulse-echo depth.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. In Doppler work, \(\theta\) is the angle between beam and target velocity.
2. The factor of two is present because moving blood cells receive and then reflect the wave.
3. Use half the round-trip distance in pulse-echo calculations.
4. Coupling gel removes the air gap and reduces the impedance mismatch at the skin.

## Common mistakes

- At \(90^{\circ}\), the Doppler shift is zero even when the blood is moving.
- Do not forget the pulse travels to the boundary and back.
