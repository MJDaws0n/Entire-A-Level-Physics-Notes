## Core notes

### Kinetic and Potential Energy in SHM

*Source slides 5–7.*

- We start the spring at an extended position.
- This has the maximum potential [[Glossary#Energy|energy]].
- Then we release and the system oscillates.
- The [[Glossary#Kinetic Energy|kinetic energy]], Ek, must be the difference between the total energy and the elastic potential energy.

## Equations

### Simple harmonic motion

$$a=-\omega^2x$$

Defining SHM relationship.

$$x=A\cos\omega t\quad\text{or}\quad x=A\sin\omega t$$

Displacement with a chosen phase origin.

$$v=\pm\omega\sqrt{A^2-x^2}$$

Speed-displacement relationship.

$$\omega=\frac{2\pi}{T}=2\pi f$$

Angular frequency.

### Energy in simple harmonic motion

$$E_{\mathrm{total}}=\frac{1}{2}m\omega^2A^2$$

Total energy of ideal SHM.

$$E_k=\frac{1}{2}m\omega^2(A^2-x^2)$$

Kinetic energy at displacement \(x\).

$$E_p=\frac{1}{2}m\omega^2x^2$$

Potential energy in an ideal SHM model.

### Work and mechanical energy

$$W=Fx\cos\theta$$

Work done by a constant force; \(\theta\) is between force and displacement.

$$E_k=\frac{1}{2}mv^2$$

Kinetic energy.

$$\Delta E_p=mg\Delta h$$

Change in gravitational potential energy near Earth's surface.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Define equilibrium as \(x=0\) and choose a positive direction.
2. Use the minus sign in \(a=-\omega^2x\) to show acceleration is restoring.
3. At equilibrium speed is maximum; at the extremes speed is zero and acceleration magnitude is maximum.
4. Use total energy as constant when damping is negligible.
5. At equilibrium energy is all kinetic; at the turning points it is all potential.
6. Identify the initial and final energy stores.
7. Include work done against resistive forces as energy transferred to thermal stores.
8. Equate energy changes only after accounting for every transfer.

## Common mistakes

- Displacement, velocity and acceleration are not all in phase.
- The potential-energy zero is chosen at equilibrium for these equations.
- Use vertical height change in \(mg\Delta h\), not distance along a slope.
