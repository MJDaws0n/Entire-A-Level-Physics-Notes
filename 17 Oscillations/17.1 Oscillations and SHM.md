## Core notes

### Oscillating Motion

*Source slide 3.*

- We have now seen how to analyse both linear and circular motion.
- But there is also a type of motion called oscillating motion.
- This type of motion is periodic in fashion, i.e., it repeats.
- For example, you can think of a pendulum swinging backwards and forwards or the wings of a humming bird beating at 50 cycles per second to be types of oscillating motion.

*Source slide 9.*

- [[Glossary#Acceleration|Acceleration]] is proportional to [[Glossary#Displacement|displacement]]

### Negative Acceleration and Displacement

*Source slide 10.*

- At zero displacement the acceleration is zero.
- At maximum displacement the acceleration is at its greatest.

## Equations

### Simple harmonic motion

$$a=-\omega^2x$$

Defining SHM relationship.

$$x=A\cos\omega t\quad\text{or}\quad x=A\sin\omega t$$

Displacement with a chosen phase origin.

$$v=\pm\omega\sqrt{A^2-x^2}$$

Speed-displacement relationship.

$$\omega=\frac{2\pi}{T}=2\pi f$$

Angular frequency.

See [[Formula Sheet]] for the complete course formula set.

## Method

1. Define equilibrium as \(x=0\) and choose a positive direction.
2. Use the minus sign in \(a=-\omega^2x\) to show acceleration is restoring.
3. At equilibrium speed is maximum; at the extremes speed is zero and acceleration magnitude is maximum.

## Common mistakes

- Displacement, velocity and acceleration are not all in phase.
