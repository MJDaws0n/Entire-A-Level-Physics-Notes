## Core notes

### Suspension and Damping

*Source slide 3.*

- Different cars and bike have different types of suspension.
- This is a damping affect which absorbs [[Glossary#Energy|energy]].
- Engineers have to decide on the amount of damping they want for their project.
- ‘Hard’ suspension systems do not absorb as much energy and thus gives a ‘sporty’ feel to driving a car.

### Types of Damping

*Source slides 4–5.*

- Damping results from air [[Glossary#Resistance|resistance]] and causes heating of the surroundings.
- We can image that the pendulum would be more heavily damped if placed in water or even treacle.
- Light, Heavy and Very Heavy Damping.
- [[Glossary#Amplitude|Amplitude]] decreases rapidly and the period increases slightly.
- Amplitude decreases with time but period is mostly unaffected.

### Free and Forces Oscillations

*Source slide 6.*

- When an object is displaced from its equilibrium position and allowed to oscillate without any external forces its motion is referred to as free oscillations.
- The [[Glossary#Frequency|frequency]] of the free oscillations is known as its natural frequency.
- A forced oscillation is when you apply a periodic driving [[Glossary#Force|force]].
- In this case the object will vibrate at the frequency of the driving force.
- If we match the driving frequency to the natural frequency the object will resonant and as a result the amplitude will dramatically increase.

## Method

1. Distinguish natural frequency from driving frequency.
2. At resonance, energy transfer from the driver is greatest and amplitude is large.
3. Greater damping lowers and broadens the resonance peak.

## Common mistakes

- Resonance occurs when driving frequency is near the natural frequency; damping lowers and broadens the peak.
