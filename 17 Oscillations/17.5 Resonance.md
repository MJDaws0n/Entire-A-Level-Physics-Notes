### Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)

*Source slide 6.*

- MRI relies on the resonance of hydrogen nuclei found in water molecules within tissues inside the body.
- Inside the scanner there is a strong magnetic field created by superconducting magnets.
- The hydrogen nuclei have a magnetic moment and begin to align themselves with the external field while precessing in a circle.
- The precession depends upon the types of molecules as different molecules have different natural frequencies.
- Radio waves are transmitted to the molecules which absorb the [[Glossary#Energy|energy]] and causes them to resonant.

### Resonance and Damping

*Source slides 9–10.*

- We saw last lesson that if we do not damp resonant systems then results which are not satisfactory endure.
- For light damping the maximum amplitude occurs at the natural [[Glossary#Frequency|frequency]], fo, of the forced oscillator.
- The amplitude of vibration at any frequency decreases.
- The maximum amplitude occurs at a lower frequency than the fo.
- The peak of the graph becomes flatter and broader

## Method

1. Distinguish natural frequency from driving frequency.
2. At resonance, energy transfer from the driver is greatest and amplitude is large.
3. Greater damping lowers and broadens the resonance peak.

## Common mistakes

- Resonance occurs when driving frequency is near the natural frequency; damping lowers and broadens the peak.
